## 🔬 Appendix: Detailed Manual for AI-Based Automated Collision Detection and Visualization (The Catholic Audit Pipeline)

This project does not stop at manual textual analysis; it has built a powerful automation pipeline, incorporating natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning algorithms, to mechanically index and visualize logical contradictions among Magisterial documents.

### 1. Detailed Operating Principles and Limitations of the Automated Doctrinal-Collision Detection Engine (Conflict Detector)
Powered by `scripts/conflict_detector.py`, this engine detects text-similarity-based collision **candidates** among Catholic doctrinal documents through the following procedure.

*   **Automated Parsing**: the engine iterates through every doctrine markdown card in `04_DOCTRINE_DB`, extracting the `Claims` and `Negates` sections separately using regular expressions. As of the current baseline, a total of 258 claims and 202 negations have been loaded into the database.
*   **Text Vectorization (TF-IDF & N-gram)**: the extracted text is converted into TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) vectors based on `character-level N-grams (2-4)`.
*   **Cosine Similarity Measurement**: a score between 0 and 1 is computed for how geometrically closely Document A's 'claim' vector matches Document B's 'negation' vector.
*   **⚠️ Known Limitations and the Two-Stage False-Positive Filter**: character n-gram similarity alone cannot reliably distinguish negating words (not/none/impossible, etc.).
    1.  **The Automated Cross-Claim Filter**: if A's claim also shows high similarity to B's **own** claims list (= meaning B itself also affirms the same proposition), the item is excluded from collision candidates regardless of how high its negate-matching score is.
    2.  **Manual Source-Text Comparison Verification**: borderline cases where the above filter's score difference is marginal (e.g., 0.318 vs. 0.317) were verified by a person directly reading the `04_DOCTRINE_DB` source text. This confirmed that (a) two cards pointing to the same document existed in the database (e.g., `DIGNITATIS-HUMANAE` and `VATICAN2-DH` are both the original 1965 declaration on religious freedom; `TRENT-S13` and `TRENT-S13-C01` are a summary/detail card pair from the same session), and (b) two distinct documents happened to affirm the same doctrine (e.g., `VAT1-PASTOR-AETERNUS` and `CCC-888_892` both affirm papal infallibility). Such pairs were reclassified as "not a collision" regardless of their negate-matching score. (No arbitrary numeric margin was applied, because a generous margin would also exclude legitimate candidates where genuine theological tension remains, such as `CCC-1861` vs. `AMORIS-LAETITIA-CH8`.)

    This two-stage filter judged and excluded a total of **46 cases** as false positives; the detailed record, including which filter excluded each case, is transparently disclosed below in §1-A and in `07_REPORT/auto_conflict_excluded_self_negation.csv`.
*   **Result**: after filtering, **21** significant (Score ≥ 0.20) 'A vs. Not A' collision candidates were automatically indexed. **This list is a first-pass candidate list requiring human theological re-review, and the algorithm does not render a final ruling on theological contradiction.** Because character n-gram similarity does not understand context, even listed items require source-text comparison and contextual confirmation before citation.

#### 1-B. 🎯 In-Depth Priority Analysis of the Top 4 Cases (Including Anticipated Rebuttals and Counter-Rebuttals)
The following 4 cases are, at present, the candidates with the strongest grounds — ranking at the top by Score and passing both stages of the §1 filter (the automated cross-claim filter plus manual source-text comparison). For each, the strongest rebuttal likely to come from the Catholic side is presented together with a counter-rebuttal, so as not to simply throw down a list and stop there.

**1. `UNAM-SANCTAM` (1302) vs. `CCC-0847`/`LG-16` (1964-1992) — The Absoluteness of "No Salvation Outside the Church" (Score 0.558 / 0.528)**
- **The Issue**: Unam Sanctam declares that "submission to the Roman Pontiff is a necessary condition of salvation for every human creature, without exception," while Vatican II (LG 16) and CCC 847 teach that "one who, through no fault of their own, does not know the Gospel — even a Muslim — can be saved by living according to their conscience."
- **The Anticipated Strongest Rebuttal**: this is not a contradiction but the development of doctrine. Unam Sanctam targets one who knows the Gospel and deliberately rejects it, while the CCC/LG address one who never had the opportunity to hear the Gospel in the first place (invincible ignorance) — the subjects addressed differ.
- **The Counter-Rebuttal**: however, the original text of Unam Sanctam contains no exception clause whatsoever for "one who did not know," and the contemporaneous Council of Florence (1442) went even further, nailing down that "even a martyr who sheds blood for Christ" admits of no exception if outside Catholicism. The distinction of "different subjects" is an interpretation introduced retroactively from the 20th century onward, and the original text itself did not anticipate this distinction.
- **Level of Scholarly Consensus**: 🟡 a matter disputed even within Catholicism itself — the mainstream interpretation ("development") since the condemnation of Fr. Feeney (1949) collides head-on with the traditionalist camp (SSPX, etc., who claim "this is precisely the Church's own apostasy"). That is, this is not a topic that would "surprise Catholicism upon discovery," but a fault line that already exists within Catholic theology itself.

**2. The CDF's `Responsum ad Dubium` (2021) vs. `Fiducia Supplicans` (2023) — Whether a Same-Sex Couple Can Be Blessed (Score 0.281)**
- **The Issue**: the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith's 2021 response prohibited every form of blessing for a same-sex union, yet a mere two years later, in 2023, Fiducia Supplicans permitted a non-liturgical pastoral blessing.
- **The Anticipated Strongest Rebuttal**: both documents agree that "blessing the marriage itself" remains prohibited. What the 2021 document blocked was the act of "blessing the couple's union," while what the 2023 document permitted was "a pastoral blessing of mercy for the individual" — the levels differ.
- **The Counter-Rebuttal**: however, Fiducia Supplicans explicitly permits being blessed together "as a couple," and in practice, numerous bishops' conferences — including the entire Symposium of Episcopal Conferences of Africa and Madagascar — have publicly objected that "this distinction does not hold up in practice and collides head-on with the 2021 response."
- **Level of Scholarly Consensus**: 🔴 an ongoing dispute — one of the rare cases in which an official document's conclusion was reversed within a mere two years, and the Catholic episcopate itself has openly expressed disagreement.

**3. `Syllabus Errorum` (1864) vs. `Dignitatis Humanae` (1965) — Religious Freedom (Score 0.273)**
- **The Issue**: Pius IX's Syllabus Errorum condemned as error the proposition that "every man has the right freely to embrace and profess the religion he, guided by the light of reason, shall consider to be true," while Vatican II's Dignitatis Humanae declared exactly that proposition to be a right grounded in human dignity.
- **The Anticipated Strongest Rebuttal**: the Syllabus was not an infallible declaration (ex cathedra) but a list of ordinary Magisterial directives, and it targeted a specific 19th-century political anti-clericalism, not an eternal condemnation of "every form of religious freedom."
- **The Counter-Rebuttal**: however, the wording of Syllabus §15 almost exactly matches, down to the phrasing, the proposition affirmed by Dignitatis Humanae §2. Furthermore, even granting the rebuttal that "it was not an infallible declaration," this amounts to admitting the fact that "a non-infallible Magisterial teaching can be reversed a century later" — which fails to rebut, and instead reinforces, the very point this case originally targets (namely, that "the Magisterium's teaching itself can be reversed").
- **Level of Scholarly Consensus**: 🟢 broadly acknowledged in scholarship — this case (Syllabus vs. Dignitatis Humanae) is one of the most frequently cited examples among theologians as a borderline case between "genuine development" and "a substantive reversal" (the controversy surrounding Fr. John Courtney Murray is representative), and few scholars deny the tension itself.

**4. `CCC-1861` vs. Amoris Laetitia, Chapter 8 — The Objective Requirements for Mortal Sin and Pastoral Discernment (Score 0.256)**
- **The Issue**: CCC 1861 stipulates that grave matter, full knowledge, and complete consent are required for mortal sin, while Amoris Laetitia requires individual conscientious discernment for the divorced and remarried, stating that "a state of grace cannot be judged from objective status alone."
- **The Anticipated Strongest Rebuttal**: this is not a contradiction — CCC 1861 itself already specifies the subjective requirements of 'full knowledge' and 'complete consent,' so Amoris Laetitia merely reaffirms the existing conditions and does not create a new principle.
- **The Counter-Rebuttal**: this rebuttal has considerable merit — the two documents share, in principle, the same framework (objective gravity + subjective responsibility). The real dispute lies not in the principle but in its 'application': the 2017 "Correctio Filialis," signed by 231 theologians and clergy, argued that Amoris Laetitia's footnote 351 effectively presumes, as a blanket matter, that remarried persons continuing in sexual relations lack full knowledge and consent, thereby nullifying the objective standard under the guise of individual discernment.
- **Level of Scholarly Consensus**: 🟡 an ongoing dispute — one of the sharpest theological controversies within Catholicism during the pontificate of Pope Francis (including the 2016 "dubia of the four cardinals" affair), where the rebuttal that "there is no contradiction in principle" and the criticism that "the norm has substantively changed" continue to coexist.

**Overall Assessment**: these 4 cases, which survived even after human source-text comparison, were without exception topics already known within Catholic theological circles. That is, the practical value of this automated detection engine lies not in "discovering a new contradiction no one knew about," but rather in "automatically re-discovering already-known controversial topics from within the 258-claim database" — this is not an achievement to be exaggerated, but a limitation to be honestly disclosed.
