# COL-008 — The Circular Logic of Ex Cathedra (The Self-Referential Fallacy of Infallibility)

- **Document A**: VAT1-PASTOR-AETERNUS — "The Roman Pontiff is infallible when he speaks ex cathedra. This very doctrine of infallibility is itself an ex cathedra declaration (First Vatican Council, Pastor Aeternus, 1870)."
- **Document B**: Logic — "A self-referential proposition cannot be independently verified and constitutes the fallacy of circular reasoning (petitio principii)."
- **Collision Type**: Circular Reasoning / Petitio Principii
- **Collision Grade**: Level 4 (Logical structural defect)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: Ex cathedra declarations are infallible
  - **Data**: The proposition "ex cathedra declarations are infallible" is itself an ex cathedra declaration. That is, to verify the truth-value of this proposition, one must first know whether ex cathedra declarations are infallible. But what tells us whether ex cathedra declarations are infallible is precisely this very ex cathedra declaration.
  - **Contradiction**: A is used to prove the truth of A. This is petitio principii in logic.
    - Circular structure: "The pope is infallible" → grounds: "Vatican I declared it" → "why is Vatican I's declaration true?" → "because the pope approved it ex cathedra" → "why is the pope's ex cathedra true?" → "because Vatican I declared it" → ∞ circularity
  - **Conclusion**: Ex cathedra infallibility has no independent grounds outside itself. A self-referential system cannot prove its own consistency (cf. Gödel's incompleteness theorems).
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-15 (Grounding in Sacred Tradition), CE-16 (Scriptural grounding in Matthew 16:18)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal to CE-15: The claim that "Sacred Tradition is the ground" — but it is also the Magisterium that determines the validity of Sacred Tradition. The Magisterium's authority is grounded in Tradition, and the authority to interpret Tradition belongs to the Magisterium. This merely extends the circle by one step; it does not escape it.
  - Rebuttal to CE-16: The claim that "Matthew 16:18 is the ground" — but the very interpretation that Matthew 16:18 signifies papal primacy is itself the Magisterium's interpretation. Scripture → Magisterium's interpretation → Magisterium's authority → authority to interpret Scripture → ∞ circularity. Furthermore, the Eastern Church interprets the same verse entirely differently.
- **Additional Note**: This circular logic becomes even more powerful when combined with COL-003 (Papal infallibility vs. Honorius). When the heresy of Honorius is evaded by saying "it was not ex cathedra," who determines whether it was ex cathedra? The pope himself, or the Magisterium. This returns again to circularity.
