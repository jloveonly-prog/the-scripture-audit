# COL-005 — Priestly Absolution Required vs Forgiveness Possible Through Perfect Contrition

- **Document A**: TRENT-S14-CONFESSION — "In the sacrament of Penance, the priest's absolution is a judicial act necessary for the forgiveness of sin. If anyone says that sacramental confession is not necessary for salvation, let him be anathema."
- **Document B**: CCC_1452 — "Perfect contrition (contritio perfecta) — contrition motivated by a love of God — remits venial sins, and it also obtains forgiveness of mortal sins if it includes the firm resolution to have recourse to sacramental confession as soon as possible."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 4 (Anathema decree vs. catechism, conditional contradiction)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: Trent S14 and CCC 1452 are simultaneously true
  - **Data**: Trent declares that priestly absolution is "necessary" for the forgiveness of sin. CCC 1452 says that perfect contrition alone can bring forgiveness of even mortal sin (prior to priestly absolution).
  - **Contradiction**: If priestly absolution is "necessary," forgiveness cannot be possible without it (through contrition alone). Yet CCC 1452 says forgiveness occurs through contrition alone.
  - **Conclusion**: "Necessary" and "possible without" cannot both be true simultaneously.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-09 (Inclusion of intent to confess), CE-10 (Deathbed exception)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal to CE-09: Even granting the condition "if it includes the intent to confess," the actual forgiveness already occurs at the moment of contrition (CCC 1452's explicit wording). If intent alone can substitute for priestly absolution, then priestly absolution is no longer "necessary" but merely "desirable."
  - Rebuttal to CE-10: Trent specifies no deathbed exception. Adding a situational exception to an absolute statement of "necessity" changes the meaning of the original text.
