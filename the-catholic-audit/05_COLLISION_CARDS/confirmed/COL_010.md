# COL-010 — Even Martyrs Go to Hell Without Exception (Florence) vs. Salvation is Possible for the Ignorant (CCC 846-848)

- **Document A**: COUNCIL-FLORENCE (Council of Florence, Bull Cantate Domino, 1442, De Fide Definita) — "It firmly believes, professes and preaches that all those who are outside the catholic church, not only pagans but also Jews or heretics and schismatics, cannot share in eternal life and will go into the everlasting fire which was prepared for the devil and his angels, unless they are joined to the catholic church before the end of their lives... and that nobody can be saved, no matter how much he has given away in alms and **even if he has shed his blood in the name of Christ**, unless he has persevered in the bosom and the unity of the catholic church."
- **Document B**: CCC-846_848 (Catechism of the Catholic Church 846-848, 1992) — "Those who, through no fault of their own, do not know the Gospel of Christ or his Church, but who nevertheless seek God with a sincere heart... may achieve eternal salvation." It also explicitly rejects the proposition that 'everyone not belonging to the Catholic Church unconditionally goes to hell' (rigorism).
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 4 (De Fide Conciliar Bull vs. Modern Catechism, Direct Contradiction)
- **Detection Path**: Automatic Embedding Detection (Similarity 0.848) → LLM Secondary Review YES → Manual Textual Verification Confirmed (2026-07-08)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: Cantate Domino and CCC 846-848 are simultaneously true.
  - **Data**: Florence specifies "without exception"—that even the extreme act of faith of martyrdom cannot serve as an exception—thereby precluding any category of exception such as ignorance, good will, or conscience at its source. CCC 846-848 acknowledges that very category of exception (invincible ignorance) and condemns the interpretation without exception under the name of 'rigorism.'
  - **Contradiction**: "There is no exception" (A) and "There is an exception" (B) cannot simultaneously be true. Furthermore, B classifies A's own position as an error (rigorism)—an infallible document has been reclassified as an error by a subsequent magisterial document.
  - **Conclusion**: P ∧ ¬P. One must be false—yet A is a document declared to be infallible (incapable of error).
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Theological Development), CE-03 (Retroactive Tradition — "The exception of ignorance was there from the beginning"), CE-05 (Flight to Mystery).
- **Containment**:
  - Refutation of CE-01: The shift from "no exception even for martyrs" to "salvation possible if ignorant" is not a refinement of scope, but a reversal of judgment. That the infallible document of 1442 went out of its way to mention martyrs was precisely to shut all doors of exception that might be opened in the future.
  - Refutation of CE-03: If the exception of 'invincible ignorance' were an apostolic tradition, why was the Council of Florence ignorant of it? If an infallible council omitted an apostolic tradition, its infallibility is broken; and if there was no such tradition, the CCC invented it—a dilemma (L-04).
  - Refutation of CE-05: The collision between "no exception" and "there is an exception" is not a mystery, but elementary logic. To abandon reason is to abandon apologetics.
