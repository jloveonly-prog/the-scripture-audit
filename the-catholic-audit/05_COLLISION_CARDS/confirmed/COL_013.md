# COL-013 — The Condemnation of Religious Liberty (Syllabus 1864) vs. Religious Liberty as a Natural Right (Dignitatis Humanae 1965)

- **Document A**: DENZINGER-SYLLABUS (Pius IX, Syllabus Errorum Articles 77-78, 1864, The Pope's Official Condemnation of Errors) — The following propositions are **condemned as errors**: "In the present day it is no longer expedient that the Catholic religion should be held as the only religion of the State, to the exclusion of all other forms of worship," and "Hence it has been wisely decided by law, in some Catholic countries, that persons coming to reside therein shall enjoy the public exercise of their own peculiar worship." That is, it condemns the allowance of public worship for non-Catholic religions and demands the exclusive status of the Catholic religion by the State.
- **Document B**: DIGNITATIS-HUMANAE (Second Vatican Council, Declaration on Religious Freedom Article 2, 1965) — "This Vatican Council declares that the human person has a right to religious freedom. This freedom means that all men are to be immune from coercion on the part of individuals or of social groups and of any human power... This right of the human person to religious freedom is to be recognized in the constitutional law whereby society is governed and thus it is to become a civil right." It also rejects the proposition that the State must protect only the true religion and suppress false religions.
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 4 (The Pope's Official Syllabus of Errors vs. Conciliar Declaration, Direct Reversal)
- **Detection Path**: Automatic Embedding Detection (VATICAN2-GS ↔ SYLLABUS series, Similarity 0.682) → LLM Secondary Review YES → Direct Document Pair (Syllabus ↔ DH) Confirmed by Manual Textual Verification (2026-07-09)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: The Syllabus and Dignitatis Humanae are simultaneously true.
  - **Data**: A (1864) placed the proposition of religious liberty in the 'syllabus of condemned errors.' B (1965) declared the same proposition as a 'right based on human dignity'—that is, a natural right bestowed by God.
  - **Contradiction**: The identical proposition cannot be, at a century's interval, both a 'condemned error' and a 'right bestowed by God.' Natural law, by definition, does not alter with the times—if it is a natural right in 1965, it was a right in 1864, and if so, the Pope condemned a natural right as an error. P ∧ ¬P.
  - **Conclusion**: If the Magisterium is an institution that condemns errors, this is an event wherein the Magisterium itself acknowledged that its very act of condemning an error was an error.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Doctrinal Development), CE-10 (The Syllabus is not Infallible), CE-02 (Historical Context).
- **Containment**:
  - Refutation of CE-01: 'Condemned Error → Declaration of a Natural Right' is not a development (A → A+) but a reversal of judgment (A → Not-A). To call it a development, one must present what part of the 1864 condemnation remains valid.
  - Refutation of CE-10: If the Syllabus is not infallible, it means even the Pope's official syllabus of errors can be in error—if so, how doth the believer know which magisterial document is in error? The moment one presents a criterion of discernment, the infallibility of that criterion is called into question (Infinite Regression, Structure of CE-08).
  - Refutation of CE-02: To appeal to the "historical context of the 19th century" is to degrade a claim of truth to a product of the times, which is a boomerang that can be applied to the entirety of magisterial documents (Filter 6)—if so, modern documents are also mere contextual products of the 21st century.
