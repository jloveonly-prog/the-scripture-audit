# COL-013 — Religious Freedom Condemned (Syllabus 1864) vs Religious Freedom Is a God-Given Right (Dignitatis Humanae 1965)

- **Document A**: DENZINGER-SYLLABUS (Pius IX, Syllabus of Errors, propositions 77-78, 1864, an official papal condemnation of errors) — **condemns as errors** the following propositions: "It is no longer expedient that the Catholic religion should be held as the only religion of the State," "it is praiseworthy that in some Catholic countries the law has provided that persons coming to reside therein shall enjoy the public exercise of their own worship." That is, it condemns permitting the public worship of non-Catholic religions and demands the state's exclusive Catholic status.
- **Document B**: DIGNITATIS-HUMANAE (Second Vatican Council, Declaration on Religious Freedom §2, 1965) — "All men are to be immune from coercion... in such wise that no one is to be forced to act in a manner contrary to his own beliefs... This right is based on the very dignity of the human person (natural law)." It also rejects the proposition that "the state must protect only the true religion and suppress false religions."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 4 (Official papal list of condemnations vs. conciliar declaration, direct reversal)
- **Detection Path**: Automated embedding detection (VATICAN2-GS↔SYLLABUS series, similarity 0.682) → LLM secondary review: YES → manual source-text comparison confirmed the direct document pair (Syllabus↔DH) (2026-07-09)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: The Syllabus and Dignitatis Humanae are simultaneously true
  - **Data**: A (1864) placed the proposition of religious freedom on the "list of condemned errors." B (1965) declared the same proposition a "natural-law right grounded in human dignity" — that is, a God-given right.
  - **Contradiction**: The same proposition cannot be, a century apart, both a "condemned error" and a "God-given right." Natural law, by definition, does not change with the times — if it was a natural-law right in 1965, it was also a right in 1864, in which case the pope condemned a natural-law right as an error. P ∧ ¬P
  - **Conclusion**: If the Magisterium is the institution that condemns error, then the very act of condemning this "error" is an event in which the Magisterium itself admits that act was itself an error.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Development of doctrine), CE-10 (The Syllabus is not infallible), CE-02 (Historical context)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal to CE-01: 'Condemnation of error → declaration of a God-given right' is not development (A→A+) but a reversal of judgment (A→Not-A). To call it development, one must show which part of the 1864 condemnation remains valid.
  - Rebuttal to CE-10: If the Syllabus is not infallible, then even the pope's official list of condemned errors could itself be in error — in which case, how does the faithful know which Magisterial document is erroneous? The moment a criterion for distinguishing this is proposed, that criterion's own infallibility again becomes the question (infinite regress, the CE-08 structure).
  - Rebuttal to CE-02: The appeal to "19th-century historical context" reduces a truth-claim to a mere product of its historical circumstances — a boomerang (Filter 6) applicable to the entire body of Magisterial documents. By the same logic, modern documents are themselves merely products of their 21st-century context.
