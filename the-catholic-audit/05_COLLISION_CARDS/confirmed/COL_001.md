# COL-001 — Baptism Required vs Muslims Can Be Saved

- **Document A**: CCC_1257 — "Baptism is necessary for salvation. The Lord himself affirms that baptism is necessary for salvation (John 3:5). The Church does not know of any means other than Baptism that assures entry into eternal beatitude."
- **Document B**: LG_16 — "The plan of salvation also includes those who acknowledge the Creator, and among these especially Muslims, who profess the faith of Abraham, and along with us adore the one and merciful God."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 5 (Doctrine vs. Doctrine, direct contradiction)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: CCC 1257 and LG 16 are simultaneously true
  - **Data**: CCC 1257 declares that "without Baptism, salvation cannot be guaranteed." LG 16 declares that "Muslims are included in the plan of salvation." Muslims, by definition, do not receive Christian baptism.
  - **Contradiction**: If baptism is necessary for salvation, then Muslims without baptism cannot be included in the plan of salvation. Yet LG 16 says they are included. P ∧ ¬P
  - **Conclusion**: The two propositions cannot both be true simultaneously. One must be false.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Extension of Baptism of Desire), CE-02 (Plan of salvation ≠ Guarantee of salvation)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal to CE-01: The claim that Baptism of Desire applies to those who do not explicitly know Christ is contradicted by CCC 1257, which explicitly states "the Church does not know of any means other than Baptism." Furthermore, Muslims belong to a religious system that explicitly rejects Christ.
  - Rebuttal to CE-02: The claim that "included in the plan of salvation" differs from "guarantee of salvation" fails, because if inclusion in the plan of salvation coexists with the impossibility of salvation, that inclusion is meaningless — and the context of LG 16 clearly implies the possibility of salvation.
