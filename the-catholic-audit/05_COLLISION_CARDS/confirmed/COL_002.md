# COL-002 — Grace is a Gift vs Justification is Impossible without Works

- **Document A**: CCC_1996 — "Our justification comes from the grace of God. Grace is favor, the free and undeserved help that God gives us... It is supernatural, transcending the power of human intellect and will, and springs solely from the free initiative of God."
- **Document B**: TRENT_S06_C09 — "If any man shall say, that by faith alone (sola fide) the impious is justified, in such wise as to mean, that nothing else is required to co-operate in order to the obtaining the grace of Justification, and that it is not in any way necessary, that he be prepared and disposed by the movement of his own will; let him be anathema (anathema sit)."
- **Type of Collision**: A → Not A
- **Grade of Collision**: Level 5 (Dogma vs Anathematizing Decree, Direct Contradiction)
- **Reductio ad absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: CCC 1996 and Trent S06 C09 are both true at once.
  - **Data**: CCC 1996 defineth grace as a "gratuitous gift" (undeserved help). Trent S06 C09 declareth that for justification, the "preparation and disposition of one's own will" (human work) is absolutely necessary.
  - **Contradiction**: If a gift be truly a gift, it cannot be conditional. Yet Trent declareth that without human works, grace cannot be obtained. Given freely (Unconditional) ∧ Conditional = P ∧ ¬P
  - **Conclusion**: "A gratuitous gift" and "Impossible without works" cannot both be true at once.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasions**: CE-03 (Distinction of Cooperative Grace), CE-04 (Distinction of Prevenient Grace / Subsequent Grace)
- **Blockade**:
  - Refutation of CE-03: If "cooperation with grace" be a condition, it is no longer a "free and undeserved gift." The demand for cooperation is itself a condition, and a conditional gift contradicteth the very definition of a gift.
  - Refutation of CE-04: The distinction that prevenient grace is unconditional and subsequent grace is conditional collideth with the comprehensive definition of CCC 1996 that "Grace is a gift." CCC 1996 defineth grace in general as a gift, not merely a certain kind of grace.
