# COL-002 — Grace Is a Gift vs No Justification Without Works

- **Document A**: CCC_1996 — "Grace is favor, the free and undeserved help God gives us... Grace is a participation in the life of God... it is supernatural and beyond the powers of intellect and will, and comes only from God's free initiative."
- **Document B**: TRENT_S06_C09 — "If anyone says that by faith alone the impious is justified... without the necessity of the preparation and disposition of his own will, let him be anathema."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 5 (Doctrine vs. anathema decree, direct contradiction)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: CCC 1996 and Trent S06 C09 are simultaneously true
  - **Data**: CCC 1996 defines grace as a "gratuitous gift." Trent S06 C09 declares that "the preparation and disposition of one's own will" (human works) is necessary for justification.
  - **Contradiction**: If a gift is a true gift, it cannot be conditional. Yet Trent declares that grace cannot be received without human works. Freely given (unconditional) ∧ conditional = P ∧ ¬P
  - **Conclusion**: "A gift freely given" and "impossible without works" cannot both be true simultaneously.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-03 (Distinguishing cooperative grace), CE-04 (Distinguishing prevenient/subsequent grace)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal to CE-03: If "cooperation with grace" is a condition, it is no longer a "gift freely given." The very demand for cooperation is itself a condition, and a conditional gift contradicts the definition of a gift.
  - Rebuttal to CE-04: The distinction that prevenient grace is unconditional while subsequent grace is conditional conflicts with CCC 1996's comprehensive definition that "grace is a gift." CCC 1996 defines grace in general as a gift, not only a specific kind of grace.
