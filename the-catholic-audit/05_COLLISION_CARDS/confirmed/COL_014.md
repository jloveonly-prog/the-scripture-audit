# COL-014 — Allowance of Sacraments for Non-Catholics (Canon 844) vs. No Salvation Outside the Church (Lateran IV)

> **Promotion History**: Registered as CAND-001 (2026-07-09) → Core defense ("Reception of Sacraments = Implicit Incorporation into the Church") definitively blocked in the OODA Promotion Hearing (2026-07-21) → Promoted to confirmed. See below for the record of the hearing.

- **Document A**: CANON-SACR (1983 Code of Canon Law, Canon 844) — "If the danger of death is present or if, in the judgment of the diocesan bishop or conference of bishops, some other grave necessity urges it, Catholic ministers administer these same sacraments (penance, Eucharist, and anointing of the sick) licitly also to other Christians not having full communion with the Catholic Church, who cannot approach a minister of their own community and who seek such on their own accord, provided that they manifest Catholic faith in respect to these sacraments and are properly disposed."
- **Document B**: COUNCIL-LATERAN_IV (Fourth Lateran Council, 1215, De Fide) — "There is but one universal Church of the faithful, outside of which there is absolutely no salvation."
- **Collision Type**: Practical Contradiction (Logic Filter 4 — Collision of Implication with its own definition that Sacraments = Channels of Salvific Grace)
- **Collision Grade**: Level 2 (Canon Law vs. Conciliar Dogma)
- **Detection Path**: Automatic Embedding Detection (0.727) → LLM Review YES (2 independent reviews: vs. Lateran IV, vs. Unam Sanctam) → Manual Verification → OODA Promotion Hearing Passed (2026-07-21)
- **Core Argument (Dilemma L-04)**:
  - By Catholic definition, the sacraments truly confer salvific grace (CCC 1129 "The Church affirms that for believers the sacraments of the New Covenant are necessary for salvation," ex opere operato).
  - Canon 844 permits the administration of the sacraments of penance, Eucharist, and anointing of the sick to a non-Catholic facing death whilst remaining outside the Church.
  - (a) If that sacrament conveys valid salvific grace → A channel of salvation operates outside the Church → Lateran IV collapses.
  - (b) If it does not convey grace → Canon Law officially commands a meaningless ritual → The doctrine of the ex opere operato efficacy of the sacraments collapses.
  - Whichever side one chooses, an internal document collapses.

## ⚖️ Record of the OODA Promotion Hearing (2026-07-21, CVCAP_Pipeline.md Procedure)

**Defense Under Hearing**: 🔵 "The moment a non-Catholic receives the sacraments, he is deemed to be (invisibly) incorporated into the Church. Thus, it is not salvation 'outside the Church'." (Ecclesiological Incorporation Logic)

**R1 🔴 Prosecutor**: The conditions for administration in Canon 844 §3-4 are not 'initiation into the Catholic Church,' but merely "manifest Catholic faith in respect to these sacraments and are properly disposed." Even after reception, the recipient is **continually classified as a non-Catholic** under Canon Law—if he were incorporated, why doth Canon Law itself continue to call him a 'non-Catholic Christian'?

**R2 🔵 Apologetic**: "It is not a visible incorporation, but an invisible and spiritual incorporation."

**R3 🔴 Prosecutor (Decisive Blow)**: That distinction is blocked by the original text of the Council of Florence (1442, De Fide)—Florence declared, "**even if he has shed his blood in the name of Christ**, unless he has persevered in the bosom and the unity of the catholic church, he cannot be saved." Martyrdom is a mark of 'invisible incorporation' far more potent than the reception of a sacrament upon the deathbed. **A document tradition that did not acknowledge even a martyr as 'incorporated' cannot deem a Protestant who hath partaken of a sacrament but once as 'incorporated.'** If the logic of invisible incorporation is true, the exclusionary clause of Florence regarding martyrs is rendered meaningless.

**R4 🔵 Apologetic (CE-10)**: "Canon Law is discipline (disciplina), not dogma, thus the grades differ."

**R5 🔴 Prosecutor**: The theology that a discipline presupposes bears dogmatic implications (lex orandi, lex credendi). A discipline that 'commands' the valid conferral of grace unto a non-Catholic is in practical contradiction with the De Fide doctrine of "no grace outside the Church"—exactly as blocked by GHQ CE-09: "If pastoral care practically neutralizes a De Fide dogma, it is an alteration."

**R6 🔵 Apologetic**: Restatement of 'invisible incorporation' (No new argument, 1st repetition).

**R7-R9**: Identical arguments repeated in the 2nd and 3rd iterations—Forced termination condition met (identical argument 3 times).

**R10 ⚖️ Mediator's Verdict**: The incorporation logic (i) contradicts Canon Law's own classification, (ii) contradicts the exclusionary clause of Florence regarding martyrs, and (iii) degrades EENS (Extra Ecclesiam nulla salus) into a tautology ("one who receives grace = inside by definition"), deleting the clear exclusionary intent of the original documents. **Blockade Confirmed → Promoted to confirmed.**
