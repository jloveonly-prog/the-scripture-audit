# COL-014 — Sacraments Permitted for Non-Catholics (Canon 844) vs No Salvation Outside the Church (Lateran IV)

> **Promotion History**: Registered as CAND-001 (2026-07-09) → in the OODA promotion hearing of 2026-07-21, the core defense ("receiving a sacrament = implicit union with the Church") was confirmed blocked → promoted to confirmed. See the hearing record below.

- **Document A**: CANON-SACR (1983 Code of Canon Law, Canon 844) — "In danger of death, or under other grave necessity, non-Catholic Christians who satisfy certain conditions (a profession of Catholic faith regarding the relevant sacrament + proper disposition) may licitly be given the sacraments (Penance, the Eucharist, Anointing of the Sick)."
- **Document B**: COUNCIL-LATERAN_IV (Fourth Lateran Council, 1215, De Fide) — "There is but one universal Church of the faithful, outside of which no one at all is saved."
- **Collision Type**: Practical contradiction (Logical Filter 4 — a conflict of implication with the Church's own definition that sacraments are the channel of saving grace)
- **Collision Grade**: Level 2 (Canon law vs. conciliar doctrine)
- **Detection Path**: Automated embedding detection (0.727) → LLM review: YES (two independent runs: vs. Lateran IV, vs. Unam Sanctam) → manual comparison → passed OODA promotion hearing (2026-07-21)
- **Core Argument (Dilemma L-04)**:
  - By Catholicism's own definition, sacraments actually confer saving grace (CCC 1129: "the sacraments are necessary for salvation," ex opere operato efficacy).
  - Canon 844 permits Penance, the Eucharist, and Anointing of the Sick to be given to a dying non-Catholic who remains outside the Church.
  - (a) If that sacrament conveys valid saving grace → a channel of salvation operates outside the Church → Lateran IV collapses.
  - (b) If it does not convey grace → canon law officially mandates a meaningless rite → the doctrine of the sacraments' efficacy collapses.
  - Whichever option is chosen, one internal document falls.

## ⚖️ OODA Promotion Hearing Record (2026-07-21, per the CVCAP_Pipeline.md procedure)

**Defense under review**: 🔵 "The moment a non-Catholic receives a sacrament, they are (invisibly) considered united to the Church. Therefore this is not 'salvation outside the Church.'" (The ecclesiological-incorporation argument)

**R1 🔴 Examination**: The conditions for administration under Canon 844 §3-4 are not 'entry into the Catholic Church' but merely "a profession of Catholic faith regarding the relevant sacrament + proper disposition." Even after receiving the sacrament, the recipient continues to be canonically classified as **non-Catholic** — if union had occurred, why does canon law itself still call them a 'non-Catholic Christian'?

**R2 🔵 Defense**: "It is not a visible union but an invisible, spiritual union."

**R3 🔴 Examination (the decisive blow)**: That very distinction is foreclosed by the original text of the Council of Florence (1442, De Fide) — Florence declared that "**even if they shed their blood in the name of Christ [martyrdom]**," they cannot be saved unless they remain within the Catholic Church. Martyrdom is a far stronger sign of 'invisible union' than receiving a sacrament on one's deathbed. **A textual tradition that did not even recognize martyrs as 'united' cannot regard a Protestant who received one sacrament as 'united.'** If the logic of invisible union were true, Florence's exclusion clause for martyrs would become meaningless.

**R4 🔵 Defense (CE-10)**: "Canon law is discipline (disciplina), not doctrine, so it is of a different rank."

**R5 🔴 Examination**: The theology presupposed by a discipline carries doctrinal implications (lex orandi, lex credendi). A discipline that 'commands' the conferral of valid grace upon non-Catholics contradicts, in practice, the De Fide teaching of "no grace outside the Church" — exactly as GHQ's CE-09 blocking states: "if pastoral consideration nullifies De Fide in practice, that is an alteration."

**R6 🔵 Defense**: Reassertion of 'invisible union' (no new argument, round 1).

**R7-R9**: The same argument repeated for rounds 2 and 3 — the forced-termination condition (same argument three times) is met.

**R10 ⚖️ Arbiter's Ruling**: The incorporation logic (i) contradicts canon law's own classification, (ii) contradicts Florence's exclusion clause for martyrs, and (iii) degrades EENS (Extra Ecclesiam Nulla Salus) into a tautology ("those who receive grace = by definition inside"), erasing the original document's plain intent to exclude. **Blocking confirmed → promoted to confirmed.**

> **Verdict**: 💥 IMPLOSION (practical-contradiction type) | **Level of scholarly consensus**: 🟡 (a genuine debate exists between traditionalist and inclusivist camps)
