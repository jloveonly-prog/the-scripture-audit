# COL-009 — The Absolute Necessity of Subjection to the Pope (Unam Sanctam) vs. The Salvation of Those Ignorant of the Gospel (LG 16)

- **Document A**: UNAM-SANCTAM (Boniface VIII, 1302, De Fide Definita) — "Outside of the one, holy, catholic, and apostolic Church there is neither salvation nor remission of sins... Furthermore, we declare, we proclaim, we define that it is absolutely necessary for salvation that every human creature be subject to the Roman Pontiff."
- **Document B**: LG-16 (Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium 16, 1964) — "Those also can attain to salvation who through no fault of their own do not know the Gospel of Christ or His Church, yet sincerely seek God and moved by grace strive by their deeds to do His will as it is known to them through the dictates of conscience. Nor does Divine Providence deny the helps necessary for salvation to those who, without blame on their part, have not yet arrived at an explicit knowledge of God and with His grace strive to live a good life. Whatever good or truth is found amongst them is looked upon by the Church as a preparation for the Gospel." (Note: Also explicitly includes Muslims).
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 5 (De Fide Dogmatic Definition vs. Conciliar Dogmatic Constitution, Direct Contradiction)
- **Detection Path**: Automatic Embedding Detection (Similarity 0.906) → LLM Secondary Review YES → Manual Textual Verification Confirmed (2026-07-08)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: Unam Sanctam and LG 16 are simultaneously true.
  - **Data**: Unam Sanctam, using a formula of definition (definimus), declares De Fide that subjection to the Pope is "absolutely necessary" for the salvation of every human creature. LG 16 teaches that those who know not the Gospel (let alone the Pope), including Muslims, can attain salvation. By definition, Muslims are not subject to the Roman Pontiff.
  - **Contradiction**: If subjection to the Pope is absolutely (omnino) necessary, then Muslims who do not subject themselves cannot attain salvation. Yet LG 16 says they can. P ∧ ¬P.
  - **Conclusion**: The two propositions cannot simultaneously be true. One must be false—yet A is a document declared to be infallible (incapable of error).
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Theological Development), CE-10 (Difference in Theological Grade), CE-02 (Magisterial Authority of Interpretation).
- **Containment**:
  - Refutation of CE-01: Development is A → A+. From "Without subjection, salvation is absolutely (omnino) impossible" to "Salvation is possible even without knowing the Gospel, let alone subjection" is not an expansion, but a reversal. The universal adverb 'omnino' itself blocked any room for exception.
  - Refutation of CE-10: Unam Sanctam is a De Fide document furnished with a formula of definition. If a document of a lower grade (the pastoral description of a dogmatic constitution) practically nullifies a higher De Fide definition, it is an admission that the grading system itself is merely a "device to bypass inconvenient infallible declarations."
  - Refutation of CE-02: That the Magisterium, possessing the authority of interpretation, taught diametrically opposite things in 1302 and 1964 is the very data of this card. Appealing to the authority of interpretation does not resolve the problem; it only restates it.
