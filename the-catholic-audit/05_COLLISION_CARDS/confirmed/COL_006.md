# COL-006 — Purgatory Is a Necessary Purification vs Indulgences Can Reduce It

- **Document A**: CCC_1030 — "All who die in God's grace and friendship, but still imperfectly purified, are indeed assured of their eternal salvation; but after death they undergo purification, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven."
- **Document B**: CCC_1471 — "An indulgence is a remission before God of the temporal punishment due to sins... A properly disposed member of the Christian faithful can obtain an indulgence, through the Church's action, for himself or for the dead."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A (Teleological contradiction)
- **Collision Grade**: Level 3 (Internal tension within the catechism, systematic inconsistency)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: Purgatory is a "necessary purification process" and is simultaneously "reducible through indulgences"
  - **Data**: CCC 1030 defines purgatory as "necessary purification." CCC 1471 says this purification can be shortened or waived through indulgences.
  - **Contradiction**: If purification is "necessary," it cannot be waived. Medical analogy: if surgery is "necessary," it is contradictory to be exempted from it with money. A necessary process cannot be skipped.
  - **Conclusion**: "Necessary purification" and "waivable purification" cannot both be true simultaneously. If purgatory is necessary, indulgences are impossible; if indulgences are possible, purgatory is not necessary.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-11 (Distinguishing temporal punishment from purification), CE-12 (Treasury of Merit)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal to CE-11: There is an attempt to distinguish "remission of temporal punishment" from "shortening of purification," but the CCC itself links purgatory's purpose to "purification of temporal punishment." If temporal punishment is remitted, there is nothing left to purify.
  - Rebuttal to CE-12: The claim that indulgences are possible through the "Treasury of Merit" raises a separate problem — substituting one's own necessary purification with the merits of another nullifies the very rationale for purgatory, namely justice and personal purification.
