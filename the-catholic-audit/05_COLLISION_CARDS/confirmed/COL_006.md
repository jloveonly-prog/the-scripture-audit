# COL-006 — Purgatory as a Necessary Purification vs. Mitigation by Indulgences

- **Document A**: CCC-1030 — "All who die in God's grace and friendship, but still imperfectly purified, are indeed assured of their eternal salvation; but after death they undergo purification, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven."
- **Document B**: CCC-1471 — "An indulgence is a remission before God of the temporal punishment due to sins whose guilt has already been forgiven, which the faithful Christian who is duly disposed gains under certain prescribed conditions through the action of the Church... one can obtain indulgences for oneself or for the dead."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A (Teleological Contradiction)
- **Collision Grade**: Level 3 (Internal Tension of the Catechism, Systematic Inconsistency)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: Purgatory is a "necessary purification" and it is "mitigable" by indulgences.
  - **Data**: CCC 1030 defines purgatory as a "necessary purification." CCC 1471 states that this purification can be shortened or remitted by indulgences.
  - **Contradiction**: If the purification is "necessary," it cannot be remitted. By way of medical analogy: if surgery is "necessary," being exempted from it by paying money is a contradiction. A necessary process cannot be bypassed.
  - **Conclusion**: A "necessary purification" and a "remissible purification" cannot simultaneously be true. If purgatory is necessary, indulgences are impossible; and if indulgences are possible, purgatory is not necessary.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-11 (Distinction between temporal punishment and purification), CE-12 (The Treasury of Merit).
- **Containment**:
  - Refutation of CE-11: Though an attempt is made to distinguish the "remission of temporal punishment" from the "shortening of purification," the CCC itself connects the purpose of purgatory to the "purification of temporal punishment." If temporal punishment is remitted, there is nothing left to purify.
  - Refutation of CE-12: The claim that indulgences are possible through the "Treasury of Merit" begets another problem—to substitute one's necessary purification with the merits of another nullifies justice and the very reason for purgatory, which is personal purification.
