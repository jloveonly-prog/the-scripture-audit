# COL-003 — Papal Infallibility vs. The Condemnation of Pope Honorius I as a Heretic

- **Document A**: VAT1-PASTOR-AETERNUS — "The Roman Pontiff, when he speaks ex cathedra, that is, when in discharge of the office of pastor and teacher of all Christians, by virtue of his supreme apostolic authority, he defines a doctrine regarding faith or morals to be held by the universal Church, is possessed of that infallibility with which the divine Redeemer willed that His Church should be endowed."
- **Document B**: COUNCIL-CONST_III — "The Third Council of Constantinople (A.D. 681) officially condemned Pope Honorius I for the heresy of Monothelitism. This condemnation was subsequently reaffirmed by the Fourth Council of Constantinople and the Second Council of Nicaea."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 5 (Dogma vs. Conciliar Decision, Collision of Historical Fact and Dogma)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: The Pope is infallible concerning faith and morals (Vatican I).
  - **Data**: Pope Honorius I taught Monothelitism. The Third Council of Constantinople (a council acknowledged as valid by the Catholic Church) condemned Honorius as a heretic.
  - **Contradiction**: If the Pope is infallible, Honorius cannot be a heretic. Yet, a council accepted as valid by the Catholic Church condemned the Pope as a heretic. An infallible Pope ∧ A heretic Pope = P ∧ ¬P.
  - **Conclusion**: The dogma of Papal Infallibility and the condemnation of Honorius as a heretic cannot simultaneously be true.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-05 (Honorius did not speak ex cathedra), CE-06 (Condemnation for heresy ≠ Promulgation of a heretical dogma).
- **Containment**:
  - Refutation of CE-05: To claim "it was not ex cathedra" is a retroactive application. The concept of ex cathedra was not defined until A.D. 1870. The council fathers of 681 simply condemned Honorius as Pope. Furthermore, when this evasion is combined with COL-008 (the circular logic of ex cathedra), it is rendered utterly impotent.
  - Refutation of CE-06: The epistles of Honorius contain dogmatic substance (concerning the doctrine of the will of Christ) and functioned as doctrinal instruction for the universal Church. If this be not the teaching of doctrine, what is it?
