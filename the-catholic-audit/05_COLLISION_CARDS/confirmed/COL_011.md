# COL-011 — Anathema for Denying the Necessity of Baptism (Trent) vs. Salvation of Those Ignorant of the Gospel (LG 16)

- **Document A**: TRENT-S07-C05 (Council of Trent, Session VII, Decree on Baptism Canon 5, 1547, De Fide + Anathema) — "If any one saith, that baptism is free, that is, not necessary unto salvation; let him be anathema." (Latin: "Si quis dixerit, baptismum liberum esse, hoc est non necessarium ad salutem: anathema sit.")
- **Document B**: LG-16 (Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium 16, 1964) — "Those also can attain to salvation who through no fault of their own do not know the Gospel of Christ or His Church, yet sincerely seek God and moved by grace strive by their deeds to do His will... Nor does Divine Providence deny the helps necessary for salvation to those who, without blame on their part, have not yet arrived at an explicit knowledge of God."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 5 (De Fide Anathema vs. Conciliar Dogmatic Constitution, Direct Contradiction)
- **Detection Path**: Automatic Embedding Detection (Similarity 0.824) → LLM Secondary Review YES → Manual Textual Verification Confirmed (2026-07-08)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Premise**: Trent S07 Canon 5 and LG 16 are simultaneously true.
  - **Data**: Trent anathematizes him who denies that "baptism is necessary unto salvation." LG 16 teaches that one who knows neither the Gospel nor the Church—and therefore can neither receive baptism nor explicitly desire it—can attain salvation.
  - **Contradiction**: The proposition that "there can exist a person who attains salvation whilst lacking baptism" (LG 16) is a practical restatement of the proposition "baptism is not necessary unto salvation," which Trent blocked with an anathema. P ∧ ¬P.
  - **Conclusion**: If one had spoken the teaching of LG 16 in 1547, he would have been subject to anathema. A council claiming to be infallible transgresses the anathema of a council claiming to be infallible.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Theological Development), CE-10 (Difference in Grade), Expansion of Baptism of Desire (votum baptismi — Trent S06-CH04).
- **Containment**:
  - Refutation of Baptism of Desire: The votum (desire for baptism) of Trent S06-CH04 is for the catechumen who **knows and explicitly desires** baptism. The subject of LG 16 is one who does not even know the Gospel—one cannot desire what one does not know. The moment one expands this to an 'implicit desire,' the votum becomes another name for "a good life independent of baptism," which is practically identical to the proposition blocked by Canon 5 ("baptism is not necessary").
  - Refutation of CE-01: The shift from "necessary (necessarium)" to "possible even if ignorant" is not a refinement, but a reversal of category. If it is a development, answer whether the anathema of 1547 remains valid today—if it is valid, LG 16 transgresses it; if it is invalid, it means a De Fide anathema can be abrogated, which causes infallibility to collapse (Dilemma L-04).
  - Refutation of CE-10: Canon 5 is of the highest De Fide grade, accompanied by an anathema. If a lower document practically nullifies a higher anathema, the grading system itself collapses.
