# COL-011 — Anathema for Denying Baptism's Necessity (Trent) vs Salvation for Those Who Do Not Know the Gospel (LG 16)

- **Document A**: TRENT-S07-C05 (Council of Trent, Seventh Session, Decree on Baptism, Canon 5, 1547, De Fide + Anathema) — "If anyone says that baptism is optional (ad libitum), that is, not necessary for salvation, let him be anathema (anathema sit)." (Latin: "Si quis dixerit, baptismum liberum esse, hoc est non necessarium ad salutem: anathema sit.")
- **Document B**: LG-16 (Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium 16, 1964) — "Those who, through no fault of their own, do not know the Gospel of Christ or his Church, but who nevertheless seek God with a sincere heart, and, moved by grace, try in their actions to do his will as they know it through the dictates of their conscience, can attain eternal salvation. Divine Providence does not deny the help necessary for salvation to such persons."
- **Collision Type**: A → Not A
- **Collision Grade**: Level 5 (De Fide anathema clause vs. conciliar dogmatic constitution, direct contradiction)
- **Detection Path**: Automated embedding detection (similarity 0.824) → LLM secondary review: YES → manual source-text comparison confirmed (2026-07-08)
- **Reductio ad Absurdum**:
  - **Assumption**: Trent S07 Canon 5 and LG 16 are simultaneously true
  - **Data**: Trent anathematizes anyone who denies that "baptism is necessary for salvation." LG 16 teaches that a person who knows neither the Gospel nor the Church — and therefore can neither receive baptism nor explicitly desire it — can attain salvation.
  - **Contradiction**: The proposition that "a person can attain salvation while lacking baptism" (LG 16) is a substantive restatement of the proposition Trent foreclosed by anathema: "baptism is not necessary for salvation." P ∧ ¬P
  - **Conclusion**: Had LG 16's teaching been spoken in 1547, it would have been subject to anathema. A council held to be infallible runs afoul of the anathema clause of a council held to be infallible.
- **Anticipated Catholic Evasion**: CE-01 (Theological development), CE-10 (Difference in rank), extension of baptism of desire (votum baptismi — Trent S06-CH04)
- **Blocking**:
  - Rebuttal via baptism of desire: The votum (desire for baptism) of Trent S06-CH04 is for catechumens who **know of baptism and explicitly desire it**. The subjects of LG 16 do not know the Gospel itself — one cannot desire what one does not know. The moment this is extended to an "implicit desire," votum becomes merely another name for "a good life unrelated to baptism," which becomes substantively identical to the proposition Canon 5 foreclosed ("baptism is not necessary").
  - Rebuttal to CE-01: The shift from "necessary (necessarium)" to "possible without knowledge" is not a refinement but a reversal of category. If this is development, then answer whether the 1547 anathema clause remains valid today — if valid, LG 16 violates it; if invalid, then a De Fide anathema clause can be abolished, and infallibility collapses (dilemma L-04).
  - Rebuttal to CE-10: Canon 5 is of the highest De Fide rank, accompanied by anathema. If a lower-ranked document substantively nullifies a higher anathema clause, the ranking system itself collapses.
