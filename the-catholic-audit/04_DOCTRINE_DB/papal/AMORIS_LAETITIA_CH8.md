# AMORIS-LAETITIA-CH8 — The Implied Eucharistic Access for the Remarried

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|

| **Anathema** | ❌ No |
| **Year** | 2016 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> **§300**: "A process of accompaniment and discernment (discernimiento) of conscience guides these faithful..."
> **§301**: "It can no longer simply be said that all those in any 'irregular' situation are living in a state of mortal sin... it is possible that in an objective situation of sin... a person can be living in God's grace."
> **§305**: "...a pastor cannot feel simply satisfied by applying moral laws... Discernment must help to find possible ways of responding to God and growing in the midst of limitations."
> **Footnote 351**: "In certain cases, this can include the help of the sacraments" — widely interpreted as implying the possibility of Eucharistic access for the remarried.

## Tags
`sacramental_theology`, `marriage`, `eucharist`, `ecclesiology`, `pastoral`, `conditional`

## Claims
1. An individual process of discernment of conscience is needed for those in irregular situations (e.g., divorced and remarried)
2. Even those objectively in an irregular situation may have diminished subjective responsibility
3. Due to mitigating factors, such persons may be living in a state of grace
4. It is implied that those in irregular situations may, "in certain cases," receive the help of the sacraments (including the Eucharist)
5. Pastoral accompaniment and discernment can take precedence over the mechanical application of general norms

## Negates
1. The proposition that all who are divorced and remarried are automatically excluded from receiving the Eucharist (a blanket prohibition)
2. The proposition that no process of discernment whatsoever is needed for those in irregular situations
3. The proposition that a state of grace can be judged from objective status alone
