# DENZINGER-SYLLABUS — The Syllabus of Errors and the Denial of Religious Freedom

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | DENZINGER-SYLLABUS |
| **Source** | Pope Pius IX, Syllabus Errorum (DH 2803-2804) |
| **Reference** | Condemned Propositions 77, 78 |
| **Doctrinal Rank** | Sententia Certa / De Fide (infallibility disputed) |
| **Infallibility** | 🔴 🟡 (authoritative within Catholicism as an official papal condemnation) |
| **Anathema** | Yes (condemned as error) |
| **Year** | 1864 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The following propositions are condemned and rejected: "In the present day it is no longer expedient that the Catholic religion should be held as the only religion of the State," "It is praiseworthy... to allow, on the migration of peoples into Catholic countries, the public exercise of their own worship." That is, it opposes permitting the public worship of non-Catholic religions.

## Tags
`syllabus_of_errors`, `opposition_to_religious_freedom`, `establishmentarianism`

## Claims (Most Important)
1. In a Catholic country, only the Catholic religion should enjoy exclusive status as the sole state religion.
2. Non-Catholics or adherents of other religions residing within a Catholic country must not be permitted the right to publicly worship according to their own religion.

## Negates (Most Important)
1. Every human being has the right to religious freedom — to choose a religion according to their own conscience and to worship publicly (a complete collision with the core claim of the Second Vatican Council's Dignitatis Humanae).

# DENZINGER-FEENEY — The Condemnation of Feeneyism and the "Baptism of Desire"

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | DENZINGER-FEENEY |
| **Source** | Letter of the Holy Office (now the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith) (DH 3866-3873) |
| **Reference** | Letter to the Archbishop of Boston |
| **Doctrinal Rank** | Authoritative Magisterial declaration |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | Fr. Feeney was excommunicated |
| **Year** | 1949 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The doctrine "there is no salvation outside the Church" is true, but this does not mean that only those visibly registered in the Catholic Church and who have received water baptism are saved. An "unconscious or implicit desire" (votum) for the Church alone is sufficient to obtain the grace necessary for salvation.

## Tags
`soteriology`, `condemnation_of_rigorism`, `baptism_of_desire`

## Claims (Most Important)
1. Even a person who has not received visible water baptism and is not externally registered in the Catholic Church can be saved if they possess a good conscience toward God and an implicit desire.
2. The extreme rigorism of Feeneyism, which restricts "no salvation outside the Church" solely to visible church membership and water baptism, is heretical.

## Negates (Most Important)
1. An implicit desire or good conscience can never substitute for baptism, and one must unconditionally be within the visible Church to be saved (paradoxically comparable to the rigorism of the Council of Florence).
