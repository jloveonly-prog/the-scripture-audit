# TRENT-S14-CONFESSION — Priestly Absolution Is Necessary for the Forgiveness of Sin

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | TRENT-S14-CONFESSION |
| **Source** | Council of Trent (Concilium Tridentinum) |
| **Reference** | Fourteenth Session, Decree on the Sacrament of Penance, Canons 6, 9 |
| **Doctrinal Rank** | De Fide |
| **Anathema** | ✅ Yes |
| **Year** | 1551 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> "If anyone says that the priest's sacramental absolution (absolutio sacramentalis) is merely a function of proclaiming or declaring the Gospel: let him be anathema. Likewise, if anyone denies that confession before a priest for the forgiveness of mortal sins committed after baptism is necessary by divine law (iure divino): let him be anathema."

## Latin Text
> "Si quis dixerit, absolutionem sacramentalem sacerdotis non esse actum iudicialem... anathema sit." (Canon 9)
> "Si quis negaverit, confessionem sacramentalem vel institutam vel ad salutem necessariam esse iure divino... anathema sit." (Canon 6)

## Tags
`sacramental_theology`, `confession`, `ordination`, `ecclesiology`, `de_fide`, `necessary`, `prohibited`

## Claims
1. Priestly absolution (absolutio) is not a mere declaration but a judicial act (actus iudicialis)
2. Confession before a priest, for the forgiveness of mortal sin committed after baptism, is necessary by divine law
3. The sacrament of confession was instituted by Christ
4. Only the priest holds the authority to forgive sins (grounded in John 20:23)
5. Contrition (contritio) alone is not sufficient for the forgiveness of sins — priestly absolution is required

## Negates
1. The proposition that priestly absolution is merely the proclamation or declaration of the Gospel
2. The proposition that sins are forgiven by confessing directly to God without a priest
3. The proposition that the sacrament of confession was not instituted by divine law
4. The proposition that every believer holds the authority to forgive sins (an expanded reading of the priesthood of all believers)
