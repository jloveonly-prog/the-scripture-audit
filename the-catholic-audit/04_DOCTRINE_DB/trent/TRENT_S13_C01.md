# TRENT-S13-C01 — Transubstantiation: The Change of Substance of Bread and Wine

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | TRENT-S13-C01 |
| **Source** | Council of Trent (Concilium Tridentinum) |
| **Reference** | Thirteenth Session, Decree on the Most Holy Eucharist, Canon 1 |
| **Doctrinal Rank** | De Fide |
| **Anathema** | ✅ Yes |
| **Year** | 1551 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> "If anyone denies that the body and blood, together with the soul and divinity, of our Lord Jesus Christ, and consequently the whole Christ, are truly, really, and substantially (vere, realiter ac substantialiter) contained in the sacrament of the most holy Eucharist, but says that Christ is present in it only as in a sign, or figure, or by his power: let him be anathema."

## Latin Text
> "Si quis negaverit, in sanctissimae Eucharistiae sacramento contineri vere, realiter et substantialiter corpus et sanguinem una cum anima et divinitate Domini nostri Iesu Christi ac proinde totum Christum, sed dixerit tantummodo esse in eo ut in signo vel figura aut virtute: anathema sit."

## Tags
`sacramental_theology`, `eucharist`, `de_fide`, `necessary`, `prohibited`

## Claims
1. In the Eucharist, the body and blood of Christ are truly (vere), really (realiter), and substantially (substantialiter) present
2. Christ's soul and divinity are also present together — that is, the whole Christ (totus Christus) is present
3. After consecration, the substance of the bread is changed into the substance of Christ's body, and the substance of the wine into the substance of Christ's blood (Transubstantiation, Transsubstantiatio)
4. This presence is not by way of a sign (ut in signo), figure (figura), or power (virtus)

## Negates
1. The proposition that the Eucharist is merely a sign (signum) or figure (figura)
2. The proposition that Christ is present in the Eucharist only by power (virtus) (Calvinism)
3. The proposition that the Eucharist is a mere memorial (Zwinglianism)
4. The proposition that the substance of the bread and wine remains unchanged even after consecration (Consubstantiation)
