# TRENT-S13-C01 — Transubstantiation: The conversion of the substance of bread and wine

| Item | Description |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | TRENT-S13-C01 |
| **Source** | Council of Trent (Concilium Tridentinum) |
| **Article** | Session 13, Decree on the Most Holy Eucharist, Canon 1 |
| **Dogmatic Grade** | De Fide |
| **Anathema** | ✅ Yes |
| **Year** | 1551 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> "If any one denieth, that, in the sacrament of the most holy Eucharist, are contained truly, really, and substantially (vere, realiter ac substantialiter), the body and blood together with the soul and divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, and consequently the whole Christ; but saith that He is only therein as in a sign (signum), or in figure (figura), or virtue (virtus); let him be anathema."

## Latin Original
> "Si quis negaverit, in sanctissimae Eucharistiae sacramento contineri vere, realiter et substantialiter corpus et sanguinem una cum anima et divinitate Domini nostri Iesu Christi ac proinde totum Christum, sed dixerit tantummodo esse in eo ut in signo vel figura aut virtute: anathema sit."

## Tags
`Sacramental Theology`, `Eucharist`, `de_fide`, `necessary`, `forbidden`

## Claims
1. In the sacrament of the Eucharist, the body and blood of Christ are truly (vere), really (realiter), and substantially (substantialiter) present.
2. The soul and divinity of Christ are also present — that is, the whole Christ (totus Christus) is present.
3. After consecration, the substance of bread is converted into the substance of Christ's body, and the substance of wine into the substance of Christ's blood (Transubstantiation).
4. This presence is not merely symbolic (ut in signo), figurative (figura), or by virtue (virtus).

## Negates
1. The proposition that the Eucharist is merely a sign (signum) or figure (figura).
2. The proposition that Christ is present in the Eucharist only by virtue (virtus) (Calvinism).
3. The proposition that the Eucharist is merely a memorial (Zwinglianism).
4. The proposition that the substances of bread and wine remain after consecration (Consubstantiation).
