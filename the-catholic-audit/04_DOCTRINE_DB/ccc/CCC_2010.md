## Original Text (Summary)
> Since the initiative in the order of grace belongeth to God, no one can merit the initial grace of forgiveness and justification at the beginning of conversion. Yet, moved by the Holy Ghost and in a state of sanctifying grace, we can then merit for ourselves and for others the graces needed for our sanctification, for the increase of grace, and for the attainment of eternal life (meritum de condigno).

## Claims
1. No merit can be claimed for the initial grace (the beginning of conversion and justification).
2. Good works performed in a state of sanctifying grace become merit according to justice (meritum de condigno).
3. Through merit, the increase of grace and sanctification is possible.
4. Through merit, one may attain eternal life.
5. The fount of all merit is the prompting of the Holy Ghost and the grace of God.
6. Merit is possible only in a state of grace.

## Negates
1. Man can claim no merit whatsoever before God (absolute interpretation of Sola Gratia).
2. Eternal life is given by faith alone (Sola Fide).
3. Good works contribute nothing to salvation.
4. The acts of man hold no worth before God.
