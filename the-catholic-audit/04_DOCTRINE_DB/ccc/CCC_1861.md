## Original Text (Summary)
> Mortal sin (peccatum mortale) destroyeth sanctifying grace (gratia sanctificans) and depriveth one of charity (caritas); and if it be not repented of, it bringeth eternal death in hell. Mortal sin severeth the relationship with God and separateth man from the state of grace. For a sin to be mortal, three conditions must together be met: grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent.

## Tags

## Claims
1. Mortal sin destroyeth sanctifying grace.
2. Mortal sin depriveth one of charity (caritas).
3. Unrepented mortal sin bringeth eternal punishment in hell.
4. Mortal sin severeth the relationship with God.
5. The conditions of mortal sin: grave matter, full knowledge, deliberate consent.
6. Man may lose the state of grace through mortal sin (possibility of losing salvation).

## Negates
1. Grace once received can never be lost (once saved, always saved).
2. The relationship with God is not severed by reason of sin.
3. All sins carry equal weight (denial of the distinction betwixt mortal and venial sin).
4. Sanctifying grace cannot be destroyed by any act of man.
