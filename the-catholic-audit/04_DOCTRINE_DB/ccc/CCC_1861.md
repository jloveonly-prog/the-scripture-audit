## Original Text (Summary)
> Mortal sin (peccatum mortale) destroys sanctifying grace (gratia sanctificans), robs the sinner of charity (caritas), and, if unrepented, brings eternal punishment in hell. Mortal sin severs the relationship with God and separates the person from the state of grace. For a sin to be mortal, grave matter, full knowledge, and complete consent are required.

## Tags
`soteriology`, `grace`, `justification`, `de_fide`, `absolute`

## Claims
1. Mortal sin destroys sanctifying grace
2. Mortal sin robs the sinner of charity (caritas)
3. Unrepented mortal sin brings eternal punishment in hell
4. Mortal sin severs the relationship with God
5. Conditions for mortal sin: grave matter, full knowledge, complete consent
6. A person can lose the state of grace through mortal sin (the possibility of losing salvation)

## Negates
1. Grace once received can never be lost (once saved, always saved)
2. The relationship with God is never severed by sin
3. All sins carry the same weight (denying the mortal/venial distinction)
4. Sanctifying grace cannot be destroyed by any human act
