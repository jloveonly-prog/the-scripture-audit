# CCC-487_511 Mary: Immaculate Conception, Perpetual Virginity, Assumption

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-487_511 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 487-511 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | De Fide Definita |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> To become the mother of the Savior, Mary was preserved free from all stain of original sin from the first instant of her conception (Immaculate Conception), and remained a virgin before, during, and after the birth of Jesus (Perpetual Virginity). At the end of her earthly life, she was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory (Assumption).

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Mary was preserved from original sin by a special grace of God from the moment of her conception.
2. Mary remained a perpetual virgin before, during, and after giving birth to Jesus.
3. Mary was taken up body and soul into heaven at the end of her earthly life.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Mary was born with original sin like all other humans.
2. Mary had other children with Joseph after giving birth to Jesus.

# CCC-880_896 Papal Primacy

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-880_896 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 880-896 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | De Fide Definita |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The Roman Pontiff, as successor of Peter, is the head of the college of bishops and possesses full, supreme, and universal power over the whole Church (Primacy). The college of bishops has authority over the universal Church only in communion with the Pope.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. The Roman Pontiff, as the lawful successor of Peter, has full, immediate, and universal supreme jurisdiction (primacy) over the entire Catholic Church.
2. Bishops possess authority only in communion with the Pope, and the college of bishops cannot exercise supreme power independently without the Pope.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. The Pope is merely the 'first among equals' (Primus inter pares) among bishops and does not possess supreme jurisdiction over the whole Church.
2. Local bishops or councils have supremacy over the authority of the Pope (Conciliarism).

# CCC-888_892 Magisterium and Papal Infallibility

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-888_892 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 888-892 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | De Fide Definita |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> When the Pope proclaims by a definitive act a doctrine pertaining to faith or morals 'Ex Cathedra', his teaching is infallible by the assistance of the Holy Ghost. The college of bishops also enjoys infallibility when gathered in an ecumenical council with the Pope.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. When the Pope teaches definitively on faith and morals 'Ex Cathedra', his declaration is infallible.
2. Doctrinal definitions of a universal council (Ecumenical Council) held in communion with the Pope are infallible.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Even doctrines declared 'Ex Cathedra' by the Pope can contain errors if they contradict the Scripture.
2. There is no authoritative system within the Church capable of making absolutely infallible judgments on faith and morals.

# CCC-954_962 Intercession of Saints

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-954_962 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 954-962 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The saints in heaven constantly intercede for the earthly Church before God, and their merits and prayers greatly help the weakness of believers on earth. Therefore, it is legitimate and beneficial for believers to invoke the saints in prayer.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Saints in heaven constantly offer prayers of intercession to God for believers on earth.
2. It is legitimate and spiritually beneficial for living believers to ask the saints in heaven to pray for them.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Asking dead saints for prayer is idolatry or necromancy, and is forbidden.
2. Seeking the intercession of saints undermines the mediatorship of Jesus Christ, the one and only mediator between God and men.
