# CCC-2068 — Salvation Through Keeping the Commandments

## Original Text (Summary)
> The Council of Trent teaches that the Ten Commandments are obligatory even for justified persons. The Second Vatican Council also affirms this: "Bishops... must teach the faithful the things which lead to knowledge of the things of God, [and] the things which lead to the doing of good and the avoiding of evil, for the glory of God and eternal happiness."

## Tags
`soteriology`, `commandments`, `justification`, `de_fide`, `necessary`, `absolute`

## Claims
1. The Ten Commandments are obligatory even for the justified
2. Keeping the commandments is necessary for salvation
3. Even after justification, one must keep God's commandments
4. Violating the commandments endangers salvation
5. Both the things to be done and the things to be avoided pertain to salvation
6. Committing mortal sin by breaking the Ten Commandments and failing to repent results in the loss of eternal salvation

## Negates
1. A justified person is exempt from the obligation to keep the commandments
2. Salvation is by faith alone and works are irrelevant (the absolute reading of Sola Fide)
3. One in a state of grace need not keep the commandments
4. The Ten Commandments are Old Testament law and thus do not apply in the New Testament era
5. A believer once saved does not lose salvation even by sinning
