# CCC-2068 — Salvation by Keeping the Commandments

## Original Text (Summary)
> The Council of Trent teaches that the Ten Commandments are obligatory on Christians and that the justified man is still bound to keep them. The Second Vatican Council also confirmeth: "The bishops... must preach to the faithful all that they must believe and practice for their salvation... what they ought to do and what they ought to avoid for the glory of God and for the eternal beatitude of man."

## Tags
`Soteriology`, `Commandments`, `Justification`, `de_fide`, `Mandatory`, `Absolute`

## Claims
1. The Ten Commandments are obligatory even upon justified persons.
2. The keeping of the commandments is necessary for salvation.
3. Even after justification, one must keep the commandments of God.
4. The breaking of the commandments jeopardiseth salvation.
5. Both what ought to be done and what ought to be avoided relate to salvation.
6. If one committeth mortal sin by breaking the Ten Commandments and repenteth not, he loseth eternal salvation.

## Negates
1. The justified man is exempt from the obligation of keeping the commandments.
2. One is saved by faith alone and works are irrelevant (absolute interpretation of Sola Fide).
3. If one be in a state of grace, he need not keep the commandments.
4. The Ten Commandments are the law of the Old Testament and therefore apply not in the New Testament era.
5. A believer once saved loseth not his salvation even if he sinneth.
