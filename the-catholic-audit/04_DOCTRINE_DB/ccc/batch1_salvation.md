# CCC-161_165 Faith and Salvation

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-161_165 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 161-165 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Believing in Jesus Christ and God is necessary for obtaining salvation. Without this faith, no one can be justified, and unless one perseveres in it to the end, they cannot attain eternal life.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Faith in Jesus Christ and God the Father is necessary for salvation.
2. Those who lose their faith or do not persevere in it to the end cannot be saved.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. One can obtain salvation without faith in Jesus Christ.
2. Once obtained, faith and salvation cannot be lost (Once saved, always saved).

# CCC-402_412 Original Sin and Its Consequences

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-402_412 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 402-412 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Through Adam's first sin, original sin is transmitted to all humanity by propagation, not by imitation. Human nature is fallen and subject to death, and original sin is washed away only through the merit of Christ's baptism.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Original sin is transmitted to all humanity through biological propagation due to Adam's first sin.
2. Original sin corrupted human nature and brought about death.
3. Original sin can only be washed away through the sacrament of Baptism.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Original sin is merely following Adam's bad example (Pelagianism).
2. Humans are born with a good nature unaffected by original sin.

| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The Word became flesh (Incarnation) to save us by reconciling us with God. Jesus Christ is true God and true man, the one mediator of salvation.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Christ's incarnation is an essential event for the salvation of mankind and the remission of sins.

# CCC-599_618 The Cross and Atonement

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|

| **Article** | 599-618 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Jesus Christ's death on the cross was part of God's eternal plan, a unique and definitive sacrifice of atonement for the sins of all mankind. Believers must unite themselves to His cross through suffering.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Christ's sacrifice on the cross is a once-for-all, perfect sacrifice of atonement that remits the sins of all mankind.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Christ's death on the cross is not an objective sacrifice of atonement that remits sins.
2. The cross of Christ is insufficient and requires additional sacrifices.

# CCC-846_848 Salvation Outside the Church (Extra Ecclesiam nulla salus)

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-846_848 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 846-848 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Salvation comes from Christ the Head through the Church which is His Body. Those who, knowing that the Catholic Church was founded as necessary, would refuse either to enter it or to remain in it, cannot be saved. But those who, through no fault of their own, do not know the Gospel and the Church, can attain salvation.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. A person who knows the Catholic Church is the necessary means of salvation but refuses or leaves it cannot be saved.
2. Those who do not know the Gospel or the Catholic Church through no fault of their own can be saved if they seek God and follow their conscience.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. One who clearly knows the necessity of the Catholic Church but intentionally rejects it can be saved.
2. Those who do not belong to the Catholic Church go unconditionally to hell (Rigorism).

# CCC-1213_1284 The Grace of the Sacrament of Baptism

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-1213_1284 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 1213-1284 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Baptism washes away all sins (original and personal), makes one a new creation, and incorporates one into the Church. Baptism leaves an indelible spiritual mark (character) and cannot be repeated.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Water baptism remits original sin and all personal sins committed prior to it.
2. Baptism imprints an indelible spiritual mark on the soul and makes one a member of the Church community.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Baptism is a mere symbol of confession of faith without the actual power to remit sins.
2. Original sin remains entirely even after baptism (only formally covered).

# CCC-1257_1261 The Necessity of Baptism

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-1257_1261 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 1257-1261 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Baptism is necessary for salvation for those to whom the Gospel has been proclaimed. However, Baptism of blood (martyrdom) and Baptism of desire bring about the fruits of Baptism without being a sacrament. Infants who die without Baptism are entrusted to God's mercy.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Water baptism is necessary for salvation for those who can hear and understand the Gospel.
2. Explicit or implicit desire for baptism, and martyrdom (baptism of blood), confer the grace of baptism without water baptism.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Without water baptism, one can never be saved under any exceptional circumstances.
2. A person who can know the Gospel but intentionally refuses the sacrament of baptism can be saved.

# CCC-1987_2016 Justification, Grace, and Merit

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | CCC-1987_2016 |
| **Source** | Catechism of the Catholic Church |
| **Article** | 1987-2016 |
| **Dogmatic Tier** | Sententia Certa |
| **Infallibility** | 🟡 Authoritative |
| **Anathema** | No |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> Justification is not only the remission of sins, but also the sanctification and renewal of the interior man. It is accomplished through the cooperation of the Holy Ghost's grace and human free will, and a justified person can truly merit eternal life through good works.

## Claims — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Justification includes both the remission of sins and interior sanctification (renewal of nature).
2. Human free will must cooperate when the grace of salvation operates.
3. A justified believer can merit eternal life through good works.

## Negates — ⚠️ Most Important
1. Justification is achieved by faith alone and is merely God covering sins without interior renewal (Denial of Justification by faith alone).
2. Human free will is completely passive before grace.
3. A Christian's good works cannot merit eternal life.
