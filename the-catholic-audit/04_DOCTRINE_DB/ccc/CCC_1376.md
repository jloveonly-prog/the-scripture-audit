# CCC-1376 — Transubstantiation

| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The Council of Trent summarizeth it thus: By the consecration of the bread and wine there takes place a change of the whole substance of the bread into the substance of the body of Christ our Lord and of the whole substance of the wine into the substance of his blood. This change the holy Catholic Church has fittingly and properly called transubstantiation. The outward species of the bread and wine remain, but the substance is wholly changed.

## Tags
`Sacramentology`, `Eucharist`, `de_fide`, `Absolute`

## Claims
1. By the consecration, the whole substance of the bread is changed into the substance of the body of Christ.
2. By the consecration, the whole substance of the wine is changed into the substance of his blood.
3. This change is called transubstantiation.
4. Only the outward species (accidents) of the bread and wine remain.
5. After the consecration, the substance of the bread and wine ceaseth to exist.

## Negates
1. The substance of the bread and wine remaineth even after the consecration (Consubstantiation).
2. The Eucharist is merely a symbol of Christ's body (Symbolism).
3. The presence of Christ is merely spiritual or figurative.
4. The substance of the bread and wine co-existeth with the substance of Christ.
