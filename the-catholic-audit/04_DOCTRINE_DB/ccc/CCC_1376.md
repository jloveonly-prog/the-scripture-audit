# CCC-1376 — Transubstantiation (Transsubstantiatio)

| Field | Content |
|:---|:---|

| **Anathema** | Yes (Council of Trent, decree on the Eucharist, Canon 2) |
| **Year** | 1992 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> The Council of Trent summarizes: "Because Christ our Redeemer said that it was truly his body that he was offering under the species of bread, it has always been the conviction of the Church of God... that by the consecration of the bread and wine there takes place a change of the whole substance of the bread into the substance of the body of Christ our Lord and of the whole substance of the wine into the substance of his blood. This change the holy Catholic Church has fittingly and properly called transubstantiation." The outward appearances (species) of bread and wine remain, but the substance is wholly changed.

## Tags
`sacramental_theology`, `eucharist`, `de_fide`, `absolute`

## Claims
1. By consecration, the whole substance of the bread is changed into the substance of Christ's body
2. By consecration, the whole substance of the wine is changed into the substance of Christ's blood
3. This change is called "transubstantiation" (transsubstantiatio)
4. Only the outward appearances (species/accidents) of bread and wine remain
5. After consecration, the substance of the bread and wine no longer exists

## Negates
1. The substance of the bread and wine remains as it was even after consecration (Consubstantiation)
2. The Eucharist is merely symbolic of Christ's body (Symbolism)
3. Christ's presence is merely spiritual/figurative
4. The substance of the bread and wine and the substance of Christ coexist
