# VAT1-PASTOR-AETERNUS — Declaration of Papal Infallibility (Ex Cathedra)

| Item | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **ID** | VAT1-PASTOR-AETERNUS |
| **Source** | First Vatican Council, Dogmatic Constitution Pastor Aeternus |
| **Article** | Chapter 4 (De Romani Pontificis infallibili magisterio) |
| **Dogmatic Rank** | De Fide |
| **Anathema** | ✅ Yes |
| **Year** | 1870 |

## Original Text (Summary)
> "When the Roman Pontiff speaketh ex cathedra, that is, when, in the exercise of his office as pastor and teacher of all Christians, in virtue of his supreme apostolic authority, he defineth a doctrine concerning faith or morals to be held by the whole Church, he is possessed of that infallibility (infallibilitas) with which the divine Redeemer willed that His Church should be endowed in defining doctrine regarding faith or morals. And therefore such definitions of the Roman Pontiff are of themselves (ex sese), and not by the consent of the Church, irreformable (irreformabiles). But if any man presume to contradict this our definition; let him be anathema."

## Latin Original
> "Romanum Pontificem, cum ex cathedra loquitur... ea infallibilitate pollere, qua divinus Redemptor Ecclesiam suam... instructam esse voluit; ideoque eiusmodi Romani Pontificis definitiones ex sese, non autem ex consensu Ecclesiae, irreformabiles esse. Si quis autem huic Nostrae definitioni contradicere, quod Deus avertat, praesumpserit: anathema sit."

## Claims
1. The Roman Pontiff is infallible when he defineth a doctrine concerning faith or morals ex cathedra.
2. This infallibility is by the divine assistance (assistentia divina) promised to him in blessed Peter.
3. The dogmatic definitions of the Roman Pontiff are irreformable of themselves (ex sese) and not by the consent of the Church (consensus Ecclesiae).
4. The primacy (primatus) of the Roman Pontiff doth comprehend the supreme jurisdiction (iurisdictio) over the whole Church.
5. This dogma is De Fide; whosoever rejecteth it is anathematized.

## Negates
1. The proposition that the Roman Pontiff may err in defining doctrine.
2. The proposition that the definitions of the Roman Pontiff require the consent of a council (Conciliarism).
3. The proposition that the definitions of the Roman Pontiff are irreformable only through the consent (consensus) of the Church.
4. The proposition that the Roman Pontiff hath not the supreme jurisdiction over the whole Church.
