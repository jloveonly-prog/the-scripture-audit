## Original Text (Summary)
> **Paragraph 2**: "This Vatican Council declareth that the human person hath a right to religious freedom. This freedom meaneth that all men are to be immune from coercion on the part of individuals or of social groups and of any human power, in such wise that no one is to be forced to act in a manner contrary to his own beliefs, whether privately or publicly, whether alone or in association with others, within due limits."

## Claims
1. Every man hath a right to religious liberty.
2. This right is founded upon the very dignity of the human person (natural law).
3. No man shall be compelled to act contrary to his conscience in matters of religion.
4. No man shall be hindered from exercising his religion according to his conscience.
5. This right to liberty is recognized within the bounds of the public order of society.
6. The true religion doth subsist in the Catholic Church, but it cannot be compelled upon others.

## Negates
1. The proposition that the state must protect only the true religion (Catholicism) and suppress false religions.
2. The proposition that heretics have no right to religious liberty.
3. The proposition that religious coercion by the state is lawful.
4. The unconditional application of the principle that "Error hath no rights."
