## ① PART A §3 "Claim #N" — Format Currently in Use (More concise and practical than the original SVAP_GHQ.md draft)

> **Observation**: The blank template in `SVAP_GHQ.md` requires 5 items per Claim ([Context]/[Preacher's Claim]/[Problem Analysis]/[Spiritual Repercussion]/[Biblical Refutation]), but well-written reports in practice have converged into four blocks: **One line of `C-Code + Application TYPE` → `KJV:` original text citation → `Analysis:` → `Judgment:`**. The fewer the items, the higher the density. `SVAP_TEMPLATE.md` adopts this practical model as the default.

> Source: `AUDIT_[Anonymous]_Godhead_20260812.md` Claim #11, #16 (Part 1)

### Claim #11 — Heb 7:1-3 Melchisedec=Jesus, "Without father, without mother"=A fact regarding the soul (father) not the body
> **C-Code**: C-03 | **Application TYPE**: TYPE-G (Grammar), TYPE-AG (Argument from silence, exact application), Hillel 6 (Kayotze Bo)
> **KJV**: Heb 7:3 "Without father, without mother, without descent...but **made like unto** the Son of God; abideth a priest continually." / Heb 7:6 "But he whose descent **is not counted** from them."
> **Analysis**: The decisive grammatical marker is "made like unto" (ἀφωμοιωμένος, perfect passive participle — "having been made like"). The author of Hebrews does not state that Melchisedec "is" the Son of God, but explicitly expresses that he was "made like unto" Him — this is a grammar indicating a **Type** relationship, not a statement of ontological identity (Hillel 6 Principle, analogy from similar passages). "Without father, without mother" parallels 7:6 "whose descent **is not counted** from them", which is a rabbinic argument based on the **literary silence** of Genesis 14 not recording Melchisedec's genealogy (unlike all other figures of the time) (a legitimate application of TYPE-AG); it is not an ontological statement that he literally lacked parents.
> **Judgment**: ❌ **UNBIBLICAL — STRONG** (Convergence of 2 types: TYPE-G "made like unto" + TYPE-AG interpretation of literary silence, cross-checked with Heb 7:6).

### Claim #16 — Hos 12:5 "The LORD God of hosts" = Evidence of "more than one part" → Body, soul, and spirit are one
> **C-Code**: C-04 | **Application TYPE**: TYPE-AL (Semantic ambiguity), TYPE-N (Exhaustive search)
> **KJV**: "Even the **LORD God of hosts**; the LORD is his memorial."
> **Analysis**: "Of hosts" is a fixed divine title appearing over 280 times throughout the KJV (e.g., 1 Sam 1:11, Ps 24:10, Isa 1:9), which is standard usage referring to the heavenly host commanded by God; there is not a single instance in the entire KJV where it refers to "God's own internal constituent parts" (Result of TYPE-N exhaustive search).
> **Judgment**: ❌ **UNBIBLICAL — IRONCLAD** (TYPE-AL semantic ambiguity + TYPE-N exhaustive search converging on all 280+ instances meaning "heavenly host", with no alternative reading).

## ② PART B §6 "Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary" — Actual Density Example

> Source: Same document §10.

### Chapter 3: There is no saviour beside me — Corresponds to Claim #9~10
**Argument**: Based on Jesus using the same phrase for Himself in John 8:24 and 8:58 as in Isaiah 43:10 "I am he", they argue for the Jesus=Jehovah identity, and further claim this as "the use of the Father's title".
**Why it sounds plausible**: This chapter is the most solid section of the entire book. Jesus' repeated use of Isaiah's absolute formula of self-declaration, and the audience's violent reaction to it as blasphemy (John 8:59, taking up stones) are two independently converging lines of evidence.
**Why it actually falls apart**: Up to this point, it does not fall apart. The conclusion "Jesus = the Jehovah of Isaiah" is firmly established. However, what this chapter misses is that this establishment does not automatically answer the separate question: "Is that Jehovah exactly the same person as the one called 'Father' elsewhere?"
**Simple Analogy**: If someone says, "I am the representative of this company," it is established that he is the representative, but whether "he is the same person as the chairman of the board" must be verified separately.
**Judgment**: ✅ BIBLICAL — STRONG(#9) / ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED(#10) → See PART A #9, #10.

### Chapter 8: The Book of John — Corresponds to Claim #24~31 (The "Finishing Blow" chapter of the entire book — includes an example of a self-contradiction trap)
**Argument**: The strongest arguments positioned in this entire book are concentrated in this chapter, such as John 14:9 ("he that hath seen me hath seen the Father") and John 10:30 ("I and my Father are one").
**Why it sounds plausible**: John 14:9 is indeed a very powerful sentence, and John 10:30 also appears almost flawless on the surface.
**Why it actually falls apart**: In this chapter lies the two most decisive refutations in the entire book.
① **The "one" (ἕν) in John 10:30 is grammatically neuter in Greek** — it is standard grammar denoting a unity of nature or will, not the masculine (εἷς) denoting "one individual entity". In the same Gospel, John 17:11 and 21-22, Jesus prays for the **believers using the exact same word**: "that they may be one." There is no disagreement from any camp that the believers being "one" is not a fusion of persons — reading John 10:30, written by the same author using the same word, as the same kind of union is the most consistent internally.
② John 14:10 "the Father that dwelleth in me" (indwelling construction) is a grammar that presupposes two distinct subjects, yet the author **interprets this identical "indwelling" construction exactly as "not a fusion"** elsewhere in this very book (2 Cor 13:5, Rom 8:9-10 "Christ in you") — applying different rules of interpretation to the same grammatical structure is a self-contradiction.
**Simple Analogy**: When a married couple says, "We are of one heart and one mind," it expresses their deep union, but it does not mean the husband and wife legally become one person.
**Judgment**: Out of 8 items in this chapter, 6 ⚠️UNSUPPORTED, 2 ❌UNBIBLICAL — STRONG → See PART A #24~31. **Self-contradiction Trap 1**: The criterion for interpreting the "indwelling" construction is applied differently between the believer-Christ relationship and the Father-Son relationship.

## Summary — 4 Practical Principles Shown by This Example

1. **4 blocks are sufficient for Claim items**: One line of C-Code/TYPE → KJV original text → Analysis (2-5 sentences) → Judgment. The blank template's forced 5 items have been condensed in practice.
2. **Truly acknowledge "why it sounds plausible"** — As in "This chapter is the most solid section of the entire book," state where the opponent's argument is strong. That gives power to "why it actually falls apart."
3. **Self-contradiction traps are found by contrasting how the same grammatical structure is interpreted elsewhere** — It is the strongest because it holds true regardless of theological labels.
4. **Corroborating arguments in PART C only count if they come from different books/authors** — Looking at the same verse from multiple angles counts as 1.
