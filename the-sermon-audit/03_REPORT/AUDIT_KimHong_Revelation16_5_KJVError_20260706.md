### C-05: Beza's Conjectural Emendation (the Core Claim)
**Claim**: without any manuscript grounding, Beza conjecturally changed Revelation 16:5's "ho hosios" (the Holy One) to "ho esomenos" (which shall be).

**KJV Original-Text Comparison**:
> **Revelation 16:5 (KJV)**: *"And I heard the angel of the waters say, Thou art righteous, O Lord, which art, and wast, and shalt be, because thou hast judged thus."*

**Textual-Criticism Fact-Check**:
- **Erasmus's TR** (1516-1535, 5 editions): ὁ ὅσιος (ho hosios) = "the Holy One"
- **Stephanus's TR** (1546-1551, 4 editions): ὁ ὅσιος (ho hosios) = "the Holy One"
- **Beza's TR** (1565-1604, especially the 1598 edition): ὁ ἐσόμενος (ho esomenos) = "the One who shall be"
- **Extant Greek manuscripts**: every known Greek manuscript supports ὁ ὅσιος or a variant thereof; 0 manuscripts support ὁ ἐσόμενος.
- **Pre-KJV English translations**: Wycliffe, Tyndale, Coverdale, Matthew's Bible, the Great Bible, the Geneva Bible, the Bishops' Bible — all translated it along the lines of "Holy One"/"Holy."

**Verdict**: ✅ **BIBLICAL (Fact Confirmed)** — that Beza's revision is a conjectural emendation, and that no Greek manuscript supporting it survives, are established facts of textual scholarship. This specific claim by the preacher is accurate.

### C-06: The Triple-Pattern Argument and Beza's Motive
**Claim**: the KJV's "shalt be" appears, on the surface, to match the triple pattern of other verses in Revelation (1:4, 1:8, 4:8), but this is Beza forcing a contextual fit.

**KJV Original-Text Comparison — the Triple-Title Pattern + Speaker Comparison**:

| Verse | KJV Original | Pattern | 🗣️ Speaker |
|------|----------|------|----------|
| Rev 1:4 | *"him which is, and which was, and which is to come"* | present + past + future | **John** (narrating the letter's opening) |
| Rev 1:8 | *"which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty"* | present + past + future | **God Himself** (a direct declaration) |
| Rev 4:8 | *"Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come"* | past + present + future | **the four beasts** (worship before the throne) |
| Rev 11:17 | *"which art, and wast, and art to come"* | present + past + future | **the twenty-four elders** (worship/thanksgiving) |
| **Rev 16:5** | *"which art, and wast, and **shalt be**"* | present + past + **future** | **the angel of the waters** 🔴 (proclaiming judgment) |

> ⚠️ **Speaker Analysis — a Core Fact the Preacher Does Not Address**:
> among the 5 verses where the triple title appears, **16:5 is the only one where an angel is the speaker.** The others are John (narration), God Himself (direct declaration), the four beasts (worship), and the twenty-four elders (thanksgiving).
> this fact can be interpreted in either direction:
> - **an Argument Favoring the Critical Text**: unlike the other speakers, an angel might have emphasized the attribute "the Holy One" — since God's **holiness** becomes the ground of judgment in a judgment context.
> - **an Argument Favoring the KJV**: since God's eternality is a universal attribute unchanged regardless of who the speaker is, an angel too would naturally have used the identical triple title.

**A Deeper Analysis — the Critical Text's Own Precedent of Deleting the Triple Pattern (Revelation 11:17)**:

in the case of Revelation 11:17, the modern critical text (NA28/UBS5) **omits** "and art to come" (καὶ ὁ ἐρχόμενος) (on the grounds of early manuscripts such as P47, the original hand of the Sinaitic manuscript, etc.). The scholars' logic is: "since God's reign has already begun, the future title 'the One who is to come' no longer applies."

this means that **the critical text itself carries a precedent of deleting the future element of the triple pattern.** Therefore, the hypothesis that **"other manuscripts may likewise have deleted the future element at 16:5"** is, at least as something that actually happened at 11:17, a hypothesis that cannot be entirely excluded.

**Verdict**: ⚠️ **UNSUPPORTED (Partially)** — the preacher's explanation, that Beza's motive was fitting the triple pattern, aligns with the facts, but the preacher, dismissing this issue simply as "forced," does not address several important facts: (1) the KJV-defense side's counter-argument regarding the consistency of the triple title, (2) the precedent at 11:17 where the critical text deletes the same type of future element, (3) the speaker analysis that 16:5 is uniquely the only verse where an angel is the speaker, and (4) the counter-hypothesis that "other manuscripts may have removed the future element." A tendency toward selective evidence (cherry-picking) is detected.

### C-12: the New Testament Application of Tithing (an Incidental Claim)
**Claim**: tithing existed before the law (Abraham, Jacob), and since Jesus said not to leave it undone, it applies to New Testament believers as well.

**KJV Scriptural Comparison**:
> **Genesis 14:20 (KJV)**: *"And blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all."*

> **Genesis 28:22 (KJV)**: *"And this stone, which I have set for a pillar, shall be God's house: and of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee."*

> **Matthew 23:23 (KJV)**: *"Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone."*

> **Malachi 3:10 (KJV)**: *"Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it."*

**In-Depth Analysis**:
1. **Abraham's Tithe** (Gen 14:20): Abraham once gave a tenth of the spoils. Scripture contains no evidence that this was a recurring obligation. It was a voluntary, one-time act.
2. **Jacob's Vow** (Gen 28:22): Jacob's tithe was a conditional vow ("if God will be with me... then... I will surely give the tenth unto thee," Gen 28:20-22), and Scripture does not record whether this was actually fulfilled.
3. **Jesus's Words** (Matt 23:23): Jesus spoke this to Pharisees living under the law, as a legal obligation. This point in time is before the cross, a situation in which the Old Testament system (the era of the law) was still in effect.
4. **Malachi 3:10**: a legal command given to the people of Israel. In the New Testament, Paul teaches concerning giving that "every man according as he purposeth in his heart" (2 Cor 9:7).

> **2 Corinthians 9:7 (KJV)**: *"Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver."*

**Verdict**: ⚠️ **UNSUPPORTED** — it is true that tithing existed before the law, but applying an obligatory tithe to New Testament believers on this basis is not fully supported biblically. Paul presents "as he purposeth in his heart" (2 Cor 9:7) as the principle of New Testament giving, setting no obligatory percentage. This is an incidental claim unrelated to the sermon's main point, but is recorded here for doctrinal accuracy.
