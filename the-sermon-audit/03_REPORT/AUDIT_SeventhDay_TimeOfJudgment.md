This sermon interprets the "2,300 days" prophecy of Daniel 8:14, converting a day into a year to define the period as running from 457 BC to AD 1844. ## 3. Detailed Verification Results by Claim

### Claim #1: the 2,300 days are a 2,300-year period running from 457 BC to 1844.
> **C-Code**: C-10 (a typological-fulfillment debate) | **TYPE Applied**: TYPE-A (vocabulary/grammar), TYPE-E (arithmetic error) | **Verdict**: ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED
> **Preacher's Claim (Paraphrased)**: the preacher calculates a biblical day as a year, claiming the 2,300 days are 2,300 years, running from 457 BC (the starting point of the 70 weeks of Daniel 9) to 1844.
> **Verse Cited (KJV Original)**: "And he said unto me, Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." (Dan 8:14)
> **Grounds for Verification**:
> 1. the original text of Daniel 8:14 reads "evening morning," and the text contains no explicit instruction to convert this period into years.
> 2. while a Day-Year Principle does appear elsewhere in Scripture (Num 14:34, Ezek 4:6), mechanically transposing this onto Daniel 8 is an external inference, not a declaration of the text of Daniel 8 itself.
> 3. binding the 2,300 days of Daniel 8 to the 70 weeks of Daniel 9 under an identical starting point (457 BC) is likewise a theological hypothesis with no direct support from the text, so a biblical confirmation (BIBLICAL) cannot be rendered.

### Claim #2: in 1844 the heavenly sanctuary was cleansed, the beginning of a judgment trying believers.
> **C-Code**: C-03 (a theological collision) | **TYPE Applied**: TYPE-C (cross-verification), TYPE-F (a redemptive-historical contradiction) | **Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL
> **Preacher's Claim (Paraphrased)**: the preacher claims that from 1844, Jesus began, in the heavenly sanctuary, a judgment (the sanctuary cleansing) trying people's faith.
> **Verse Cited (KJV Original)**: "Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us." (Heb 9:12)
> **Grounds for Verification**:
> 1. Hebrews 9:12 declares, in the perfect tense, that Christ entered the sanctuary "once" and had already "obtained eternal redemption."
> 2. Hebrews 10:12 records, "But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God," specifying that His atoning work is entirely concluded.
> 3. Therefore, the claim that the heavenly sanctuary is cleansed only once a new phase of redemptive work (the investigative judgment) begins at a specific future point, 1844, directly collides with the once-for-all, eternal atoning work completed at the cross (Heb 10:14).

### Claim #3: even the believer, though escaping condemnation, must first undergo a trial (investigation) of his life.
> **C-Code**: C-04 (a logical self-contradiction) | **TYPE Applied**: TYPE-A (vocabulary/grammar), TYPE-G (a soteriological contradiction) | **Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL
> **Preacher's Claim (Paraphrased)**: the preacher claims that the verse "he that believeth... shall not come into judgment" means only that he escapes punishment (condemnation), and that believers too must pass through God's trial (investigation) per 1 Peter 4:17.
> **Verse Cited (KJV Original)**: "Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life." (John 5:24)
> **Grounds for Verification**:
> 1. the preacher arbitrarily splits "judgment (krisis)" into a trial process and a guilty verdict, applying them separately. But John 5:24 declares that the believer "hath everlasting life" and "is passed" from death unto life — a present possession and a completed past state.
> 2. Romans 8:1 declares, "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus."
> 3. if believers' salvation depended on the result of an individual investigative trial of their works, this is a regression to salvation-by-works, and it nullifies Christ's explicit declaration that eternal life is obtained the instant one believes — an unbiblical claim.

### Claim #4: believers living in the time of judgment must afflict themselves, as in the Old Testament Day of Atonement, or be cut off from salvation.
> **C-Code**: C-09 (confusing law and grace) | **TYPE Applied**: TYPE-D (a covenantal/dispensational contradiction) | **Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL
> **Preacher's Claim (Paraphrased)**: the preacher claims that believers living in the judgment era after 1844 must repent of their sins and afflict themselves, following the Day of Atonement ordinance of Leviticus 16, or else be cut off from among God's people.
> **Verse Cited (KJV Original)**: "And this shall be a statute for ever unto you: that in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, ye shall afflict your souls..." (Lev 16:29)
> **Grounds for Verification**:
> 1. Colossians 2:16-17 specifies that Old Testament ordinances such as feast days, new moons, and sabbaths are "a shadow of things to come," while "the body is of Christ."
> 2. applying the Old Testament's Day of Atonement ordinance to New Testament Christians, and tying the maintenance of salvation (being cut off from the people) to the presence or absence of that act (self-affliction), is a legalistic contradiction.
> 3. Hebrews 10:14 ("For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified") guarantees that the believer is made eternally perfect not by his own self-affliction, but by Christ's once-for-all sacrifice.
