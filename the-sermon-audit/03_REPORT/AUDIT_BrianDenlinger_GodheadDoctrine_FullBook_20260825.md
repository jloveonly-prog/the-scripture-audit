## 1. Summary of the Document's Doctrinal Development (AI Paraphrase)

* **Preface · Introduction (pp. 5-9)** — the author presupposes that understanding this book requires three things — ① being born again, ② the King James Bible, ③ prayer — and declares that **"the King James Bible alone is the final authority."** He then summarizes his own position as "Jesus is God," and states that he rejects both the Trinity and Modalism. The skeleton of this book is that God is **one Person** composed of three parts — **body (Jesus), soul (the Father), and spirit (the Holy Spirit).**

* **Part 1 — The Old Testament Argument (pp. 10-37)** — starting from Isa 9:6's "the everlasting Father," he cites, in sequence, Gen 22:8 (God providing Himself as the lamb), Isa 43:10-11 (no Savior besides Me), Heb 7:1-3 (Melchizedek), Gen 32:30 and Hos 12:4 (Jacob wrestling / "us"), the Exod 16:10 family (the glory of the LORD), and Gen 1:26-27 ("our image" being **singular**). The recurring form of argument is **"a singular expression appears, therefore one Person."**

* **Part 2 — The New Testament Argument (pp. 38-68)** — this continues through John 1:1 ("the Word=the body, God=the soul"), Col 1:17 and 1 Tim 6:15-16 (all things consist by Him, the sole Potentate), 2 Cor 13:5, Rom 8:9, Eph 4:6 (the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit all indwelling believers), Col 2:8 (philosophy), James 3:9 and 1 Thess 5:23 (God's likeness = a tripartite structure), Gal 4:14 and Judg 13:21-22 (the angel of the LORD = Jesus), and Rev 22:1-5 (**the throne is singular**).

* **Part 3 — Answering Objections (pp. 69-91)** — this answers, in sequence, seven attacks commonly leveled by the opposing side: the Gethsemane self-dialogue (answered with Eph 5:19), ventriloquism at the baptism (answered with the martyrs' souls of Rev 6:9-11 acting independently), the term `persons` (a full-scan enumeration of some 50 KJV instances), John 14:28 ("the soul is greater than the body"), the right hand of Heb 1:3 (answered with the `until` of Heb 1:13), the handover of the kingdom in 1 Cor 15:24-28 (the body hands over to the soul, and the two then merge), and the three spirits of Rev 16:13 (Satan's trinity).

* **Conclusion (p. 92)** — the Godhead is the Father (soul), Son (body), and Holy Spirit (spirit), and since terms such as `Trinity`, `God the Son`, and `Three Persons` do not appear in the KJV, the reader is exhorted not to compromise. The decision demanded of the reader is: **"do not fellowship with anyone who uses Trinitarian terminology."**

### Claim #61-b — Rev 22:1's Throne Is Singular, Therefore One Person

> **Location** Ch. 14, p. 66 | **C-Code** C-03 | **Verdict** ❌ UNBIBLICAL — **IRONCLAD**

**[The Author's Claim (Paraphrased)]** the author claims that since Rev 22:1 and 3 record "the throne" in the singular, not "thrones," God and the Lamb are one Person, and no separate throne is needed.

**[The Observation Is True]** the throne genuinely is singular (#61-a ✅). But **the Korean sentence the author quotes and the English KJV differ.**

> **[KJV 1769] Rev 22:1** — *"proceeding out of the throne of God **and of** the Lamb."*
> **[KJV 1769] Rev 22:3** — *"the throne of God **and of** the Lamb shall be in it"*

`of God **and of** the Lamb` — **a parallel possessive with the preposition `of` repeated twice.** This is not apposition. The author's quotation reads as though it were apposition, "God, **that is**, the throne of the Lamb," but **the KJV was not written that way.**

**[The Decisive Reason — the Author's Own Principle]** the author declares, on **page 5 of the preface**:

> *"you must look up 'every' verse from the King James Bible yourself — other translations will not be of help in understanding this book."*

**Applying that very standard causes Chapter 14's argument to fail.** This is not the auditor imposing an outside standard — it is **the result of applying the author's own self-set standard to his own text.**

**[Decisive Reinforcement — the KJV Uses a Different Word When It Means "That Is"]** 🔥

The KJV distinguishes **apposition (A=B)** from **a parallel listing (A and B)** with different markers.

| Meaning | KJV Marker | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **A = B (apposition)** | **`even`** | **1 Cor 15:24** — *"delivered up the kingdom to God, **even** the Father"* |
| **A and B (two)** | **`and of` / `and unto`** | **Rev 22:1** — *"the throne of God **and of** the Lamb"* |

**Rev 22:1 has no `even`.** Had the KJV meant to say "God, that is, the Lamb," it would have written `of God, **even** the Lamb` — because the same Bible uses exactly that marker at 1 Cor 15:24. **It did not.**

> 🔑 **What This Comparison Accomplishes**
> ① **It blocks the defense that "the Korean translation reads that way"** — since the distinction genuinely exists **within the English**, the argument cannot be shifted to a dispute over the Korean edition.
> ② **It expands the cross-witness from 1 author (John) to 2** — since the comparison item is **Paul (1 Corinthians)**, this fills the weakness of the cross-witness below being confined entirely to Revelation.

**[Comparison of the Korean Edition — Actual Measurement]** in the same edition (the KSKJB) the author and this audit both use, `and of` is rendered **"that is."** Yet **the same edition renders the rest of Revelation's parallel constructions as "and."**

| Verse | KJV | KSKJB |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Rev 21:22 | God Almighty **and** the Lamb **are** | the Lord God **and** the Lamb |
| Rev 5:13 | unto him… **and unto** the Lamb | to the One seated, **and** to the Lamb |
| Rev 7:10 | to our God… **and unto** the Lamb | to our God, **and** to the Lamb |
| Rev 14:4 | unto God **and to** the Lamb | to God **and** to the Lamb |
| **Rev 22:1, 3** | of God **and of** the Lamb | of God, **that is,** of the Lamb ← **only place that differs** |

→ this internal inconsistency in the edition **is not grounds for this audit's verdict** (the verdict rests solely on the English KJV). It is recorded only as material showing **which sentence the author's argument stands on.**

**[Cross-Witness — the Same Structure 3 More Times]**

> **Rev 5:13** — *"unto him that sitteth upon the throne, **and unto** the Lamb"* (the preposition `unto` repeated)
> **Rev 21:22** — *"the Lord God Almighty **and** the Lamb **are** the temple of it"* — **the plural verb `are`**
> **Rev 21:23** — *"the glory of God did lighten it, **and** the Lamb **[is]** the light thereof"*

Rev 21:22's `are` is especially weighty. If it were one Person, it would have to be `is`.

**[Spiritual Consequence]** the final scene of Revelation is **that the Lamb shares the throne together.** Folding that structure into one erases the climax of the entire book — that the slain Lamb ascends the throne.

**[Reason for IRONCLAD]** ① holds by the standard the author himself declared (the KJV's final authority) ② the same structure repeats in 4 places ③ Rev 21:22's plural verb blocks any alternative reading ④ the counter-hypothesis (a body/soul distinction) cannot explain `are` ⑤ **the `even`-comparison expands the cross-witness from 1 author (John) to 2 authors (John and Paul)** (reinforced 2026-08-25). The first edition's grounds were all drawn from Revelation, leaving a single witness; this comparison fills that gap.

### Claim #14-b — Gen 22:8, "God Provided Himself as the Lamb"

### Chapter 1: The Everlasting Father (pp. 11-12) — Claims #9-13

**The Argument**: Isaiah 9:6 calls one Person both "a Son" and "the everlasting Father." Just as one man can simultaneously be a father and a son, Jesus is Himself God the Father.

**Why It Sounds Plausible**: the verse genuinely reads that way. And everyone knows the analogy, "I am my father's son, and my son's father." **There isn't a single difficult word.**

**Why It Actually Falls Apart**: reading the same verse to the end shows the tenses diverge.

> **Isa 9:6** — "unto us a child **is born**... his name **shall be called**..."

**Being born is a present event; being called is something yet to come.** Being called by a name and that person being that very One are different claims.

And Ephesians 4:6, which the author cites as grounds, must be read together with **the two verses immediately before it.**

> **Eph 4:4-6** — "there is one **Spirit**... **One Lord**, one faith, one baptism, One **God and Father** of all"

**One Spirit, one Lord, and one God are listed side by side, as three.** The author cites only verse 6.

**Simple Analogy**: "Manager Kim will one day be called president" and "Manager Kim is president now" are different statements.

What was actually provided was **a ram**, offered **in place of Isaac.** So reading it as **"for himself"** rather than "Himself" matches that chapter's own record.
