> Since all 5 sermons concern the Trinity debate, these words cannot be avoided — so **their meanings alone are fixed first, before proceeding.**

## 1-1. Person · Hypostasis · Nature · Essence — Distinguishing Four Words

| Word | Western Original | Meaning (Within Christian Tradition) | One-Line Test |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **person** | persona / πρόσωπον | answers "**who**." The **subject** that speaks, wills, loves, sends, and is sent | "Who did it?" |
| **hypostasis** | ὑπόστασις | refers to the same thing as above, but as the technical term used within the doctrine of God. Became functionally synonymous with "person" by the 4th century | "Who?" (in theological context) |
| **nature** | natura / φύσις | answers "**what by**." The **set of properties** that determines what that subject can do and what it undergoes | "What is it?" |
| **essence/substance** | essentia / οὐσία | almost the same layer as nature. "That which makes an existing thing exist" | "What is it?" (ontology) |

**The Key Grammar**: in traditional usage, **person stands in the subject position, and nature stands in the predicate position.** One says "the person has the nature," not "the nature did something." This distinction is not used in the actual verdicts below, but is necessary for the reader to recognize **what actually happens when Sermon 5 says "the two natures carry different meanings from one another."**

## 1-3. The Concept of "the Trinity" — What Does It Actually Claim?

What the traditional formula actually claims is the following three sentences. (This audit **neither presupposes these sentences to be true, nor presupposes them to be false.**)

```
① God is one           (essence/nature is one)
② the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct from one another   (persons/hypostases are three)
③ ① and ② are not spoken of on the same level  (the level of "one" and the level of "three" differ)
```

**What the Godhead doctrine actually denies is ②.** ① is shared by both sides. Therefore, from beginning to end, the sole point at issue in this audit is **"does the KJV text require ②, or exclude it,"** and that judgment is reached solely by the text's **pronouns, verbs, prepositions, case, and articles.**

## 1-4. The Concept of "the Two Natures (Deity and Humanity)"

```
nature ─┬─ divine nature : untemptable · omniscient · omnipresent · immortal
        └─ human nature  : hunger · fatigue · capable of being tempted · death
```

In the traditional formula (Chalcedon, AD 451), the point of this framework is **"two natures in one person,"** where the singularity of "person" means the "who" of 1-1 above is one. **Sermon 5 borrows this exact framework.** That in itself is not a problem — the problem is that, having declared a rejection of post-Nicene theological terminology in Sermon 4, this framework becomes the skeleton just 6 days later (→ §11-2, Trap 4).

### Claim #1-7-b — the Application of 1 Cor 14:33 → ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED

**[KJV 1769]** 1 Cor 14:33 — *"For God is not [the author] of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints."*

Three things are at stake.
1. **"[the author]" is italicized.** Since it is a translator's supplement with no corresponding word in the original language, resting the weight of an argument on this phrase is risky.
2. **Sweeping the Immediate Context (⓪)**: verse 33 leads into the **worship-order passage** continuing at verse 34, "let your women keep silence in the churches." It addresses the conduct of a gathering, not the manner of God's existence.
3. **Logic**: "difficult for a person to understand" and "God creates confusion" are different propositions. This very sermon itself, at 1-1, defines biblical mystery as **"things that cannot be known by human reason"** — applying that same definition, "incomprehensibility" instead becomes a mark of mystery.

> 🔴 **An Internal Tension (Within Sermon 1 Itself)**: at 0:53, *"what Scripture calls a mystery, you see, is things that cannot be known by human reason."* ↔ at 11:53, *"something that cannot be understood intellectually... can we really accept, as a fully sound doctrine, something said to be the very foundation of our faith and salvation?"*
> The same quality (unknowable by reason) is used earlier as **a mark of mystery**, and later as **a mark of falsehood.** This does not change the verdict, but is kept as a §11 reserve card.

## 4-2. In-Depth Analysis — The 5 Core Items

### Claim #2-6 — the "One Throne" Argument → ❌ UNBIBLICAL, IRONCLAD

**The Preacher's Claim (Paraphrased)**: since heaven has only one throne (Rev 4:2), the Father and the Son cannot be two separate persons each seated on his own throne. Rev 22:1 says "the throne of God, even the Lamb," but other translations render it "the throne of God **and of** the Lamb," making it seem as though there were two thrones.

**[Biblical Rebuttal — the Literal KJV 1769 Wording]**

```
Rev 22:1  ...proceeding out of the throne of God AND OF the Lamb.
Rev 22:3  ...but the throne of God AND OF the Lamb shall be in it;
```

🚨 **The very form the preacher points to as "the error of other translations" is the English KJV itself.** the KJV 1769 repeats the possessive `of` **twice**: `of God **and of** the Lamb`. The appositive reading, "God, even the Lamb," comes from the vocabulary of the **Korean translation** in his hand, not from the **English KJV** he declared to be the sole standard for judgment.

Three independent rebuttals converge here.

| # | Verse | KJV Wording | Why It Collapses This Argument |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Rev 3:21** | *"am set down **with my Father** in his throne"* | Christ **Himself** says He is "set down **with** my Father in his throne." The text itself says that **the throne being one and the One seated being one are different questions** |
| 2 | **Rev 5:7** | *"he came and took the book out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne"* | the Lamb takes the book **out of the hand of the One sitting on the throne.** This cannot be a scene of Himself taking it from His own hand |
| 3 | **Rev 21:22** | *"the Lord God Almighty **and** the Lamb **are** the temple of it"* | a compound subject + **plural verb `are`.** Had John reckoned them as one person, it would have been `is` |

### Claim #4-3 — "No Dialogue Is Exchanged Between the Father and the Son" → ❌ IRONCLAD

**[KJV 1769] John 12:28** — *"**Father, glorify thy name.** Then came there a voice from heaven, [saying], **I have both glorified [it], and will glorify [it] again.**"*

A request → an answer is recorded, paired, within a single verse. The word of the request ("glorify thy name") and the word of the answer ("I have both glorified it") **correspond exactly.**

**His Escape Route, and Its Closure**:
* Escape: *"John 12:30 says, 'this voice came not because of me, but for your sakes,' so it was not an answer to Jesus"* (5:00)
* Closure: **[KJV 1769] John 12:30** — *"This voice came not **because of me**, but for your sakes."* "Because of me (= for my need)" is not the same as **"not to me."** This sentence speaks to the **purpose** of the utterance, not a denial of its **recipient.**
* A Second Closure: **Luke 3:22**, *"**Thou art** my beloved Son"* (2nd person) / **Matt 3:17**, *"**This is** my beloved Son"* (3rd person). Both persons are recorded at the same scene. If it were purely a public declaration, there would be no reason for the 2nd-person form to exist, nor would one refer to oneself in the 3rd person as "This is."

The grammar itself blocks the conclusion. ## 8-2. Three Structural Consequences This Shift Has Produced

### ① The "Image of Man" Analogy No Longer Holds

The **starting point and sole structural grounds** of this doctrine is the argument at 8/18, 10:30–11:36 — *"man was made in the image of God. So he is a single person made of three parts — spirit, soul, and body. Then God, too... we are laying out the logic that He is a single person composed of these same three elements."*

Yet the incarnate Christ of 8/24 simultaneously possesses the following.

```
deity ─ the Father (God's soul) + God's Spirit (the Holy Spirit)
humanity ─ a human body + a human soul + a human spirit
```

🚨 **Man has no such structure.** A man has one body, one soul, one spirit. The analogy "just as man is three, so God is three" **loses its explanatory power the instant the very thing it was meant to explain becomes five parts.** The analogy of 1 Thess 5:23 fails the same way — a man's body does not separately possess its own additional soul.

### ② The "Body=Jesus / Soul=Father" Label Has Been Quietly Replaced

At 8/17, 3:34, he reads Lackman's sentence aloud and approves it with **"Amen. Amen. Right?"** — *"His body is Jesus Christ, His soul is God the Father, and His spirit is undoubtedly the Holy Spirit."*

At 8/24, 15:20: **"The deity is the Father, the humanity is the Son."**

The two label systems are not the same. In the earlier system, "Son" is **the body**; in the later system, "Son" is **the entire humanity, complete with body, soul, and spirit.** Then where, in 8/24, is the soul that was "=the Father" back in 8/17? The answer at 8/24, 36:13 is, *"the Father present within Christ does not die, because He is the soul"* — but this differs in location from 8/12, 13:41's, *"the soul is in heaven and the body is on earth, isn't it?"*

### ③ "Two Natures" Is Precisely the Very Tool the Opponent Used

The declarations at 8/17 (2:10) and 8/18 (24:20) were **cautions against post-Nicene theological terminology.** Yet the formula "two natures (deity, humanity) within one person" belongs to **Chalcedon, AD 451**, and he himself already began using this framework at 8/18, 28:43, saying, *"this is a matter not of two persons but of two natures,"* and by 8/24 it had become the very title of the sermon.

> ⚖️ **This Is Recorded Fairly.** The word "nature" itself does appear in the KJV (2 Pet 1:4, *"partakers of the divine nature"*). Therefore the charge "he is using a word not found in Scripture" does not hold. What does hold is **a matter of consistency** — if the terminology the opponent uses is rejected as "Greek philosophy" while one's own framework is built from terminology of the same lineage, that standard has been applied to only one side.
