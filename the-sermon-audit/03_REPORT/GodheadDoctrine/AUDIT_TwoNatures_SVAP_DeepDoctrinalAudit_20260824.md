## 1. Summary of the Sermon's Doctrinal Development (AI Paraphrase)

* **Introduction (0:00~8:10)** — the preacher first introduces three counter-questions leveled at single-Godhead doctrine (is the body just a shell / is Jesus merely a body / were baptism and prayer a self-dialogue), then defines those counter-questions as the result of failing to understand "the two natures of God." He answers the other side's argument for the pre-existence of the Son (Prov 8:22-23, John 17:5) with the past-tense idiom of Eph 1:3-4 and Rom 8:29-30, and sets up an analogy of a circle to argue that God is not bound by time. At the end of this section, the scheme is presented: **"God is not three persons who are one, but three who are God,"** and **"of those three, the body of God will put on humanity and become the last Adam."**

* **Body 1 — The Timing and Manner of Acquiring Humanity (8:10~20:00)** — citing Gal 4:4, Matt 1:20, Luke 1:30-35, Heb 10:5, and 1 Tim 3:16 in sequence, he argues that what was made in Mary's womb is **the body God would dwell in**, and that God merely entered into that prepared body. Here comes the sermon's central proposition — **"God took on humanity, and as a result came to have a Father-Son relationship. The deity is the Father; the humanity is the Son."** He reads Ps 2:7 and Heb 1:5's "this day have I begotten thee" as pointing not to the origin of the Son, but to the point of the Incarnation.

* **Body 2 — The Problem of Two Souls and the Juxtaposition Argument (20:00~36:10)** — he assigns 1 John 5:7-8 to deity/humanity, and reads the "Holy Ghost" of verse 8 as **a man's spirit** set apart for the Incarnation. He then answers the anticipated counter-question, "doesn't this make God's soul and a human soul two?" with: **having a soul does not mean having personhood.** After establishing the coexistence of deity and humanity from Phil 2:5-8 and Col 2:9, he lists **nine examples** in which human weakness and divine power are recorded together within a single context.

* **Deepening and Conclusion (36:10~48:16)** — he says the Father within Christ is the soul and therefore does not die, and defines death as "not annihilation, but separation." He reads Stephen's vision in Acts 7:55-56 as "Christ present within the **glory** of God," and in the handing over of the kingdom in 1 Cor 15:23-26, identifies "He who shall subdue" as **the Father**. Finally, on the basis of the "everlasting Father" of Isa 9:6, he concludes that at the Second Coming, Israel will recognize Christ as that very Father of the Old Testament and address Him as such. The decision demanded of the audience is: **"rightly divide the two natures, and have a right relationship with the Lord."**

### Claim #66 — 1 Cor 15:24's "He Who Shall Subdue" Is the Father

> **Timecode** 42:50 | **C-Code** C-03 (Theological Conflict) + C-04 (Logical Self-Contradiction) | **Verdict** ❌ UNBIBLICAL — **STRONG**

**[Context]** to answer "how can Jesus, who occupies the position of the Son, become the Father," the preacher opens 1 Corinthians 15. After reading through verses 23-26, he designates the subject of the latter half of verse 24 as **the Father.**

**[The Preacher's Claim (Paraphrased)]** the preacher claimed that in 1 Cor 15:24's *"when he shall have put down all rule and all authority and power,"* the "he" refers to the Father, and that this must be so read because when Israel is restored, the God who once ruled over them will become King.

**[Analysis of the Problem — the Referent of the Pronoun Shifts]** within a single sentence in verse 24, `he` appears three times.

```
Then [cometh] the end,
  when HE(1) shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father;
  when HE(2) shall have put down all rule and all authority and power.
For HE(3) must reign, till HE(4) hath put all enemies under his feet.   (v. 25)
```

`he(1)` **hands the kingdom over to the Father**, and therefore cannot be the Father himself — one cannot hand something over to oneself. Yet the preacher switches only `he(2)`, within the coordinate `when` clause of the same sentence, to the Father. This violates `DEOVERLAP_Serial.md` **Principle 7 (the Pronoun-Consistency Lock)**, and this is **a problem at the level of the sentence itself**, prior to any theological position.

**[⓪ Result of Sweeping Adjacent Verses — 🎯 Hit]** the preacher **stopped reading at verse 26.** The very next two verses settle the verdict.

> **[KJV 1769] 1 Cor 15:27-28** — *"For he hath put all things under his feet. But when he saith all things are put under [him, it is] **manifest that he is excepted**, which did put all things under him. And when all things shall be subdued unto him, then shall **the Son also himself be subject unto him** that put all things under him, that God may be all in all."*
>
> **[Paraphrase]** He who put all things under His feet is **explicitly excepted** by the author. Then **the Son himself** will be **subject** to Him who subjected all things to Him.

`excepted` is, in this paragraph, **a word the author himself uses to explicitly mark that two figures are being distinguished.** It is not an interpretation added by the auditor.

**[Spiritual Consequence]** switching the subject of the kingdom's handover to the Father causes the believer to lose the place where they read the crowning act of obedience — "Christ presents the kingdom to the Father." The **voluntary submission of the Son** that verses 27-28 establish is a pattern for the believer's own submission; that pattern disappears.

**[Why the Grade Is STRONG — Why It Is Not Raised to IRONCLAD]** the preacher's own system already explicitly holds, at 23:11, the structure in which **the humanity (the Son) obeys the deity.** On that premise, there is room for the obedience of the Son in verse 28 to **be absorbed** into his own explanation. Therefore verses 27-28 are not a knockout blow but **pressure**. However, the pronoun shift within verse 24 itself holds regardless of his premises, so the ❌ verdict stands.

### Claim #8 — John 17:5's Pre-Existence Argument Is Answered by the Past-Tense Idiom of Rom 8:29-30

③ **⓪ Sweeping Adjacent Verses — 🎯 Hit.** verse 24 of the same prayer repeats the same expression.

> **[KJV 1769] John 17:24** — *"for **thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world**."*

## 6. Chapter/Part Explanations

### Chapter 1. "God Already Had a Body From the Start" — Addressing Claims #11·#12

**The Argument**: the preacher says God is not three who are one, but **simply three who are God.** Of those three, he says the body of God will put on human nature and become the last Adam.

**Why It Sounds Plausible**: just as a person has spirit, soul, and body, saying God likewise has three lets one picture "three" and "one" holding together at a single glance. The greatest strength of this picture is that it finishes the explanation without using a single difficult word.

**Why It Actually Falls Apart**: opening the place where Scripture actually uses the phrase "the last Adam" reveals something different.

> **1 Cor 15:45, 47** — *"the last Adam was made a quickening **spirit**. ... The second man is **the Lord from heaven**."*

Scripture calls the last Adam not a **body** but a **spirit**, and says he **came from heaven.** In the preacher's picture, that body is prepared in Mary's womb. But something that comes from heaven and something prepared in a womb have different origins.

**Simple Analogy**: it is like someone saying "this package was made in our own warehouse," while the shipping label on the package reads "Origin: Seoul." Reading the label settles the argument.

**Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL → see PART A #11·#12

### Chapter 2. "What Was Made in Mary's Womb Was Only a Body" — Addressing Claim #20

**The Argument**: the preacher says what was made in Mary's womb is the **body** God would **enter and dwell in.** God was not born, he says — He merely entered into a prepared body.

**Why It Sounds Plausible**: Hebrews 10:5 actually does say "thou hast prepared me a body." Since the sentence "a body was prepared" is right there in Scripture, the explanation that God entered that body follows naturally. This explanation also instantly removes the discomfort produced by the phrase "God was born of a woman."

**Why It Actually Falls Apart**: simply look at what the very sentence the preacher himself read on air calls that thing.

> **Luke 1:35** — *"that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called **the Son of God**."*

Scripture calls it **the Son of God.** The preacher calls it a **body.** The names are different. And Galatians 4:4 is the same — the sentence itself directly states what was made of the woman. It is **"his own Son."** The word **"body"** never once appears in that sentence.

**Simple Analogy**: it is like an invitation reading "Mr. Kim Cheolsu is invited," and someone saying "here, 'Kim Cheolsu' doesn't mean the person, it means the clothes Kim Cheolsu will wear." Reading the invitation again gives the answer.

**Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL — STRONG → see PART A #20

### Chapter 3. "Father and Son Are Names That Arose at Christmas" — Addressing Claims #27·#28

**The Argument**: the preacher says that, **as a result** of God taking on human nature, the relationship called Father and Son came to exist. God's side is the Father, the human side is the Son.

**Why It Sounds Plausible**: this makes the difficult question, "were the Father and Son each separately present?", disappear entirely. If the two names arose at Christmas, there was nothing to divide before that. The explanation is short and requires no difficult words.

**Why It Actually Falls Apart**: simply count, in Scripture, how far back the name "Father" is used. **Long before Jesus was born**, in the Old Testament, there are places calling God Father — **eleven times, across eight books.** One of them nails it down explicitly.

> **Isa 63:16** — *"O LORD, thou art our father, our redeemer. **Thy name is from everlasting.**"*

if the name "Father" arose at Christmas, "from everlasting" has no place to stand.

And the preacher himself, near the end of the sermon (44:52), says that **God was already the Father of Israel in the Old Testament.** In other words, this rebuttal comes not only from Scripture but from his own mouth.

**Simple Analogy**: it is like someone saying "this shop's name was only given last year," while a photograph of the shop's sign from ten years ago still exists. Once the photo is produced, there is nothing more to say.

**Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL — STRONG → see PART A #27·#28

### Chapter 4. "That Spirit Is Not the Holy Spirit but a Man's Spirit" — Addressing Claim #43

**The Argument**: the preacher says that, of the three who bear witness in 1 John 5:8, the first is not the **Holy Spirit but a man's spirit.** He adds that it makes no difference whether it is written with a capital or lowercase letter.

**Why It Sounds Plausible**: since verse 7 bears witness from heaven and verse 8 from earth, it seems tidy that something belonging to man would come on the earthly side. The water and blood came from a human body, so it seems to match that the first item is also something human.

**Why It Actually Falls Apart**: reading two verses earlier shows the author already wrote the answer down.

> **1 John 5:6** — *"it is the Spirit that beareth witness, because **the Spirit is truth**."*

the author himself, in verse 6, already stated who that witnessing Spirit is. Moreover, he calls that Spirit **"truth"** — a word never attached to a man's spirit. The preacher began reading from verse 7, but the answer was in verse 6.

In addition, the claim that upper- and lowercase make no difference does not match the actual Bible text. Romans 1:4, which he cites as grounds, is printed with a **lowercase** letter, while 1 John 5:8, which he is discussing, is printed with a **capital** letter. The Bible itself has already marked the two places differently.

**Simple Analogy**: it is like solving question 3 on a test and saying "I can't tell who this is about," while the answer is written right there in question 1. Just turn back a page.

**Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL — STRONG → see PART A #43

### Chapter 5. "What Stephen Saw Was Not God, But the Glory of God" — Addressing Claim #61

**The Argument**: the preacher says Acts 7 does not say "God" but "the **glory** of God," and so what Stephen saw was Christ present within that glory.

**Why It Sounds Plausible**: the phrase "the glory of God" really is in the text, and Jesus appears right after it. Since the two expressions sit side by side, one is tempted to read them as one and the same.

**Why It Actually Falls Apart**: simply read to the end of the very sentence he read.

> **Acts 7:55** — *"...saw the glory of God, and **Jesus standing on the right hand of God**"*

three things appear in one sentence. The glory of God is one; Jesus is one; and God, at whose **right hand** Jesus stands, is yet another one. And in the very next verse, "the right hand of God" appears **once more.** No one can stand at their own right hand.

**Simple Analogy**: it is like a sentence saying "I saw Minsu standing beside Cheolsu and Younghee," and someone claiming "only one person appears here." The word "beside" already requires two people.

**Verdict**: ❌ UNBIBLICAL — STRONG → see PART A #61

### Chapter 6. "The Soul Does Not Disappear" — Addressing Claims #57·#58·#59

**The Argument**: the preacher says that even when a person dies, the soul only separates from the body and **does not disappear.** Death is not disappearance, he says, but division. And he uses this to support the claim that "the Father within Christ, being the soul, does not die."

**This Part Is Correct — It Should Not Be Rebutted**: Scripture genuinely says this. The rich man in Hades **lifts up his eyes, sees, speaks, and feels torment** (Luke 16:23-25). Revelation says the torment continues **"for ever and ever"** (Rev 20:10, 14:11). Mark repeats **three times** that *"their worm dieth not"* (Mark 9:44, 46, 48). James describes death as *"the body without the spirit,"* that is, as **separation** (James 2:26).

> 🚨 **This audit got this point wrong once.** The first edition set up two verses that say "the soul dies/is destroyed" (Ezek 18:4, Matt 10:28) as counterexamples. But the word rendered "destroy" in Matt 10:28 is also translated **"lost"** elsewhere in Scripture — *"this child was **lost**, and is found"* (Luke 15:24), *"let nothing be lost"* (John 6:12). **What is lost can be found. It has not disappeared.** And the word "die" is a word **the preacher had already defined as "separation,"** so it cannot be used to strike him with. **Ruin and disappearance are different words.**

**So the Only Question That Remains Is This**: it is correct that the soul does not disappear. The problem is the sentence before that — **where is it written that "the Father is the soul"?**

The sentence in which Scripture directly defines **what** God **is**, as "He is," reads as follows.

> **John 4:24** — *"**God is a Spirit**, and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."*

it says **"a Spirit."** There are places where the expression **"my soul"** is used of God (Matt 12:18, Isa 42:1, Heb 10:38) — this is granted. But **"God has a soul"** and **"the Father is the soul"** are different statements, and **there is not a single verse** that states the latter as it stands.

**Simple Analogy**: "that person has hands" and "that person is hands" are entirely different statements. The truth of the first does not make the second follow.

**Verdict**: #58·#59 → ✅ BIBLICAL (granted) / #57 → ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED → see PART A #57·#58·#59
