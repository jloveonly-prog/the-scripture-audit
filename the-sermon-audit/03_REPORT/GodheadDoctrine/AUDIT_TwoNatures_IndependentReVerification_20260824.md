**SERMON VERDICT: 🔴 ALERT**

This manuscript contains **confirmable observations**, such as the true humanity and divine power of Jesus, the virgin conception, and death being described not as the soul's simple annihilation but as separation. # PART A — Verdicts by Claim

## 2. Core Proposition P

**P (the sermon's conclusion paraphrased in one sentence)**: the one God entered a human body prepared in Mary and took on humanity, and as a result the deity became the Father and the humanity became the Son. Therefore Jesus Christ is the everlasting Father.

### Verdict on P: ❌ UNBIBLICAL — STRONG

The following passages sever P's chain of connection from several different directions.

1. **The Precedence of the Title "Father"** — Deut 32:6 calls the LORD "thy father" prior to the Incarnation, and Isa 63:16 says "thou, O LORD, art our father … thy name is from everlasting." Therefore the proposition that the title "Father" first arose at the Incarnation cannot be sustained.
2. **The Dialogue Between Father and Son / Distinct Referents** — in John 12:27 Jesus speaks to the "Father," and 12:28 records the response that came from heaven as a separate verse. John 17:5 likewise distinguishes first and second person: "O Father … the glory which I had with thee before the world was."
3. **The Context of Handing Over the Kingdom** — in 1 Cor 15:24, the one who delivers up the kingdom to "God, even the Father" is distinguished within the sentence from "the Son also himself" and "him that put all things under him" in verses 27-28. There is no basis in the text for switching the referent of `he` in the latter half of verse 24 alone.

> **Scope Limitation**: this judgment extends only to the fact that Scripture describes the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as distinct. This report does not attach the name of a separate philosophical system to that fact and render judgment on that basis.

# PART B — Plain-Language Explanation

## 4. What Is Correct, and Where the Conclusion Leaps

This sermon places side by side the record that Jesus grew weary, grieved, and died, and the record of Him ruling over wind, death, and disease. This is an honest observation. This report does not deny it either.

But one more step is needed after the observation. To move from "two kinds of record exist together" to "therefore the name 'Father' arose at the Incarnation, and 'Son' means humanity" requires a text that states that connection. That connecting statement was not produced.

The simplest way to check this is to read the very next line after the sermon stops quoting.

* Reading 1 Cor 15:24 alone leaves room to switch the pronoun's referent. But verses 27-28 speak, within a single paragraph, of both the Son himself and the One who subjected him.
* Isolating 1 John 5:7 alone tempts one to define the "Spirit" of verse 8 separately. But 5:6-8 is a single flow stating that the Spirit bears witness and is truth.
* Seizing only on the word "glory" in Acts 7:55 lets one fold the vision into a single subject. But the very same verse records Jesus as the one standing at God's right hand, and the next verse again records Him as the Son of man.

## 5. Statements to Fairly Grant the Other Side First

The following three statements should be granted at the outset.

1. The observation that human weakness and divine power appear together in Jesus is present in the text.
2. The claim that death is described not as the soul's simple annihilation but as separation is consistent with James 2:26 and the texts on conscious continuance.
3. Isa 9:6, 1 Tim 3:16, and Col 2:9 must not be used in a way that diminishes the divine majesty of Jesus.

Even granting these three points, the core proposition — reading the Father-Son relationship as having arisen after the Incarnation — does not automatically follow.
