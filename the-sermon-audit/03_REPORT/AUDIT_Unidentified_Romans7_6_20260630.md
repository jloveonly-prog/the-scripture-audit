## 3. Sub-Report (BVCAP 2.0 Precision-Strike Analysis)

### The Issue: Does Romans 7:6 Mean "The Law Died" (KJV), or "We Died" (the Majority Text)?

**① The Collapse of the First Line of Defense: the Limits of Surface-Level Isomorphism (TYPE-AU)**
- Beza and the KJV translators were fixated on the structure of the analogy in verses 1-3 (the wife is freed only once the husband dies). Reasoning within the analogy that the first husband — the law — had to die for the wife to be freed, they adopted a conjectural emendation and chose the singular form ("that being dead").

**② TYPE-AT (Reductio ad Absurdum on a Divine Attribute) Deployed 💥**
- "Can the law die?"
- Anchoring the attributes of the law in Scripture: "the law is holy... and good" (Rom 7:12), "the law is spiritual" (Rom 7:14), "one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law" (Matt 5:18).
- **Verdict**: since the law is an essential reflection of God's holiness and justice, it is eternal and imperishable. The claim "the law died" (KJV) produces a fatal contradiction (Reductio Ad Absurdum), destroying the eternality and holiness (a divine attribute) of the God who gave the law.

**③ TYPE-AC (Counter-Hypothesis Verification) and Paul's 'Deliberate Twisting of the Analogy'**
- If the law could die, Paul would have had no need, in verse 4, to say specifically "ye also are become dead."
- In verses 1-3, Paul draws the analogy of the husband dying, but when he applies it to reality in verse 4, he **deliberately twists the analogy.**
- Because the first husband (the law) is eternal and imperishable — it can never be put to death! The only way to end this absolutely unbreakable marriage (the law's condemnation) was for **'we,' the wife, to die together with Jesus on the cross.**

**④ Comprehensive Conclusion (Verdict)**
- Contextually, theologically, and text-critically (TYPE-H), Romans 7:6 is correct as **"because we are dead (ἀποθανόντες)."**
- Beza failed to grasp Paul's deep theological reversal (the law does not die — we die), and in his effort to force it to fit the surface-level formula of the analogy in verses 1-3, he committed the fatal error of inserting a singular form (ἀποθανόντος) into Scripture that exists in no manuscript at all.
- Therefore, the KJV's "that being dead" (i.e., because the law died) is ❌ **UNBIBLICAL**, and the preacher's criticism is ✅ **BIBLICAL**.

## 4. Spiritual Lesson (LESSON-6)
The Word of God is far too deep and intricate to be confined within one or two surface-level analogical structures. The paradox of the cross — in which, to preserve the holiness of the law, God gave 'ourselves' over to death together with His Son, rather than abolishing (killing) the law — is the true gospel of Romans 7. Through the full arsenal of BVCAP, we were able to precisely expose how one human scholar's (Beza's) hasty conjecture distorted the heart of the gospel (our death) into "the death of the law." True truth is revealed not through the blind defense of a particular version (the KJV), but by going wherever the logic of the Word itself leads.
