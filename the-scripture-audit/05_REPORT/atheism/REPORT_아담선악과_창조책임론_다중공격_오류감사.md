**"The bird from the east"** = The plan of judgment upon Babylon through Cyrus (King of Persia).

This verse does not mean **"God planned every daily choice of every human individual."** It is a declaration of the **historical plan of judgment and salvation that God Himself will execute**. The "My" in "My counsel" is God, and the content of that counsel in context is **"the judgment of Babylon through Cyrus."**

**TYPE-G Verdict**: The error of **context hijacking**, ignoring the context and illegitimately expanding "all my pleasure" to mean "every individual human choice." ✅

The "all things" in **"who worketh all things"** refers to the **context of the entire chapter of Ephesians 1 = the plan of salvation**. It does not mean "God decided even what a human individual will eat tomorrow."

**TYPE-G Verdict**: Unjustified expansion of the scope of "all things" from the context (the plan of salvation) to "all human choices." ✅

## Phase 2: TYPE-R (Detecting Subject/Syntax Confusion)

Judges 1:19 KJV:

> *"And the LORD was with Judah; and **he** drave out the inhabitants of the mountains; but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron."*

**Core Analysis: Who is the antecedent of "he"?**

```
Sentence Structure:
  Clause 1: "the LORD was with Judah"
        → Subject: the LORD / Verb: was / Prepositional phrase: with Judah
        → The LORD was with Judah.

  Clause 2: "and he drave out the inhabitants of the mountains"
        → Subject: he / Verb: drave out

  Clause 3: "but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley"
        → Subject: (he — implied) / Verb: could not drive out

Grammatical Verdict:
  → In English grammar, the antecedent of "he" is the nearest singular masculine noun.
  → Structure of Clause 1: "the LORD was with Judah"
  → "with Judah" — Judah is the topical focus of the sentence.
  → "he" = Judah (the tribe of Judah).

  → Additional evidence: If "he" after "the LORD was with Judah" referred to God,
    it would mean "God was with him, but God could not drive them out."
    → This self-negates the meaning of "being with" within the same sentence.
    → In Hebrew narrative, "the LORD was with" is an idiomatic expression for assistance/backing,
      not a statement that Jehovah Himself is the direct agent of combat.
```

**Cross-Verification with Parallel Passages:**

| Passage | KJV Original Text | Structure |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Jdg 1:22 | "the house of Joseph... and the LORD was with **them**" | Jehovah "with" = backing. The agent of action is the house of Joseph. |
| Jos 14:12 | "if so be the LORD will be with **me**, then **I** shall be able to drive them out" | Caleb's words: If the LORD is with me, then "**I** shall be able to drive them out" — Agent of action = Caleb. |

**TYPE-R Verdict**: "he" = Judah. It was not God who was pushed back by chariots of iron, but **the tribe of Judah** that could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley. Subject misreading confirmed. ✅
