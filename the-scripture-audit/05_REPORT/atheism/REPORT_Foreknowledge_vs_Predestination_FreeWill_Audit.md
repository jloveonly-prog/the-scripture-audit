### 🔵 TYPE-AL (Equivocation Detection)

> **Trigger Reason**: The attacker equivocates "know" with "determine"

**The single pillar upon which the attacker's entire argument parasitizes** is this:

```
❌ The Attacker's Stealthy Equivalence:
   "God knows in advance" = "God determined in advance"
   (Foreknowledge)         (Predetermination)
```

These two are in **completely different conceptual categories**:

| Concept | Definition | Agent | Analogy |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Foreknowledge** | The ability to **observe/perceive** the outcome in advance | Observer | A security guard watching a CCTV screen |
| **Predetermination** | The act of **deciding/controlling** the outcome in advance | Controller | A programmer coding a robot |

When moving from P2 ("He knows the future") to C1 ("Choices are determined"), the attacker **sneakily swapped "knowing" for "determining."** In classical logic, this is a logical fraud called the **Equivocation Fallacy**.

**TYPE-AL Verdict**: **Equivocation fallacy confirmed** by shifting the meaning of "know" to "determine" ✅

## Phase 4: Modern Analogy (ANALOGY-5)

### Analogy 1: The CCTV Security Guard

> In a casino, there is a CCTV that can see the future. The security guard looks through the monitor and sees in advance **what card the gambler will play tomorrow**. Did the guard "determining the gambler's choice" by "seeing" the result? **No.** The guard merely observed. It was the gambler's hand that picked up the card. **Observation and control are different.**

### Analogy 2: The Exam Professor

> A professor with 30 years of experience accurately pierces through a student's ability and **predicts with 100% accuracy** before the exam, "This student will score 70 points." Just because the professor's prediction was correct, **did the professor decide the 70 points?** The one who wrote the answers was the student. The professor's ability of foreknowledge does not deprive the student of free will.

### Analogy 3: Rewatching a Movie

> You rewatch today a movie you saw yesterday. You **already know** what choice the protagonist will make at the end. Did your knowing the ending **determine the protagonist's choice**? No. You are merely an observer. **God's perspective is that of someone watching a movie for the second time.** He knows everything, but He does not make the choices on behalf of the characters.
