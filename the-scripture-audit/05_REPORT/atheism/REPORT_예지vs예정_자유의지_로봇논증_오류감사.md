### 🔵 TYPE-AL (Equivocation Detection)

> **Trigger Reason**: The attacker equates "know" with "determine"

The **single pillar upon which the attacker's entire argument is parasitic** is this:

```
❌ The attacker's stealthy equation:
   "God foreknows" = "God predetermined"
   (Foreknowledge)   (Predetermination)
```

These two are **completely different conceptual categories**:

| Concept | Definition | Agent | Analogy |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Foreknowledge** | The ability to **observe/perceive** the result in advance | Observer | A CCTV security guard watching the screen |
| **Predetermination** | The act of **determining/controlling** the result in advance | Controller | A programmer coding a robot |

When moving from P2 ("knows the future") to C1 ("choices are fixed"), the attacker **subtly swapped "knowing" with "determining."** In classical logic, this is a logical fraud called the **Equivocation Fallacy**.

**TYPE-AL Judgment**: **Equivocation fallacy confirmed** for shifting the meaning of "know" to "determine" ✅

## Phase 4: Modern Analogies (ANALOGY-5)

### Analogy 1: The CCTV Security Guard

> In a casino, there is a CCTV that can see the future. The security guard looks through the monitor and sees in advance **what card the gambler will play tomorrow**. Does the guard "seeing" the result mean he "determined the gambler's choice"? **No.** The guard merely observed. It is the gambler's hand that picks up the card. **Observation and control are different.**

### Analogy 2: The Exam Professor

> A professor with 30 years of experience accurately perceives a student's ability and predicts with **100% accuracy** before the exam, "This student will score 70." Just because the professor's prediction was correct, **did the professor determine the score of 70?** It was the student who wrote the answers. The professor's foreknowledge does not strip the student of free will.

### Analogy 3: Rewatching a Movie

> You rewatch a movie today that you saw yesterday. You **already know** what choice the protagonist will make at the end. Does your knowing the ending mean that **you determined** the choice of the protagonist in the movie? No. You are merely an observer. **God's viewpoint is like the viewpoint of someone watching the movie for a second time.** He knows everything, but He does not make the choices on behalf of the characters.
