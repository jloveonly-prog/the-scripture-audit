### 🔴 Fallacy #1: False Dichotomy (E-12 / TYPE-AM Triggered)

**Pattern**: *Setting up the framing as if only these two cases exist: "Evil Oppressor = Heaven" vs. "Good Victim = Hell"*

| The Opponent's Dichotomy | Actual Biblical Categories |
|:---|:---|
| A: If the wicked believeth → Heaven | ① If the wicked **truly** repenteth and believeth → Salvation |
| B: If the good believeth not → Hell | ② If the wicked believeth **with lips only** → No Salvation (Matt 7:21-23) |
| *(Third Category Hidden)* | ③ If the good **heareth** the gospel and believeth → Salvation |
| *(Fourth Category Hidden)* | ④ He that **hath not heard** the gospel → God's Righteous Judgment (Rom 2:12-16) |

> **TYPE-AM Verdict**: The opponent presenteth only the most extreme cases A and B, intentionally hiding categories ②③④.
> Especially ② — **"If he saith he believeth yet remaineth wicked, did he truly believe?"** (Script line 151) directly breaketh this dichotomy.
> This is a False Dichotomy. **Dismissed.**
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Acknowledgment of the Legitimacy of Corner Case Verification and Re-defense:**
>
> When verifying the fairness of a system, substituting the most vulnerable extreme case (Corner Case) is a legitimate methodology. This point is acknowledged.
>
> However, for this Corner Case to actually operate, one premise is required: the oppressor's **"true" repentance**.
> In Christian theology, the scenario of "believing in the final moment before death" doth not mean a simple utterance of "I believe," but rather **sincerely acknowledging one's sin and turning to God** (the thief in Luke 23:39-43).
>
> Thus, this Corner Case is not "the oppressor goeth free without cost," but rather "grace is applied the moment the oppressor truly faceth his own wickedness."
> It is entirely understandable to still feel this is unjust — but that debate is not a "false dichotomy fallacy" but rather a separate subject regarding the **"fairness of grace itself."**

### 🔴 Fallacy #2: Equivocation — Shifting Definition of "Good" (No E-Code / TYPE-AL Triggered)

**Pattern**: *Using the word "good" based on human standards in the first half of the argument, and as if by God's standard in the second half*

| Point of Utterance | Definition of "Good" | Purpose |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| Initial Attack | "One who liveth kindly without harming others" (Human societal standard) | To establish the victim as guiltless |
| Deepened Attack | "One whom God ought not to cast into hell" (Standard of absolute perfection) | To argue the injustice of God |

> **TYPE-AL Verdict**: The definition of "good" hath shifted during the argument.
> Relative goodness in human society (a pickpocket < a murderer) was equated with **absolute innocence in the court of God**.
> PART 3 of the video script accurately detecteth this: *"That is merely a standard by which humans measure themselves against one another."* (Line 63)
> This is the fallacy of Equivocation. **Dismissed.**
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Conditional Limitation Acknowledged:**
>
> If the opponent's argument is not "that person is absolutely flawless," but rather raising an issue of **proportional justice** — "Is it just that a more wicked person receiveth a better outcome?" — then this is an independent issue regarding retributive justice, not a shifting definition of the same word.
> In this case, it shifteth not to equivocation but to a **debate over the standard of salvation itself**, which belongeth to the realm of #5 (Burden of Proof) rather than this section.
> Since the opponent's actual utterance currently under analysis equateth "good" with "one whom God ought not to cast into hell," this verdict is maintained, but it is acknowledged that this verdict doth not directly apply to the proportional justice framing.

### 🔴 Fallacy #3: Straw Man + Equivocation — The Attacked Doctrine Itself Doth Not Exist (E-01 + TYPE-AL Triggered)

**Pattern**: *The opponent attacketh the position of "forgiveness = moral exemption" which Christianity doth not actually profess — this is not attacking the doctrine itself but attacking a Straw Man.*

| Distinction | The Opponent's Understanding (Confusion) | The Actual Structure of Scripture |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Function A | Forgiveness = Sin is erased (Moral Exemption) | Forgiveness = The penalty is **executed by proxy** (Judicial Substitution) |
| Function B | The oppressor goeth free without cost | The penalty is **fully executed** upon the cross |
| Analogy | A judge simply letteth the criminal go | A judge **putteth his own son to death in his stead** and letteth the criminal go |

> **E-01 Verdict (1st Fallacy — Straw Man)**: The object the opponent attacketh is **not orthodox Christian doctrine.**
>
> | Distinction | The Position the Opponent Attacketh | Orthodox Christian Doctrine (Actual) |
> |:---|:---|:---|
> | Opponent's Understanding | "God simply turneth a blind eye to sin" → Moral Exemption | The penalty is **fully executed** upon Christ → Judicial Substitution |
> | Conclusion | God hath abandoned justice | The justice of God is **fully satisfied** upon the cross |
>
> Christianity doth not teach that "if the wicked believeth, he goeth free without cost." Christ bore that penalty in his stead.
> The opponent is not attacking the doctrine Christianity actually professeth, but is **attacking a doctrine he hath misunderstood**.
> This is the Straw Man fallacy (E-01).
>
> **TYPE-AF Verdict**: The attacker claimeth it is "unjust," but **hath not explicitly developed which principle of justice hath been violated.**
>
> **Refinement**: It is true that many atheists implicitly presuppose principles such as proportionality, retributive justice, or moral desert.
> Therefore, rather than saying "no principle was presented at all," it is more accurate to say — **"A principle was implicitly presupposed but not explicitly developed."**
>
> **Distinction Between What is Required and What is Not Required:**
>
> | Distinction | Content | Required? |
> |:---|:---|:---:|
> | Excessive Demand | Submit a completed objective moral framework | ❌ Not Required |
> | Legitimate Demand | If it is "unjust," **explicitly develop** upon **which principle of justice** the judgment is based | ✅ Required |
>
> It is true that many moral arguments begin in the manner of "a specific case intuitively conflicteth with a principle of justice."
> However, **moral intuition alone is not an argument but a sentiment**.
> To transition from intuition to an argument, one must at least present:
>
> 1. **Which principle** was violated (e.g., proportionality, retribution, equity, etc.)
> 2. **Why that principle** ought to be applied to this situation
>
> The opponent hath not explicitly developed these two.
> Because the argument is incomplete, it is **Dismissed.**
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Conditional Limitation Acknowledged:**
>
> In philosophy, presenting counterexamples based on moral intuition is also recognized as a legitimate starting point for an argument (e.g., one doth not need a completed ethical framework to argue that "slavery is unjust").
> However, whereas the moral injustice of slavery is based on widely shared intuition, the issue of the fairness of God is a **meta-controversial domain**.
> Therefore, while maintaining the verdict that the opponent's argument "did not explicitly develop the principle," it is acknowledged that this is closer to **"the argument is incomplete"** rather than "a violation of the burden of proof is confirmed."
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Anticipated Counter-objection and Preemptive Defense:**
>
> The objector may raise the legal-philosophical question: "Is it just for an innocent third party to bear the punishment instead?"
> This question is not a category mistake but an independent attack aimed at the **fairness of Penal Substitution itself**.
>
> However, this attack doth not hold for the following three reasons:
>
> 1. **Fulfillment of the Voluntariness Condition**: Jesus is not an innocent third party dragged by force, but a **voluntary party** (John 10:18 — *"I lay it down of myself"*). In legal philosophy, voluntary vicarious suffering is categorically different from forced vicarious suffering.
> 2. **Fulfillment of the Authority Condition**: The redeemer is not a creature but **the Lawgiver Himself** (God). It is not the judge sending his son, but **the judge Himself standing at the place of execution** (Col 2:9, John 1:14).
> 3. **Acknowledgment of Analogy's Limits**: The analogy of a human court is but an approximation and cannot fully contain the ontological asymmetry (Creator-creature) of the God-human relationship. However, this doth not prove the logical impossibility of atonement itself.
>
> ∴ The legal-philosophical counter-objection is **valid as an independent topic of debate**, but it cannot invalidate the exposure of the original category confusion (exemption vs. substitution).
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Defense of Victim-Centered Justice:**
>
> The question, "Even if atonement resolveth the God-sinner relationship, **what becometh of justice for the human victim who truly suffered?**" is an entirely understandable inquiry.
>
> Biblical Response:
>
> 1. **Substitutionary atonement is the resolution of the God-sinner relationship, not the resolution of the oppressor-victim relationship.** The cross resolved "the penalty for God's law," and justice between humans is addressed separately.
> 2. **Justice for the victim is eschatologically guaranteed.** Scripture saith, "God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes" (Rev 21:4), promising final Restoration.
> 3. **Scripture also acknowledgeth that justice on this earth is imperfect.** Ecclesiastes 8:14 — lamenting *"there be just men, unto whom it happeneth according to the work of the wicked; again, there be wicked men, to whom it happeneth according to the work of the righteous: I said that this also is vanity"* — and promiseth it shall be set right in the final judgment.
>
> However, whether this response fully assuageth the present suffering of the victim remaineth as an independent topic of debate.

### 🔴 Fallacy #4: Appeal to Emotion + Red Herring (E-06 + E-02)

**Pattern**: *"How grieved the victim must be!" — Substituting logical refutation with emotional indignation*

```
The opponent's actual argument structure:
  Premise: The victim is grieved (Emotion)
  Conclusion: ∴ God's system of salvation is flawed (Logic)
  
  → There is no logical link in the leap from premise (emotion) to conclusion (logic).
```

**Precise Analysis — Distinction Between Emotional Elements and Logical Fallacies:**

| Distinction | Content | Verdict |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| Observation 1 | The opponent's expression **containeth** emotional elements (indignation, grievance) | ✅ Fact |
| Observation 2 | Just because emotion is included, it **doth not automatically make the whole argument an emotional fallacy** | ✅ True |
| Observation 3 | But after removing the emotion, **is there a remaining logical structure?** | ❌ None |

What remaineth of the opponent's attack if emotion is removed:
- ❌ Internal contradiction of the biblical text → None presented
- ❌ Logical self-contradiction of soteriology → None presented
- ❌ Argument for an alternative system of Justice → None presented
- ✅ "It is unjust that the outcomes of the oppressor and the victim are reversed" → **This is the sole substantive claim**

Yet this sole substantive claim itself **doth not present the standard of "unjust"** (→ Linked to Fallacy #5: Shifting the Burden of Proof).

> **E-06 + E-02 Verdict**: The mere mixing of emotions doth not decisively conclude it as an emotional fallacy.
> However, after removing the emotional element, **no independently standing logical argument existeth**,
> and the remaining sole substantive claim ("it is unjust") faileth to present its own standard.
> ∴ Since the **substantive driving force of the argument relieth on emotion**, it is judged as an Appeal to Emotion.
> Shifting the focus from the structural coherence of soteriology to the emotion of the victim is a Red Herring.
> **Dismissed.**
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Conditional Limitation Acknowledged — The Philosophical Status of Moral Intuition:**
>
> In philosophy, the feeling of "grievance" is not a mere emotion but can function as a **Moral Intuition**.
> Aversion to slavery, racism, and torture also initially began from emotion, and philosophers acknowledge this as a legitimate starting point for argument.
>
> Furthermore, the opponent's argument can be **reconstructed in the form of Retributive Justice as follows:**
>
> ```
> [Reconstruction of Retributive Justice]
>   Premise 1: Justice requireth outcomes proportional to similar moral deserts.
>   Premise 2: The outcome of Christian salvation is determined independently of deeds.
>   Conclusion: ∴ It is unjust.
> ```
>
> In this case, the opponent's argument becometh an **argument for retributive justice** rather than a simple appeal to emotion.
> If properly developed formally, this argument can no longer be classified as E-06.
>
> Therefore, this verdict is refined as follows:
>
> | Distinction | Verdict | Reason |
> |:---|:---:|:---|
> | "I am grieved" → **As a moral intuition** | ✅ Legitimate starting point | Moral intuition is a philosophically valid basis for argument |
> | "I am grieved" → **Developed in the form of retributive justice** | ✅ Legitimate argument (but requireth development) | E-06 cannot be applied in this form |
> | "I am grieved" → **Therefore the system is flawed** | ❌ Argument incomplete | A conclusion cannot be drawn merely by presenting intuition/principle |
>
> What this report classifieth as an "appeal to emotion" is not the emotion itself, but the fact that
> **there is no logical mediation in the leap from emotion to systemic critique**.
> If the opponent presenteth an explicit principle of justice (e.g., "Giving different outcomes for the same crime goeth against retributive justice"), that argument can no longer be classified as a simple appeal to emotion.
> At that point, the debate shifteth to the **applicability of that principle of justice** (→ Linked to #5 Burden of Proof).
> Since there is no such explicit development of a principle in the opponent's actual utterance currently under analysis, this verdict is maintained, but **the legitimacy of the moral intuition and the retributive justice framework is acknowledged**.

### 🔴 Fallacy #6: Inverse Application of Reductio ad Absurdum — The Opponent's Alternative Begetteth Greater Absurdity (TYPE-AI Triggered)

**Pattern**: Assuming the opponent's claim to be true and tracing its logical consequences

**Assumption**: "It is only fair if one can go to heaven by good works"

> **⚠️ Legitimacy of Applying Reductio ad Absurdum — Preemptive Clarification:**
>
> The objector may counter that "atheists have never explicitly professed 'salvation by works'."
>
> However, reductio ad absurdum can be applied not only to **explicit claims** but also to **implicit alternatives**:
>
> 1. To criticize that "salvation by faith is unjust," that criticism **implicitly presupposeth a fairer alternative**.
> 2. The alternative implied by the criticism of fairness is logically one of three: (a) salvation based on good works, (b) universal salvation, or (c) the absence of salvation itself.
> 3. Whichever alternative is chosen, it becometh a target for reductio ad absurdum.
> 4. **Criticism without an alternative is not an argument but an expression of discontent**, and in this case, it reduceth back to Fallacy #5 (Shifting the Burden of Proof).
>
> ∴ Even if there is no explicit claim, tracing the **alternative implied by the criticism** and applying reductio ad absurdum is a **legitimate argumentative strategy**.

| Consequence | Result | Verdict |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| Consequence 1: A "Baseline of Good Works" is needed | What percentage of goodness passeth? 51%? 80%? 99%? → Impossible to set a baseline | ❌ |
| Consequence 2: Who measureth? | Measured by humans? → Biased. Measured by God? → Ultimately God's judgment = Identical to the structure of Scripture | ❌ |
| Consequence 3: Only the victim is saved? | The victim may be an oppressor to someone else → All humans are simultaneously victim+oppressor | ❌ |
| Consequence 4: The cross becometh unnecessary | If good works suffice, Jesus' death is a meaningless event → Total denial of Christianity | ❌ |

> **TYPE-AI Verdict**: If the alternative "it is fair to go to heaven by good works" is assumed to be true,
> **all 4 consequences collapse**: inability to set a baseline + contradiction in the measuring subject + overlapping of victim-oppressor + making the cross meaningless.
> ∴ The opponent's alternative begetteth **greater absurdity** than Christian soteriology. **Dismissed.**
>
> ---
> **⚠️ Acknowledgment of the Atheist's Actual Conclusion and Re-defense:**
>
> The true conclusion implied by the atheist's criticism might not be a "better system of salvation," but a declaration that **"a god with such a system of salvation is a product created by humans."**
> In this case, the target of reductio ad absurdum changeth: not the contradiction of salvation by works, but the **loss of the moral perfection of the Christian God** becometh the conclusion.
>
> The Christian response to this:
> 1. **The conclusion that it is a "human product" is itself an interpretation, not an argument**. The same fact (moral paradox) can be interpreted as the "paradox of grace" or "human bias," and which one is correct requireth a separate argument.
> 2. **The self-defeating nature of criticizing the alternative remaineth valid.** Even if the conclusion "A is a human product" is true, the absurdity of B (the alternative) existeth separately.
> 3. However, since this debate risketh the entirety of Christianity's **truth claim**, it is a higher-level debate beyond the scope of this section (the internal logic of soteriology).

> **⚠️ Finite Sin vs. Infinite Punishment — Defense of the Proportionality Issue:**
>
> The counter-argument that "inflicting eternal punishment for sins committed during a finite time goeth against proportionality" is an entirely understandable question.
>
> Biblical Response:
>
> 1. **The basis of punishment is not the "quantity of deeds" but the "severance of relationship"**. Hell is not because one "committed many sins," but is the "result of rejecting relationship with God." Eternal separation is the continuation of the severed relationship with the eternal Source.
> 2. **The principle of proportionality presupposeth a "deed-outcome" frame**, whereas the structure of judgment in Scripture is a **"relationship-outcome" frame**. Failing to acknowledge the difference between the two frames causeth a category mistake.
> 3. **C.S. Lewis's perspective**: "The doors of hell are locked on the inside" — that is, the interpretation existeth that hell is not a prison where God forcibly locketh people up, but a **state voluntarily chosen by those who rejected God**.
>
> However, this response is persuasive only when the "relationship frame" itself is accepted, and against an opponent who criticizeth from the "deed frame," the additional task remaineth to first argue the **legitimacy of the frame itself**.
