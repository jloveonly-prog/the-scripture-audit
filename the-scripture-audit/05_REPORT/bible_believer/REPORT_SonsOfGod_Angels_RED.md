# RED — "The sons of God" are not angels

> **Base Document**: This RED argument is developed based on [[REPORT_그하나님의아들들_천사인가_조사.md]] (results of the definite article investigation).
> **Application MODE**: B (Theological Court — doctrinal debate within Bible-believing Christians). Operating on the premise that "the Bible is 100% truth," the identity of "the sons of God" in Genesis 6 and Job 1, 2, and 38 is determined solely by internal KJV evidence.

---

## Introductory Issue — Why equate them with "angels" when the words themselves are different?

The English expression "sons of God" appears repeatedly in five places: Genesis 6:2, 6:4, Job 1:6, 2:1, and Job 38:7. The traditional angel theory groups these five passages into a single category, concluding that "the sons of God = angels." RED argues that this very first step is a fallacy — because the first fact confirmed by the investigation document is that **the same English expression can refer to different beings in different dispensations**.

---

## 1. Original Language Definite Article Evidence — Job 38 is a different syntax from Genesis 6 and Job 1-2 (TYPE-G)

As confirmed by the investigation document (§3):

| Passage | Hebrew Construction | Definite Article (ha) |
|---|---|:---:|
| Gen 6:2, 6:4 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | Present |
| Job 1:6, 2:1 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | Present |
| Job 38:7 | כָּל־בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים | **Absent** |

Genesis 6 and Job 1-2 are **identical syntax** in Hebrew. Only Job 38:7 is a different syntax, and this difference is best explained by the genre conventions of prose (the prologue of Genesis and Job) versus poetic books (the main body of Job) (Investigation Document §5, Gesenius §126). That is:

> **"The sons of God" in Genesis 6 and Job 1-2 are highly likely to refer to the same category, whereas Job 38 is a different syntax that must be examined separately.**

This is the starting point of RED — whatever conclusion is drawn from Job 38 (whether it is angels or not), there is no basis to automatically transfer and apply that conclusion to Genesis 6. Just because the name "the sons of God" is the same does not mean they are all in the same dispensation or the same beings.

---

## 2. The Judicial Verdict of Genesis 6 — The subject of the sentence is "man (ha-adam)" (TYPE-C, TYPE-R)

Gen 6:3 KJV: *"My spirit shall not always strive with **man**, for that **he also** is **flesh**: yet **his days** shall be an hundred and twenty years."*

The grammatical subject of this verdict from beginning to end is **`ha-adam` (man)**. If the agents of the crime were angels, there should be at least a mention of "those angels" or "because of these angels" somewhere in the verdict. However, between 6:2 (the crime) and 6:3 (the verdict), the subject quietly changes from "the sons of God" to "man (ha-adam)" — this is a reversal of the judicial subject, and cannot be explained if the criminals were angels.

## 3. Inability of Angels to Reproduce (TYPE-E, EXPLICIT)

Matt 22:30 KJV: *"in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven."*

Jesus explicitly declares angels as beings outside the institution of marriage and reproduction. Heb 1:14 defines the nature of angels as *"ministering spirits, sent forth to minister"* — servants sent forth, and Heb 1:5 confirms that angels have never received a coronation declaration like that of Psalm 2:7 ("Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee") (however, expanding this verse alone to a general proposition that "angels can by no means be sons" is a TYPE-AL fallacy and therefore is not used — instead, it is reinforced by the §4 evidence below).

## 4. 1 Pet 3:20 vs 2 Pet 2:4 — Conflicting Attitudes of the Same Author (TYPE-P, TYPE-G)

Placing two passages written by the same author, Peter, side by side:

| Passage | Subject | God's Attitude |
|---|---|:---:|
| 1 Pet 3:20 | The spirits in prison in the days of Noah | *"longsuffering"* — suffered long |
| 2 Pet 2:4 | The angels that sinned | *"spared not"* — spared not, summary imprisonment |

If the criminals of Genesis 6 were identical to the "angels that sinned" in 2 Pet 2:4, it would mean that the same author left completely opposite records for the same subjects: "suffered long" and "spared not." If the KJV is inerrant, the subjects of these two cannot be the same beings.

## 5. The "ἀρχή (first estate)" of Jude 1:6 — Traces of Demotion (TYPE-G)

Jude 1:6 KJV: *"the angels which kept not their **first estate**, but left their own habitation..."*

The Greek word **ἀρχή** for "first estate" is the very same word translated as "principalities" in Eph 6:12 (confirmed by re-verification of the original language, `영적존재기준표_추정.md` §1-C). What they failed to keep was not merely a position of an errand-runner, but a **principality**. The premise that "these beings were angels (servants) from the beginning" and the text's description that "they lost their initial principality (ἀρχή)" stand in tension with each other — rather, it is more coherent with the text to read it as a structure in which they originally held a different status, but their status was demoted, so they are now called by the name "angels."

> ⚠️ **Honest Disclosure of Unresolved Issues**: Exactly when the event of Jude 1:6 occurred (whether in the first world prior to Genesis 1:2 or in Genesis 6 itself) is not yet fully agreed upon even within this group of documents (`영적존재기준표_추정.md` §5-B places it in the first world, while some early documents place it in Genesis 6). RED does not hide this unresolved point — however, the conclusion itself that "the angels of Jude ≠ the sons of God in Genesis 6 (not the same beings)" remains as an independent evidence of §2-4, regardless of the determination of the timing.

## 6. Conclusion

"The sons of God" in Genesis 6 differs from "the sons of God" in Job 38 starting with the Hebrew syntax (§1). The subject of the judicial verdict of the Genesis 6 event is "man" from beginning to end (§2); the Bible explicitly defines angels as beings outside the institution of reproduction and marriage (§3); the conflicting attitudes left by the same author (§4); and the language of "loss of estate" in Jude (§5) all align better with the reading that they were "beings originally of a different category whose status was demoted due to the fall" rather than the "angel theory."

**Verdict: "The sons of God" in Genesis 6 (and Job 1-2) are not literal angels (servants/errand-runners), which is the CLASS defined by Hebrews 1:14.** Their exact origin (whether sons of Adam prior to the knowledge of good and evil, or separate residual beings of the first world) requires a separate argument beyond the scope of this RED document, and `bible_believer/영적존재기준표_추정.md` §5 (CLASS 3) contains that detailed argument.
