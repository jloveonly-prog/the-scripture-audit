## Q1. Who is the object of "longsuffering" in 1 Peter 3:20?

**🔴 Pastor's Objection**

> The direct objects of "when once the longsuffering of God waited" in 1 Peter 3:20 are the **people** of Noah's day.
> It points to the humans who were given a chance to repent but ultimately did not believe, not fallen angels.

**🔵 Our Answer**

This objection is structurally self-contradictory. Please place the two verses recorded by the **same author, Peter**, side by side.

| Verse | KJV Original Text | God's Attitude |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| **1 Pet 3:20** | *"the longsuffering of God waited"* | Longsuffering → **Mercy, Patience** |
| **2 Pet 2:4** | *"God spared not the angels that sinned"* | Spared not → **Summary Execution** |

If the "spirits in prison" in 1 Pet 3:19 are fallen angels, and the "longsuffering" in 1 Pet 3:20 is God waiting for those angels, then Peter left completely opposite records for the same object (angels): **"showed mercy (longsuffering)"** and **"spared not"**.

If the KJV is inerrant, the objects of these two **cannot be the same being.**
Angels of 2 Pet 2:4 = Summary judgment / Object of "longsuffering" in 1 Pet 3:20 = Humans or Sons of God.
Peter recorded them by separating the objects.

## Q2. Is the "120 years" in Genesis 6:3 the ark construction period (countdown to repentance)?

**🔴 Pastor's Objection**

> During the approximately 120 years of building the ark (Gen 6:3), God gave people an opportunity to repent.
> "Longsuffering" is an expression that shows God's grace.

**🔵 Our Answer**

**Original Text of Gen 6:3:**

> *"yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years"*

The core is **"his days"**. The Hebrew original is **יָמָיו (yamav)** — this word is used throughout the Bible to indicate **lifespan or lifetime**.

> - Psalm 102:11: *"My **days** are like a shadow that declineth"* — Meaning lifespan
> - Job 10:20: *"Are not my **days** few?"* — Meaning lifespan

God's sentence is not "the flood will come in 120 years," but a sentence of life abbreviation: **"I limit the lifespan of these beings to 120 years."**

**Why is this important?**
If 120 years were a countdown to judgment, it would mean God **publicly announced the exact year and month** of the judgment.
However, Jesus declared this in Matt 24:36:

> *"But of that day and hour knoweth **no man**, no, not the angels of heaven, but **my Father only**."*

If one claims a "120-year countdown", it directly conflicts with this verse.
**Proclaiming a specific date of judgment is an error structurally identical to Date-setting.**

## Q3. Are the beings in Genesis 6 "angels" or "sons of God"?

**🔴 Pastor's Objection**

> The "spirits in prison" in 1 Peter 3:19 are the **fallen angels** of Genesis 6.
> Fallen angels are already under judgment (2 Pet 2:4) and are not the objects of waiting for repentance.

**🔵 Our Answer**

The original Hebrew text of Genesis 6:2, 4 is **בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (Bene Ha-Elohim, Sons of God)**.
The Bible never, not even once, recorded these beings as **"angels (מַלְאָך / malak)"**.

**Decisive difference seen through etymology:**

| Word | Original Word | Etymological Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Angel** | מַלְאָך (malak) / ἄγγελος (angelos) | **Messenger, Envoy** — Functional title |
| **Son** | בֵּן (ben) / υἱός (huios) | **Essential relationship, Existential category** |

The word 'angel' itself points to a **function and office**, not the essence of existence.
Hebrews 1:14 confirms the essence of angels in a single sentence.

> *"Are they not all **ministering spirits**, **sent forth** to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?"*

**The two core attributes of an angel: "ministering" + "sent forth"**
Sons inherit, angels are sent.

Jude 1:6 drives the wedge in.

> *"angels which kept not their **first estate (ἀρχή)**, but left their own **habitation (οἰκητήριον)**"*

**οἰκητήριον (oikētērion, habitation)** appears **only twice** in the entire KJV (Jude 1:6 / 2 Cor 5:2).
In 2 Cor 5:2, it means the **resurrection body**. In other words, they are beings with their own domain of rule and home base.
**A messenger (angel) does not have its own habitation.** The place the master sends them is their habitation.
The beings in Genesis 6 are royal rulers who **abandoned and left** their own habitation on their own accord.

## Q4. If angels have bodies, can't they reproduce?

**🔴 Pastor's Objection**

> Angels have bodies, but they do not have flesh.
> Equating body and flesh is an error.
> They can be fruitful and multiply, but having a body does not mean they have the ability to reproduce.

**🔵 Our Answer**

We agree. That logic actually strengthens our argument.

**"Body" and "flesh" are different categories strictly distinguished by the Bible:**

| Word | Original Word | Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Body** | σῶμα (sōma) / גּוּף (guf) | Form, substance — Angels can also have form in this dimension |
| **Flesh** | σάρξ (sarx) / בָּשָׂר (basar) | Biological organism — **Lineage of reproduction, growth, and death** |

The fact that angels can appear with a form (σῶμα) (Heb 13:2 — entertaining angels while entertaining strangers) and having reproducible flesh (σάρξ/basar) are **completely different dimensions**.

Look at the reason for God's judgment in Genesis 6:3.

> *"for that he also **is flesh (בָּשָׂר / basar)**"*

The very reason God judged them is because these beings entered the lineage of **basar (reproducible biological flesh)**.
If they were angels, it means angels belonged to the lineage of basar.
However, Jesus blocked this head-on in Matthew 22:30.

> *"For in the resurrection they neither **marry**, nor are **given in marriage**, but are as the **angels of God** in heaven."*

There is a reason Jesus compared the state of resurrected saints to angels.
It is because angels are beings that do not marry, that is, **beings that do not belong to the reproductive lineage (basar)**.
This is the premise of the comparison. If this premise collapses, Jesus's comparison itself becomes invalid.

#### 🔗 3-Step Logic Chain — Confirmed by Jesus's Direct Statement

> **Heb 1:14 (KJV)**: *"Are they not all **ministering spirits**"*
> → Essence of angel = **spirit**

> **Luke 24:39 (KJV)**: *"a **spirit** hath not **flesh and bones**, as ye see me have"*
> → A spirit has **no flesh and bones** (Jesus's direct statement)

```
Angel = Spirit (Heb 1:14)
Spirit = No flesh (Luke 24:39)
∴ Angel = No flesh (reproductive flesh)
```

#### 📊 1 Corinthians 15:39-40 — Angels are not in the flesh classification table

> **1 Cor 15:39-40 (KJV)**:
> *"All flesh is not the same flesh: but there is one kind of flesh of **men**, another flesh of **beasts**, another of **fishes**, and another of **birds**. There are also **celestial bodies**, and bodies terrestrial"*

The kinds of flesh directly listed by Paul: **Men · Beasts · Fishes · Birds**
And **separately** — Celestial bodies / Terrestrial bodies

> Heavenly beings (angels) are **not in the flesh list**, and are classified into a separate **"body" category**.

**Conclusion:** Since angels have form (σῶμα) but no reproducible flesh (σάρξ/basar),
the beings who copulated with the daughters of men and reproduced in Genesis 6 cannot be angels.

## Q5. Is "Angel" a species name or a status/office name?

**🔴 Pastor's Objection (Counterattack)**

**🔵 Our Answer — This objection actually strengthens our thesis**

Your observation is accurate, Pastor. And for that very reason, it is confirmed that the beings in Genesis 6 are not angels but **beings who were originally "sons"**.

---

### ① KJV `-eth` Suffix — The true meaning of "maketh his angels spirits"

Psalm 104:4 / Hebrews 1:7 (KJV):

> *"Who **maketh** his angels spirits; his ministers a flaming fire"*

Here, `maketh` is the **3rd person singular present continuous** in archaic KJV grammar.

| Form | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `make` | Simple verb base form |
| `maketh` (He + `-eth`) | **"Is constantly and continuously making even now"** — Continuous Present |

That is, God didn't just make the angels once and that was it, but **He is holding them in that state even at this moment**. This simultaneously shows that the verb `maketh` indicates **the granting and maintenance of status** — being held by God as "servants (ministering spirits)" is the current state of angels.

---

### ② Daniel 10:13 — Why the KJV calls spiritual beings "Prince" instead of "Angel"

> *"But the **prince** of the kingdom of Persia withstood me one and twenty days:
> but, lo, Michael, one of the **chief princes**, came to help me..."*

**Decisive Observation**: The KJV called the same spiritual beings `prince`, not `angel`. Michael is also a **`chief prince`**, not an `angel`.

Why is that? Because they are **royal beings with their own domain of rule and authority**. This is **exactly consistent** with our interpretation of the "sons of God" in Job 38:7 as royal rulers. The Bible calls a royal ruler a `prince`, not an `angel (messenger)`.

---

### ③ Revelation 12:7-9 — "Satan's angels" is decisive proof of status/affiliation language

> *"Michael and **his angels** fought against the dragon;
> and the dragon fought and **his angels**"* (Rev 12:7)
> *"he was cast out... and **his angels** were cast out with him."* (Rev 12:9)

**This verse confirms the core of our thesis:**

| Expression | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **"Michael's angels"** | Servants who receive and execute Michael's commands |
| **"Satan's angels (his angels)"** | Servants who receive and execute Satan's commands |

The word `angel` is actually used in the KJV as a status/affiliation language meaning **"one who belongs to someone and executes their commands"**. Just as the beings who followed Satan became "Satan's angels", the beings in Jude 1:6 were also **originally "sons" but were demoted to the status of "angels (servants)" after leaving their habitation and falling**.

This means that your objection is correct, while simultaneously supporting our thesis that **the beings in Genesis 6 took the daughters of men before that demotion occurred, that is, while still in the status of "sons"**.

### ④ Philippians 2:7 + Hebrews 2:9 — The only biblical precedent for "a son descending to servant status"

To your question, "Can a son be demoted to a servant (angel)?", the Bible presents only one case. It is not a creature, but **the incarnation of Jesus Christ, the actual Son of God**.

> **Phil 2:7 (KJV)**: *"But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him **the form of a servant**"*

> **Heb 2:9 (KJV)**: *"Jesus, who was made **a little lower than the angels**"*

The event where the Son of God **voluntarily** put on the form of a servant and descended to a position lower than the angels — this is the **only legal precedent** for a "son → servant status" shift in the entire Bible. And it was not a forced demotion, but **voluntary humility**.

The beings in Jude 1:6 are the opposite. They were demoted as a result of sinning by **abandoning and leaving their own habitation (oikētērion) on their own accord**.

## Q6. Colossians 1:20 — "things in heaven" reconciled = Are angels saved too?

### ① Colossians 1:16 — "Things in heaven" was already defined 4 verses earlier

> **Col 1:16 (KJV)**: *"by him were all things created, that are **in heaven**, and that are **in earth**... whether they be **thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers**"*

**"Things in heaven" in Col 1:20 = "Things in heaven" in Col 1:16 = Thrones, dominions, principalities, powers**
This is not the salvation of individual angels, but the **restoration of the cosmic order of authority**.

---

### ② "Reconcile" ≠ "Save" — Philippians 2:10 proves it

> **Phil 2:10 (KJV)**: *"That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things **in heaven**, and things **in earth**, and things **under the earth**"*

"Things in heaven" bowing their knees = This is not salvation. **It also includes submitting through judgment**.

```
Reconcile = All things are subsumed under the sovereignty of Christ
          = Includes both the saved + the judged
         ≠ All beings are saved
```

---

### ③ Colossians 1:22 — The method of reconciliation limits the scope

> **Col 1:22 (KJV)**: *"In **the body of his flesh through death**, to present you holy"*

The mechanism of reconciliation = **"through death in the body of his flesh"**
**Beings without flesh** cannot be beneficiaries of this method.
Since angels have no flesh (Q4 argument) → They are outside the scope of this reconciliation.

| Verse | Role |

| **Col 1:16** | "Things in heaven" = Thrones, dominions, powers (Not individual angels) |
