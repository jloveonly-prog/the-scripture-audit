## Q1. Who is the target of the "longsuffering" in 1 Peter 3:20?

**🔴 Researcher's Rebuttal**

> The direct target of "when once the longsuffering of God waited" in 1 Peter 3:20 is the **people** in the days of Noah.
> It points to the humans who were given a chance to repent but ultimately did not believe, not fallen angels.

**🔵 Our Response**

This rebuttal creates a structural self-contradiction. Place the two verses recorded by the **same author, Peter**, side by side.

| Verse | KJV Original Text | God's Attitude |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| **1 Peter 3:20** | *"the longsuffering of God waited"* | Longsuffering → **Mercy, Patience** |
| **2 Peter 2:4** | *"God spared not the angels that sinned"* | Spared not → **Immediate Execution** |

If the "spirits in prison" in 1 Peter 3:19 are fallen angels, and the "longsuffering" in 1 Peter 3:20 was God waiting for those angels, then Peter has recorded two diametrically opposed attitudes—**"showing mercy (longsuffering)"** and **"sparing not"**—toward the exact same subjects at the exact same time.

If the KJV is inerrant, these two **cannot be the same subjects.**
The angels in 2 Peter 2:4 = Immediate judgment / The target of "longsuffering" in 1 Peter 3:20 = Humans or the Sons of God.
Peter recorded them by dividing the subjects.

## Q2. Is the "120 years" in Genesis 6:3 the ark construction period (a countdown to repentance)?

**🔴 Researcher's Rebuttal**

> God gave people a chance to repent during the approximately 120 years of building the ark (Gen 6:3).
> "Longsuffering" is an expression showing God's grace.

**🔵 Our Response**

**Genesis 6:3 Original Text:**

> *"yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years"*

The key is **"his days."** The Hebrew original is **יָמָיו (yamav)** — this word is used throughout the Bible to indicate **lifespan or lifetime**.

> - Psalm 102:11: *"My **days** are like a shadow that declineth"* — Refers to lifespan.
> - Job 10:20: *"Are not my **days** few?"* — Refers to lifespan.

God's sentence is not "the flood is coming in 120 years," but a sentence of life abbreviation: **"I am limiting the lifespan of these beings to 120 years."**

**Why is this important?**
If 120 years were a countdown to judgment, it would mean God **publicly announced the exact year, month, and day** of judgment.
However, Jesus declared this in Matthew 24:36:

> *"But of that day and hour knoweth **no man**, no, not the angels of heaven, but **my Father only**."*

If one argues for a "120-year countdown," it directly clashes with this verse.
**Proclaiming a specific date for judgment is a structural error identical to Date-setting.**

## Q3. Are the beings in Genesis 6 "Angels" or "Sons of God"?

**🔴 Researcher's Rebuttal**

> The "spirits in prison" in 1 Peter 3:19 are the **fallen angels** of Genesis 6.
> Fallen angels are already under judgment (2 Pet 2:4) and are not the subjects God waited for to repent.

**🔵 Our Response**

The Hebrew original of Genesis 6:2, 4 is **בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (Bene Ha-Elohim, Sons of God)**.
The Bible never, not even once, records these beings as **"Angels (מַלְאָך / malak)"**.

**The Decisive Difference Seen Through Etymology:**

| Word | Original Word | Etymological Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Angel** | מַלְאָך (malak) / ἄγγελος (angelos) | **Messenger, Envoy** — A functional title |
| **Son** | בֵּן (ben) / υἱός (huios) | **Essential relationship, Existential category** |

The word 'Angel' itself refers to **function and office**, not the essence of existence.
Hebrews 1:14 confirms the essence of angels in a single sentence:

> *"Are they not all **ministering spirits**, **sent forth** to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?"*

**The two core attributes of an angel: "ministering" + "sent forth."**
Sons inherit; angels are sent.

Jude 1:6 drives the final nail:

> *"angels which kept not their **first estate(ἀρχή)**, but left their own **habitation(οἰκητήριον)**"*

**οἰκητήριον (oikētērion, habitation)** appears only **twice** in the entire KJV (Jude 1:6 / 2 Cor 5:2).
In 2 Corinthians 5:2, it means the **resurrection body**. It means a being possessing its own inherent domain of rule and headquarters.
**A messenger (angel) has no habitation of his own.** The place where the master sends him is his habitation.
The beings in Genesis 6 are royal rulers who **voluntarily abandoned and left** their own habitation.

## Q4. Do angels also have flesh and reproductive capabilities?

**🔴 Researcher's Rebuttal (Actual Statement)**

> *"I don't understand on what basis you divide body and flesh.
> The word flesh is also used for angels. It is used for animals too.
> And angels also have reproductive capabilities.
> On what grounds do you say they have no reproductive capabilities?"*

**🔵 Our Response**

If the researcher's claim were true, it could not pass **Matthew 22:30**.

**"Body" and "flesh" are distinct categories strictly separated by the Bible:**

| Word | Original Word | Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Body** | σῶμα (sōma) / גּוּף (guf) | Form, substance — Angels can possess form in this dimension |
| **Flesh** | σάρξ (sarx) / בָּשָׂר (basar) | Biological organism — **The lineage of reproduction, growth, and death** |

The fact that angels can appear with a form (σῶμα) (Heb 13:2 — entertaining angels unawares) and possessing reproductive flesh (σάρξ/basar) are on **completely different dimensions**.

Look at God's reason for judgment in Genesis 6:3:

> *"for that he also **is flesh(בָּשָׂר / basar)**"*

The very reason God judged them is that these beings entered the lineage of **basar (reproductive biological flesh)**.
If they were angels, it would mean angels belonged to the lineage of basar.
However, Jesus blocked this head-on in Matthew 22:30:

> *"For in the resurrection they neither **marry**, nor are **given in marriage**, but are as the **angels of God** in heaven."*

There is a reason Jesus compared the state of resurrected saints to angels.
It is because angels are beings that do not marry; that is, they are **beings not belonging to the reproductive lineage (basar)**.
This is the premise of the comparison. If this premise collapses, Jesus' comparison itself cannot be established.

#### 🔗 3-Step Logic Chain — Confirmed by Jesus' Own Direct Statement

> **Heb 1:14 (KJV)**: *"Are they not all **ministering spirits**"*
> → The essence of an angel = **spirit**

> **Luke 24:39 (KJV)**: *"a **spirit** hath not **flesh and bones**, as ye see me have"*
> → A spirit has **no flesh and bones** (Jesus' direct statement)

```
Angel = Spirit (Heb 1:14)
Spirit = No flesh (Luke 24:39)
∴ Angel = No flesh (reproductive flesh)
```

#### 📊 1 Corinthians 15:39-40 — Angels are absent from the classification table of flesh

> **1 Cor 15:39-40 (KJV)**:
> *"All flesh is not the same flesh: but there is one kind of flesh of **men**, another flesh of **beasts**, another of **fishes**, and another of **birds**. There are also **celestial bodies**, and bodies terrestrial"*

The types of flesh directly listed by Paul: **men · beasts · fishes · birds**
And **separately** — celestial bodies / bodies terrestrial.

> Heavenly beings (angels) are **not in the flesh list**, but are classified under a separate **"body" category**.

**Conclusion:** Because angels have form (σῶμα) but no reproductive flesh (σάρξ/basar), the beings in Genesis 6 who cohabited with the daughters of men and reproduced could not be angels.

## Q5. Is "Angel" a species name, or a status/office name?

**🔵 Researcher's Point (Counterattack)**

> The concept of angel also includes the "status of a servant." In Daniel, a prince withstood Gabriel, and in Revelation, Satan's servants are expressed as "angels."
> In other words, couldn't a being who was originally a "son" fall and be demoted to a servant (angel)?

**→ This observation actually strengthens our thesis.**

This point confirms that the beings in Genesis 6 were not originally angels, but **beings who were originally "sons."**

### ① KJV `-eth` Suffix — The true meaning of "maketh his angels spirits"

Psalm 104:4 / Hebrews 1:7 (KJV):

> *"Who **maketh** his angels spirits; his ministers a flaming fire"*

Here, `maketh` is the **third-person singular continuous present** in archaic KJV grammar.

| Form | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `make` | Simple verb base |
| `maketh` (He + `-eth`) | **"Continually making them even now"** — Continuous Present |

In other words, God did not make the angels once and that was it, but **He is holding them in that state even at this moment**. At the same time, this shows that the verb `maketh` represents the **bestowal and maintenance of status**—the current state of an angel is being held by God as a "servant (ministering spirit)."

---

### ② Daniel 10:13 — Why the KJV calls spiritual beings "Prince" instead of "Angel"

> *"But the **prince** of the kingdom of Persia withstood me one and twenty days:
> but, lo, Michael, one of the **chief princes**, came to help me..."*

**Decisive Observation**: The KJV called the same spiritual beings **`prince` (ruler, monarch)**, not `angel`. Michael is also not an `angel`, but a **`chief prince`**.

Why is this? Because they are **royal beings possessing their own inherent domain of rule and authority**. This is **exactly consistent** with our interpretation of the "Sons of God" in Job 38:7 as royal rulers. The Bible does not call royal rulers `angels (messengers)`, but `princes (rulers)`.

---

### ③ Revelation 12:7-9 — "Satan's angels" is conclusive proof of status/affiliation language

> *"Michael and **his angels** fought against the dragon;
> and the dragon fought and **his angels**"* (Rev 12:7)
> *"he was cast out... and **his angels** were cast out with him."* (Rev 12:9)

**This verse confirms the core of our thesis:**

| Expression | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **"Michael's angels"** | Servants who receive and execute Michael's commands |
| **"his angels" (Satan's angels)** | Servants who receive and execute Satan's commands |

The word `angel` is actually used in the KJV as a status/affiliation language meaning **"one who belongs to someone and executes their commands."** Just as the beings that followed Satan became "his angels," the beings in Jude 1:6 were also **originally "sons" but were demoted to the status of "angel (servant)" after leaving their habitation and falling.**

This means that **the researcher's observation is correct**, while at the same time supporting our thesis that **the beings in Genesis 6 took the daughters of men before that demotion occurred, that is, while they still held the status of "sons."**

### ④ Philippians 2:7 + Hebrews 2:9 — The only biblical precedent of a "Son descending to the status of a servant"

Regarding the question raised by the researcher, "Can a son be demoted to a servant (angel)?", the Bible presents only one case. It is not a creature, but the **incarnation of God's own Son, Jesus Christ.**

> **Phil 2:7 (KJV)**: *"But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him **the form of a servant**"*

> **Heb 2:9 (KJV)**: *"Jesus, who was made **a little lower than the angels**"*

The Son of God **voluntarily** took the form of a servant and came down to a position lower than the angels—this is the **only legal precedent** in the entire Bible of a transition from "Son → Servant status." And it was not a forced demotion, but **voluntary humility.**

The beings in Jude 1:6 are the opposite. They were demoted as a result of sinning by **voluntarily abandoning and leaving their own habitation (oikētērion).**

"Things in heaven" bowing their knees = not salvation. **It also includes subjugation by judgment.**

```
Reconcile = All things being brought under the sovereignty of Christ
          = Includes both those saved + those judged
          ≠ All beings are saved
```

### ③ Colossians 1:22 — The method of reconciliation limits the scope

> **Col 1:22 (KJV)**: *"In **the body of his flesh through death**, to present you holy"*

The mechanism of reconciliation = **"through death in the body of his flesh"**
**Beings without flesh** cannot be the beneficiaries of this method.
Since angels have no flesh (Q4 argument) → they are outside the scope of this reconciliation.

| Verse | Role |

| **Col 1:22** | Method of reconciliation = Body of flesh → Angels without flesh are excluded |
