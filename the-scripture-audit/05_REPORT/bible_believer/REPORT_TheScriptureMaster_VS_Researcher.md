### 🔧 TYPE-G (KJV Grammar Dissection) — Genesis 6:3 Original Language Anatomy

> **Reason for Deployment:** Necessary to analyze the Hebrew structure of the subject, object, and reason clause in the sentence of judgment in Gen 6:3

#### 📖 Genesis 6:3 KJV Original Text Breakdown

> *"And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with **man**, for that **he also** is **flesh**: yet **his days** shall be an hundred and twenty years."*

| KJV Word | Hebrew Original Word | Grammatical Analysis |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **man** | **אָדָם (ha-adam)** | Object of judgment sentence = Adam (Mankind). Not an angel. |
| **he also** | **בְּשַׁגָּם הוּא (beshaggam hu)** | `be-`(because) + `she-`(that) + `gam`(also) + `hu`(he). Subject of reason clause = ha-adam |
| **flesh** | **בָּשָׂר (basar)** | Perishable mortal flesh. Exact opposite category of ruach(spirit) in Isa 31:3. |
| **his days** | **יָמָיו (yamav)** | Biological lifespan. Same root as the genealogy formula in Gen 5. |

#### 🔍 TYPE-G Verification Result

**Analyzing the entire sentence of judgment (Gen 6:3), there is not a single mention of angels:**

| Judgment Sentence Element | Original Text Subject/Object | Expected in Angel Theory | Actual Original Text |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---:|
| God's striving partner | `ha-adam` | Angel (Criminal) | ❌ ha-adam |
| Reason clause (Why judge?) | `he also is flesh` | Angel's nature/crime | ❌ Mankind's nature (basar) |
| Object of lifespan/period sentence | `his days` | Angel or Angel+Mankind | ❌ days of ha-adam |

> **TYPE-G Conclusion:** The sentence of judgment in Gen 6:3 deals exclusively with **ha-adam (Mankind)** from beginning to end. If angels were the ringleaders of the crime, the complete absence of angels in God's judgment sentence is a **Structural Void**.

#### 📖 TYPE-G Additional Analysis — Isaiah 31:3 Category Opposition Verification

> *"their horses **flesh (בָּשָׂר basar)**, and **not spirit (רוּחַ ruach)**"* (Isa 31:3 KJV)

Isaiah 31:3 declares an **explicit dichotomous opposition** between `basar (flesh)` and `ruach (spirit)`.

| Category | Hebrew | Biblical Usage |
|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **Flesh** | בָּשָׂר (basar) | Humans, beasts — mortal material beings |
| **Spirit** | רוּחַ (ruach) | Angels (Heb 1:7), Spirit of God — immaterial beings |

Heb 1:7: *"Who maketh his angels **spirits**"* — Angel = spirits

Gen 6:3: *"he also is **flesh (basar)**"*

> **TYPE-G Judgment:** Angels are defined as **spirits** in Heb 1:7. In Isa 31:3, **basar (flesh) ≠ ruach (spirit)** is a dichotomous opposition. The interpretation that an angel, which is a spirit, "became" basar (perishable flesh) violates this category opposition.

It is true that the words are separated. However, that separation is not **"angels vs humans" but "the dead vs the living"**:

| Word | Original Language | KJV Usage | Actual Meaning |
|:---:|:---:|:---|:---|

```
v19: spirits in prison = those who died and are in prison in a non-flesh state (spirit)
v20: eight souls       = those who survived the flood alive in the flesh in the ark
```

> *"your whole **spirit** and **soul** and **body**"*

|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|

| **Those who survived in the ark** | ✅ Survives | ✅ Survives | ✅ Survives | **"eight souls"** = Complete person |

It is correct that Peter **intentionally distinguished** between "spirits" and "souls". However, that distinction is not **angels vs humans**, but **those whose soul and body were destroyed leaving only the spirit (spirits) vs survivors with complete souls and bodies (souls)**.
