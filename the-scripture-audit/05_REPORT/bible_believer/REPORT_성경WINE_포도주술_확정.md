### 🔍 8-B. "Even When Wine is Mingled with Water, It is Still Wine" — Ancient Naming Customs and Metonymy Analysis

#### Core Question

> The ancients drank wine extract mingled with water at a 3:1 (Water 3 : Wine 1) ratio. Then 75% of what is in that cup is **water**. Yet the Bible still calls this mingled beverage **"wine"**. Why is it called "wine" and not "water"? Are there such naming customs elsewhere?

#### Answer: Metonymy — "The core ingredient that imparts identity" determines the name

In linguistics, this phenomenon is called **metonymy**. The name of the whole beverage does not follow the ingredient with the most volume, but the name of the **core ingredient that determines identity, taste, and function**.

#### Universal Usages in Modern Daily Life

| Beverage | Actual Composition | Volume Ratio | Called Name | Basis of Name |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| ☕ **Coffee** | Water + Coffee extract | Water 98% : Coffee 2% | "Coffee" | Coffee, not water, determines identity |
| 🍵 **Tea** | Hot water + Steeping tea leaves | Water 99% : Tea 1% | "Tea" | Tea leaves determine taste and function |
| 🥛 **Almond milk** | Water + Almond extract | Water 97% : Almond 3% | "Milk" | Not mammalian milk, but similar appearance/usage |
| 🍷 **Ancient Wine** | Water + Wine extract | Water 75% : Wine 25% | **"Wine"** | Wine determines taste, sterilizing power, and function |
| 🧃 **Lemonade** | Water + Lemon juice + Sugar | Water 90%+ | "Lemonade" | Lemon determines identity |
| 🍺 **Beer** | Water + Fermented malt | Water 90~95% | "Beer" | Malt determines identity |

**Core Principle:** 98% of a cup of coffee is water, but no one calls it "water". 97% of almond milk is water, but it is called "milk". By exactly the same principle, although 75% of a cup of wine at an ancient table was water, it was called "wine". **The name follows identity, not volume.**

#### A Usage to Particularly Note: Modern Greek "Krasi (κρασί)"

The word for "wine" in modern Greek, **Krasi (κρασί)**, is derived from the ancient Greek **Krasis (κρᾶσις)**. The meaning of krasis is **"mixture"**. That is, to the ancient Greeks, the very word "wine" already meant **"a thing mingled (with water)"**. Modern Greeks no longer mingle water in their wine, but the name "mixture" remains as the standard word for "wine".

#### Similar Naming Customs within the Bible

| Bible Usage | Actual Composition | Called Name | Analysis |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|
| Ancient table wine | Water 75% + Wine extract 25% | **Wine (Yayin/Oinos)** | Core ingredient (wine) determines name |
| Wine mingled with myrrh (Mark 15:23) | Wine + Myrrh (anesthetic) | **Wine mingled with myrrh** | Distinguished by attaching a modifier to "wine" |
| Mixed liquor (Prov 23:30) | Wine + Spices/strong drink mixed | **Mixed wine (Mimsak)** | "Mixed" modifier to "wine" |
| Vinegar beverage (John 19:29) | Water + Oxidized wine (vinegar) | **Vinegar (Oxos)** | Originally wine, but separate name after oxidation |
| Honey water (Judg 14:18) | Honey + (Implicit) medium | **Honey** | Core ingredient is the name |
| Land of milk and honey (Exod 3:8) | The sum of various blessings | **Milk and honey** | Naming the whole by representative elements (Metonymy) |

#### ⚖️ The Conclusive Insight this Analysis Gives to the Wine Debate

> **"Since the 'wine' in the Bible is mingled with water, it is actually water, not liquor"** — This objection is automatically dismissed once the operating principle of metonymy is understood.

The reason the ancients called that beverage **"wine"** instead of **"water"** is because what determined the identity of that beverage was not water but **wine (the alcoholic component)**. Water is the background (diluent), and wine is the foreground (identity).

At the same time, the opposite extreme ("the wine of the Bible is strong drink like modern soju") is also dismissed. Ancient wine was drinking water of 2~3% ABV mingled 3:1, not entirely a completely different object from modern 12~40% liquor. It is like the difference between **the caffeine content of one cup of coffee and eating 30 caffeine pills**. Both are "caffeine", but the risk level is entirely different.

> **Conclusion:** The "wine" of the Bible is neither water nor strong drink. It is **"ancient daily drinking water safely mingled with water, while its identity remains wine."** The name follows the core ingredient (wine), but the actual form of consumption was a safely mingled state.
