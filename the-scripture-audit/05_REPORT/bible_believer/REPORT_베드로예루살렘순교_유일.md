**[The Broken Vow]**

In the upper room, at the place of the Last Supper, Peter speaks. *"I will lay down my life for thy sake"* (John 13:37). But that vow did not survive that same night. Yet the Lord already knew that failure that night. *"Thou canst not follow me now (νῦν); but thou shalt follow me afterwards (ὕστερον)"* (John 13:36). This was not merely a prophecy but protection. If Peter had followed in his fleshly vigor all the way to Calvary that night and died with Jesus, it would not have been a redemption, but would have ended as the meaningless death of a failed human. The Roman soldiers compel a passerby, Simon, a Cyrenian. *"and on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus"* (Luke 23:26). An unknown stranger, without even knowing it himself, fulfills those words with his body. Bearing the burden against his will, after Jesus, to that hill. The shadow of the path Peter would later walk alone was first cast upon the shoulders of a stranger.

**[From Him Who Girded Himself, to Him Who is Girded]**

Time passes. The young Peter always took the lead — drawing his sword, throwing himself into the water, girding himself and going whither he would on his own feet. But the Lord prophesied. *"when thou shalt be old... another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not"* (John 21:18). Just as Jesus Himself girded Himself with a towel at the Last Supper (John 13:4), Peter too finally walks that path not by his own strength, but by complete yielding. Faith that begins with active zeal being perfected in passive obedience — this is the path every disciple walks.

**[I Will Look Again]**

To Peter ministering in Babylon, the land where human pride began (Gen 11) (1 Pet 5:13), that call finally comes. The words Jonah prayed from the fish's belly come to mind — *"yet I will look again toward thy holy temple... I will pay that that I have vowed"* (Jonah 2:4, 9). The steps that stopped in the high priest's court, the vow he could not fully pay that night, finally continues and is completed at the place he could not go — the very place where the Lord, the true temple, tore Himself. Just as he who was delivered from the fish's belly paid his vow, he who was delivered from the mire of denial finally pays his vow with his body.

**[Not a Single Letter is Cast Away]**

νῦν and ὕστερον, σύ and ὅπου, φέρω — none of these were words placed by coincidence. In the Word recorded by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, there is no wasted syllable. The world tries to lump all this together into a single line, "he just was martyred," but every time a layer of the original text is peeled back, the cogs pointing to exactly one place are revealed.

Is there such a night for you too? Is there a place you wanted to follow but could not, a promise you vowed but failed to keep? Peter's story tells us — the Lord's "not now" is not an end but a protection, and a broken vow is not canceled but delayed. The steps that stopped that night will finally begin again "afterwards."
