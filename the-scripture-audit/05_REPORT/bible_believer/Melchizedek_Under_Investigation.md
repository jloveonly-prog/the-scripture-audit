## 4. Reconstructed Arguments

### 4-A. Internal Lexical Field of Hebrews 7 — A New Pillar [Strongest Argument]

The rebuttal that "Heb 7:8 is merely the silence of the scriptural record" is pierced by **the entire way the author uses 'die/live' within Hebrews chapter 7**.

| Verse | KJV | Subject |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 7:8 | "here **men that die** receive tithes; but there he receiveth them, of whom it is witnessed that **he liveth**" | Melchisedec |
| 7:16 | "after the power of an **endless life**" | Christ |
| 7:23 | "they were **not suffered to continue by reason of death**" | Levitical priests |
| 7:24 | "because he **continueth ever**, hath an unchangeable priesthood" | Christ |
| 7:25 | "seeing **he ever liveth** to make intercession" | Christ |

**Argument 1 — Identical Verb**: The "he liveth" of 7:8 and the "he ever liveth" of 7:25 are **the same Greek verb ζῇ**. In 7:25, this verb indisputably means **actual endless life** (since Christ's intercession is currently effective). It is the same chapter, the same argumentative flow, and the same verb.

**Argument 2 — Identical Syntax**:
- 7:3 Melchisedec: "**abideth** (μένει, present tense) a priest **continually** (εἰς τὸ διηνεκές)"
- 7:24 Christ: "**continueth ever**"

If εἰς τὸ διηνεκές is actual perpetuity for Christ but "recorded silence" for Melchisedec, the author would be **using the same expression in two different senses within an 8-verse interval**.

**Argument 3 — Argumentative Function**: The accidental omission of a record cannot support the theological conclusion that "Christ's priesthood is superior to Levi's". The argument of superiority established in Heb 7:7 ("without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better") demands a **real contrast**.

> **This is the independent basis for reading Heb 7:3 literally.** The original document leaned on 7:8 alone, but the reconstructed version leans on the entire lexical field of 7:3+7:8+7:16+7:23+7:24+7:25.

#### 4-A-B. ⚠️ Task ⑫ Resolved — The Absence of Record Theory Has the Answer (2026-07-30 4th)

§4-A argued that "the ζῇ of Heb 7:8 and the ζῶν of Heb 7:25 are the same verb and must therefore be read with the same force". Upon verifying whether the absence of record theory could explain this, **it can be explained. §4-A must be downgraded.**

**Syntactical Contrast**

| Verse | Greek | Structure |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Heb 7:8 | **μαρτυρούμενος ὅτι ζῇ** | "**being witnessed (passive participle)** — that he liveth" → "liveth" is a clause **subordinated under the verb of testimony** |
| Heb 7:25 | **πάντοτε ζῶν** | "ever living (active participle)" → **Direct statement**, no subordination |

**Core**: **The vocabulary (ζάω) in both places is the same, but the syntax is different.** 7:8 **wraps "liveth" in a content clause of testimony (μαρτυρέω)**, while 7:25 affirms it directly without wrapping. Commentary consensus points this out as well — "The author is not saying that Melchisedec continues to live, but that **the testimony about him is that 'he liveth'**" ([Precept Austin Heb 7:8-10](https://www.preceptaustin.org/hebrews_78-10)).

**Impact on §4-A**: §4-A relied on "same verb → same force". But the answer of the absence of record theory is that **even if the verb is the same, the force can change if the syntax is different**, and this answer is grammatically sound. Therefore, the argumentative power of §4-A substantially decreases.

**What Remains of §4-A**: 7:3's "μένει ἱερεὺς εἰς τὸ διηνεκές" (abideth a priest continually) is a **direct statement** not subordinated to a verb of testimony, and is syntactically equivalent to 7:24's "continueth ever". This part still remains a burden for the absence of record theory to explain — though with 7:8 removed, §4-A's lexical field argument is reduced from 5 points to **just two points: 7:3+7:24**.

**Downgrade: STRONG → VIABLE**

**Verdict on ⑫**: Resolved. And the resolution **favors the absence of record theory**. With §4-A thus weakened, the last STRONG argument supporting the hypothesis of this document has vanished.

#### 4-B-1. Targets and Results of Exhaustive Survey

The entire 3-phrase negation in Heb 7:3 was surveyed at the lexical level.

| Word | Strong's | Verified Usage | Nature |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---:|
| **ἀπάτωρ** (without father) | G540 | "one with no recorded genealogy/paternal history", "one whose father is unknown", "one born after the father's death". **Frequently used in this sense by classical authors** | Record/Registration |
| **ἀμήτωρ** (without mother) | G282 | "one whose mother's name is unknown", "one not recorded in Hebrew genealogical tables". 🔴 **Philo called Sarah ἀμήτωρ** — Sarah obviously had a mother, but there is no mention in the records | Record/Registration |
| **ἀγενεαλόγητος** (without descent) | G35 | "without **recorded** pedigree", "**unregistered as to birth**" — Vine's | Record/Registration |

Sources: [Strong's G35](https://biblehub.com/greek/35.htm), [Vine's Expository Dictionary — Genealogy](https://studybible.info/vines/Genealogy), [StudyLight G1076](https://www.studylight.org/lexicons/eng/greek/1076.html), [StudyLight Heb 7:3 Commentary](https://www.studylight.org/commentary/hebrews/7-3.html)

#### 4-B-2. Exhaustive Root Survey — ἀγενεαλόγητος Series

| Form | Strong's | Occurrence | Meaning |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| ἀγενεαλόγητος (Adjective) | G35 | **Heb 7:3 alone** (hapax legomenon) | unregistered in a genealogy |
| **γενεαλογέω** (Verb) | G1075 | **Heb 7:6** (passive) | to trace/calculate descent → "whose descent is not counted from them" |
| γενεαλογία (Noun) | G1076 | 1 Tim 1:4, Titus 3:9 | genealogy (Jewish genealogical records) |

**Crucial Observation**: The adjective in Heb 7:3 and the verb in Heb 7:6 are of **the same root** and separated by **3 verses**. Since the verb in 7:6 means "descent not counted from Levi" (a registry issue), the adjective in 7:3 also belongs to the same lexical field.

#### 4-B-3. This Is the Same Logic as §4-A [TYPE-P Self-Precedent]

| Logic | Application | Derivation |
|:---|:---|:---|

**If we adopt the logic of §4-A, we must also adopt the results of this survey.** Accepting only one side is committing the very "double standard" criticism that the original Masterpiece document hurled against the absence of record theory.

#### 4-B-4. Verdict and Scope of Impact

**Verdict**: There is **no lexical basis for reading the "without father, without mother, without descent" of Heb 7:3 as a literal ontology (directly created without parents).** All three words were commonly used in that era to mean "not in the records", and the instance of Philo using ἀμήτωρ for Sarah directly confirms this.

### 4-H. 🆕 Principled Non-Genealogy Theory — "Genealogies Are Necessary Because of Death" [2026-07-30 5th, §4-A Restored]

> **Background**: The 4th exhaustive survey (§4-B) confirmed that the three terms in Heb 7:3 are "absence of record/registration" terms, giving lexical evidence to the pure absence of record theory. However, this counterargument was raised — **"A genealogy (family tree) is inherently a mechanism necessary because of death. One who lives forever has no need for a genealogy. Therefore, Melchisedec's lack of a genealogy is not a coincidental omission of the record, but a principled result."** Upon verification, **this counterargument is established by the Bible's own logic.**

#### 4-H-1. Verification — The Bible Directly States "Genealogy = Succession Mechanism Due to Death"

**Step 1: Enrollment in a genealogy was a prerequisite for the Levitical priesthood**

| Verse | KJV |
|:---|:---|
| Neh 7:64 | "These **sought their register among those that were reckoned by genealogy**, but it was not found: therefore were they, **as polluted, put from the priesthood**" |
| Ezra 2:62 | (Same content) Those who could not find their genealogical record were **put from** the priesthood |

Source: [Nehemiah 7:64 KJV](https://www.bibleref.com/Nehemiah/7/Nehemiah-7-64.html), [Ezra 2:62 KJV](https://www.bibleref.com/Ezra/2/Ezra-2-62.html)

→ Genealogical registration was a **qualification requirement** for the Levitical priesthood, and its absence was grounds for **disqualification**.

**Step 2: Hebrews specifies the reason that requirement is necessary as "death"**

| Verse | KJV |
|:---|:---|
| Heb 7:23 | "they truly were **many priests**, because they were **not suffered to continue by reason of death**" |
| Heb 7:24 | "But this man, because he **continueth ever**, hath an **unchangeable** priesthood" |
| Heb 7:16 | "not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after **the power of an endless life**" |

→ **The reason there were many priests = death.** Because there is death, succession is necessary, and because succession is necessary, a genealogy is necessary. Hebrews directly states this causality.

**Step 3: That mechanism does not apply to Melchisedec**

Melchisedec is a priest without a genealogical registration (Heb 7:3, 7:6). According to Levitical regulations (Neh 7:64), he should be **disqualified**. Yet Hebrews does not disqualify him but presents him with a **superior priesthood**, placing its basis not on a genealogy but on **"the power of an endless life"** (Heb 7:16).

#### 4-H-2. Precise Distinction of Three Positions

This counterargument creates a new third position. Treating only two positions as being in conflict until now was an error.

| Position | Claim | Lexical Evidence (§4-B) | Argument Structure Evidence |

### 4-G. Unifying Principle — "One Maintained in the Status of Son" [Answer to Task ⑦, Verified 2026-07-30]

This is the answer to the rebuttal (former Task ⑦) that the 3-tier classification is an "ad hoc categorical division." **Arbitrary assignment is blocked because there are two constraining mechanisms.**

#### 4-G-1. Proposed Unifying Definition

> "Sons of God" is not a term pointing to a **kind of being (species)**, but a term pointing to **one who has been granted the status of a son of God (heirship) and is maintaining it**. Therefore, angels (servants, Heb 1:14) are not the object of this title, and even if the origins of existence are different (direct creation, descendants of Adam, born again), they can receive the same title.

**Consistency Check — Adam**: Luke 3:38 calls Adam "the son of God". Adam was made directly from dust (Gen 2:7) but received the status of a son. Namely, Scripture directly shows that **status, not origin, is the criterion for the title**.

#### 4-G-2. Constraint 1 — The subject of assignment is the text, not the analyst

| Tier | Point in Time Specified by Text | Basis for Assignment |
|:---|:---|:---|
| ① | "when I **laid the foundations of the earth**" (Job 38:4,7) | **Textual Specification** |
| Job 1:6/2:1 | During Job's life = Era of a real historical person post-Adam | Textual Context |
| ② | Genesis 6 = Just before the flood | Textual Context |
| ③ | New Testament, believing in Christ (John 1:12, Rom 8:14) | Textual Specification |

**Core**: The assignment to a tier is not chosen by the arguer but **fixed by the text's description of time.** Therefore, the evasion that "if a counterexample emerges, move it to another tier" is principally impossible — there is no discretion to move it.

#### 4-G-3. Constraint 2 — Jude 1:6 provides the condition for falsification ★

> **Jude 1:6 (KJV)**: "the angels which **kept not their first estate**, but **left their own habitation**, he hath reserved in everlasting chains"

Because Scripture **directly records a case of losing status**, the condition of "maintaining" is not empty. A falsifiable prediction derives from here:

> **Prediction**: Scripture does not call beings who have lost their status "sons of God" after the loss.

**Passed First Self-Test**: Job 1:6 says "the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, **and Satan came also among them**", syntactically separating Satan rather than including him among the "sons of God". Exactly as the principle predicted.

> ⚠️ **Anticipated Counterattack from BLUE**: "Satan coming 'among them' suggests he is a being of the same kind." → This counterattack depends on whether "among" means belonging or simple location, so separate verification is needed (Task ⑪).

#### 4-G-4. Auxiliary Verification — "Ate, therefore human flesh" is not established

A block against the rebuttal attempting to define Melchisedec as human by the fact that he brought bread and wine.

| Verse | KJV |
|:---|:---|
| Gen 18:8 | "he stood by them under the tree, and **they did eat**" |
| Gen 19:1,3 | "there came **two angels** to Sodom... he made them a feast... and **they did eat**" |

**Angels ate.** Therefore, the act of eating does not prove a human physical body. Additionally, Gen 14:18 does not even say Melchisedec ate (only up to "brought forth").

> ⚠️ **Double-Edged Warning**: This argument establishes that "non-human beings can also possess functioning bodies". This is also a **necessary premise when BLUE (Angel Theory) claims "angels could also take on flesh to reproduce"** in Genesis 6. It is valid in the Melchisedec context, but requires separate review when transferred to the Genesis 6 document.

#### 4-G-5. The Effect This Principle Has on F

BLUE's inference of "same phrase → same kind of being" operates only when the title is a **species name**. Once the definition of 4-G-1 is established, that inference is fundamentally blocked, and **at the same time it merges into a single argument with §4-C (Servant/Son axis) because it uses the same axis.**

**Rating: STRONG (Conditional)** — 2 constraints exist and falsification conditions are secured, but full canonical verification remains (Task ⑦-Remaining).

### 7-1. 4th Verdict — Conclusion Retreated, Byproducts Survived

**Core Conclusion ("Melchisedec = Son of God of Job 38:7") Rating: Conditional STRONG → 🔴 OPEN**

There is only one reason for the downgrade. The entire structure of this hypothesis depended on **literally reading Heb 7:3 as "directly created without parents"**, and as a result of the §4-B exhaustive survey, the **lexical basis for that reading was extinguished**:
- All three words ἀπάτωρ, ἀμήτωρ, ἀγενεαλόγητος were terms meaning "not in the record/registration" at that time
- Philo used ἀμήτωρ for Sarah, a real historical mother
- The root of ἀγενεαλόγητος is identical to Heb 7:6 γενεαλογέω (trace descent in a registry), at a 3-verse interval

If the absence of parents cannot be established, there is no basis to assign Melchisedec to the ① tier (direct creation), and if not assigned, no conclusion can be reached.
