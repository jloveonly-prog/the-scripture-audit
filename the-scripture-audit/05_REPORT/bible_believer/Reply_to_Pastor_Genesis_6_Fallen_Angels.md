## ✉️ Our Reply to the Pastor

Pastor, thank you for your answer and your precious words.  
Regarding the two points you raised—the interpretation of the "120 years" in Genesis 6:3 and the identity of the "sons of God" in Genesis 6—there may be some shortcomings on my part, but I have investigated in detail what the biblical text itself says and am sending this reply.

---

### First, the "120 years" in Genesis 6:3 is not a countdown to judgment.

The original text (KJV) of Genesis 6:3 is recorded as follows:
> "And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with man, for that he also is flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years."

The core expression in this verse is **"his days"**. The Hebrew original is **יָמָיו (yamav)**, which is clearly used throughout the Bible, such as in Psalm 102:11 or Job 10:20, to indicate the lifetime, that is, the lifespan of a being.

In other words, what God pronounced here is not the public announcement of a judgment schedule saying "the flood will come in 120 years," but **a sentence of life abbreviation stating "I will limit the lifespan of these beings to 120 years."**

If this 120 years were the "ark construction period" or a "countdown to repentance," it would mean that the Bible had already publicly announced the exact year and month of the judgment in advance. Being able to calculate the time of judgment by pre-determining a specific date and period seems akin to the so-called 'Rapture date-setting prophecies'. The 120 years is not a schedule for judgment, but a change in the lifespan of the sons of God who were meant to live forever.

Angels do not have a lifespan. They go straight to hell. They did not go to prison after their lifespan was reduced. If you believe in the re-creation and the Gap Theory, going to hell was an event that occurred in Genesis 1:2 and has absolutely nothing to do with Genesis 6.

---

### Second, the subjects in Genesis 6 are not "angels" but "sons of God". The Bible strictly distinguishes between these two categories.

The original Hebrew text of Genesis 6:2, 4 is **בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (Bene Ha-Elohim)**, which literally means **"sons of God"**.

The Hebrew word **מַלְאָך (malak)** etymologically means "errand runner, messenger", and the Greek word **ἄγγελος (angelos)** is likewise a functional title meaning "deliverer, messenger". That is, the word 'angel' itself refers to a function and office, not the essence of a being. On the other hand, **"son (בֵּן / ben)"** refers to an essential relationship and existential category, not a function. This difference is by no means trivial.

Viewing the sons of God in Job 38 as angels is the same error. An angel cannot be a son.

Hebrews 1:14 confirms this difference in a single verse.
> "Are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?"

According to this verse, the essence of an angel is summarized in two words: **"ministering"** and **"sent forth"**. This is the definition of a dispatched servant running errands, not a royal being ruling by their own will. A son inherits, and an angel is sent — the biblical distinction is this clear.

Jude 1:6 further confirms this.
> "And the angels which kept not their first estate (ἀρχή), but left their own habitation (οἰκητήριον)..."

The Greek word **οἰκητήριον (oikētērion)** for "habitation" here appears only twice in the entire KJV Bible (Jude 1:6 and 2 Corinthians 5:2). In 2 Corinthians 5:2, this word is used to refer to the resurrection body. What this means is that these beings in Jude did not simply "come down from heaven," but had their own domain of rule and home base.

In the case of Jude, their original status was the sons of God, but their status dropped as they left their habitation. They fell to the rank of angels. Because they had reproductive capabilities, they engaged in strange fleshly acts with the nations of that time. In 2 Peter, fleshly acts are not recorded. Therefore, the angels in 2 Peter and the angels in Jude are different. Just as the chains of darkness and the everlasting chains are different.

A messenger (angel) does not have a habitation of its own. Because the place the master sends them is their habitation. However, the beings in Genesis 6 and Jude 1:6 are beings who abandoned and left their own habitation on their own accord. This is a behavioral pattern of a royal ruler, not the act of a functional messenger.

---

### Third, angels have a body (sōma) but do not have reproducible flesh (sarx/basar).

This distinction is very important. In the Bible, "body" and "flesh" are not the same word.

* **Body:** Greek **σῶμα (sōma)** / Hebrew **גּוּף (guf)** — Form, substance, the physical form of a being
* **Flesh:** Greek **σάρξ (sarx)** / Hebrew **בָּשָׂר (basar)** — Biological organism made of blood and flesh (lineage of reproduction, growth, and death)

Angels can appear with a form. Hebrews 13:2 records a case of "entertaining angels while entertaining strangers." This is possible in the dimension of σῶμα (form). However, that does not mean they are basar (biological flesh) with reproductive capabilities.

Look again at the words God declared as the reason for judgment in Genesis 6:3.
> "for that he also is flesh (בָּשָׂר / basar)"

What God took issue with is the fact that these beings entered the lineage of **basar**, that is, flesh with biological reproductive capabilities.

Why this is important was directly declared by Jesus in Matthew 22:30.
> "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven."

In this verse, Jesus used the fact that "angels do not marry" as a standard of comparison to explain the state of resurrected saints. If angels actually married and gave birth in Genesis 6, this declaration by Jesus loses its basis.
