# BLUE — The "sons of God" are angels

> **Supporting Document**: Based on [[REPORT_Are_The_Sons_Of_God_Angels_Investigation.md]] (the definite article investigation results), the BLUE argument is developed.
> **Role**: Under the BVCAP MODE B rules, BLUE is obligated to faithfully present the best counterargument. The following constitutes the strongest case for the angel theory, constructed solely upon KJV internal evidence.

---

## 1. Job 1, 2, and 38 — The Weight of Identical Expression (TYPE-S)

In all three places—Job 1:6, 2:1, and 38:7—the "sons of God" appear as heavenly beings or attendees of a celestial council. As the investigation document confirmed, the English expression has "the" in all five verses, showing surface uniformity (§2). While it is true that the Hebrew difference regarding the presence or absence of the definite article (§3) is real, attributing that difference to "prose/poetry genre conventions" is merely an **assumption based on the general rules of grammar (Gesenius)**, not a declaration by the text itself that "these are different beings." If the absence of the definite article is completely explained by genre conventions, it remains open that Job 38:7 could still refer to the same kind of beings (a heavenly celestial council).

## 2. Job 1:6 Context — Satan Came "Among Them" (TYPE-C)

Job 1:6 KJV: *"Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them."*

The fact that Satan came "among them" is strong circumstantial evidence that this gathering is a regular assembly of heavenly spiritual beings (divine council). Traditionally, such a scene has been read as a royal court of angels. If Satan himself is a heavenly being who originally was a cherub (Ezek 28:14), there is a probability that the "sons of God" in the gathering he attends belong to the same category (heavenly spiritual beings).

## 3. Jude 1:6-7 — The Traditional Reading (TYPE-S)

Jude 1:6-7 KJV: *"And the angels which kept not their first estate... Even as Sodom and Gomorrha... going after strange flesh."*

The text explicitly calls these beings **"angels"**. RED's counterargument that "ἄγγελος is also used for men" (Mark 1:2, James 2:25) is a fact at the original language level, but an exhaustive verification of KJV translation precedents shows that the translators rendered this word as "angel" 179 times, and as "messenger" only 7 times (and those are extremely rare exceptions where it clearly refers to men) (Re-verification results of `Spiritual_Beings_Criteria_Table_Estimate.md` §1). In other words, the KJV English text itself treats this word overwhelmingly as an exclusive term for supernatural beings — RED's argument that "it is not an angel but merely a functional word indicating a demoted status" is not conclusive under the principle of KJV literalism based on the original language alone.

## 4. Counterargument Regarding Matt 22:30 — "Neither Marry" is a Comparison of Current State, Not Capability

Matthew 22:30 ("angels do not marry") used by RED compares the **current state in heaven** of the resurrected saints with the angels, and does not directly state the biological proposition that "it is fundamentally impossible for angels to physically marry and reproduce." If the event of Genesis 6 actually took place (since the text actually records that "the sons of God... took them wives... and they bare children to them"), this means the text itself testifies that union and procreation occurred in this specific event, and Matthew 22:30 does not explicitly exclude the possibility of an exception for this particular event.

## 5. Conclusion (BLUE's Best Argument)

Synthesizing the expressive uniformity of Job 1, 2, and 38 (§1), the royal court context of Job 1:6 (§2), the explicit designation of "angels" and the KJV translation precedents in Jude (§3), and the limited scope of application of Matt 22:30 (§4) — the traditional reading that views the "sons of God" in Genesis 6 as angels (heavenly spiritual beings) possesses textual evidence that cannot be ignored.
