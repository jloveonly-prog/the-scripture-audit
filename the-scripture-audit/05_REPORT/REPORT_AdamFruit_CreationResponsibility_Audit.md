## Phase 2: TYPE-G (Grammar/Context Anatomy) — Precise Analysis per Verse

### Verse 1: Isaiah 46:10 KJV

> *"Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure"*

**Context (Isa 46:9-11)**:
- 46:9 — "I am God, and there is none like me"
- 46:10 — My counsel shall stand
- 46:11 — **"Calling a ravenous bird from the east"** ← This is the core.

**"A ravenous bird from the east"** = The plan to judge Babylon through Cyrus (King of Persia).

This verse does **not mean "God planned every daily choice of individual humans."** It is a declaration about **the historical plan of judgment and salvation that God Himself will execute.** The "My" in "My counsel" is God, and the content of that plan in context is **"the judgment of Babylon through Cyrus."**

**TYPE-G Verdict**: An **error of context hijacking** that ignores the context and unjustly broadens "all things" to "every individual human choice." ✅

---

### Verse 2: Ephesians 1:11 KJV

> *"In whom also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestinated according to the purpose of him who worketh all things after the counsel of his own will"*

**Context (Eph 1:3-14 as a whole)**: This paragraph speaks of **"the plan of salvation in Christ."**
- 1:4 — "he hath chosen us **in him** before the foundation of the world"
- 1:5 — "Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ"
- 1:7 — "In whom we have redemption **through his blood**"

The "all things" in **"who worketh all things"** refers to the **context of Ephesians chapter 1 entirely = the plan of salvation**. It does not mean "God decided even what an individual human will eat tomorrow."

Furthermore, **"in him"** is the core:
- What God predestined was not **"specific individuals" but the category of "those in Christ"**.
- **The act of entering into Christ = Faith = Human choice** (John 3:16 "believeth").
- TYPE-C (Category Separation): **"Predestination of a plan"** ≠ **"Predestination of individuals"**.

**TYPE-G Verdict**: Unjustifiably broadening the scope of "all things" from the context (plan of salvation) to "all human choices". ✅

### Verse 3: Proverbs 16:9 KJV

> *"A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps."*

This verse is rather **evidence of free will**:

```
Sentence Structure Analysis:
   Subject 1: "A man's heart"
   Verb 1: "deviseth" ← Human act of devising = Presupposes free will

   Subject 2: "the LORD"
   Verb 2: "directeth" ← Directing ≠ Controlling

Core: "Direct" is not "force".
   → A navigation system "directs" the route, but whether to follow it is the driver's choice.
   → God provides direction, but does not replace human choice.
```

**TYPE-G Verdict**: This verse does not support predestination, but is evidence of compatibilism showing the coexistence of **human capacity to plan (free will) + God's direction (sovereignty)**. ✅

## Phase 2: TYPE-R (Detecting Subject/Syntax Confusion)

Judges 1:19 KJV:

> *"And the LORD was with Judah; and **he** drave out the inhabitants of the mountains; but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron."*

**Core Analysis: Who is the antecedent of "he"?**

```
Sentence Structure:
   Clause 1: "the LORD was with Judah"
             → Subject: the LORD / Verb: was / Prepositional phrase: with Judah
             → God was with Judah

   Clause 2: "he drave out the inhabitants of the mountains"
             → Subject: he / Verb: drave out

   Clause 3: "but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley"
             → Subject: (he — omitted) / Verb: could not drive out

Grammatical Verdict:
   → In English grammar, the antecedent of "he" is the nearest singular masculine noun.
   → Structure of Clause 1: "the LORD was with Judah"
   → "with Judah" — Judah is the topical focus of the sentence.
   → "he" = Judah (the tribe of Judah).

   → Additional evidence: If "he" were God after "the LORD was with Judah,"
     it would mean "God was with them but God couldn't drive them out."
     → This self-negates the meaning of "being with" in the same sentence.
     → In Hebrew narratives, "the LORD was with" is an idiomatic expression for help/sponsorship,
       not meaning that the LORD is the direct subject of the battle.
```

**Parallel Verse Cross-Verification:**

| Verse | KJV Original Text | Structure |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Judg 1:22 | "the house of Joseph... and the LORD was with **them**" | The LORD "with" = sponsorship. The acting subject is the house of Joseph. |
| Josh 14:12 | "if so be the LORD will be with **me**, then **I** shall be able to drive them out" | Caleb's words: If the LORD is with me, then "**I**" shall be able to drive them out — Acting subject = Caleb. |

**TYPE-R Verdict**: "he" = Judah. God was not pushed back by iron chariots; rather, **the tribe of Judah** failed to drive out the inhabitants of the valley. Misreading of the subject confirmed. ✅
