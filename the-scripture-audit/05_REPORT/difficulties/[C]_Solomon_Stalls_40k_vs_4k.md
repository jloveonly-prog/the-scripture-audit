## 3. Functional Category Separation Verification: The Two Numbers Point to Different Things (A-16)

### Reconstruction of Solomon's Chariot Military System

In **1 Kings 10:26**, the number of Solomon's chariots is specified:
> *"And Solomon gathered together chariots and horsemen: and he had a thousand and four hundred chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen"*

**1,400** chariots → This is the basic combat unit.

**Stall Calculation per Unit:**

| Classification | Description | Expected Quantity |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Operational Unit Stalls (2 Chron 9:25)** | A combined facility that stores 1 chariot + the trained horses assigned to that chariot together. Based on 1,400 chariots, estimated deployment of 3~4 chariot units per stall | **4,000** |
| **Overall Horse Accommodation Stalls (1 Kings 4:26)** | Total number of individual accommodation stalls for all horses across the kingdom. Includes all foals, mares, horses in training, reserve horses, transport horses, and cavalry horses | **40,000** |

### Figurative Understanding: Contrast with Modern Military Systems

```
4,000 in 2 Chron 9:25 ← Like "4,000 fighter squadrons" (Combat unit grouping aircraft + pilots + maintenance team together)
40,000 in 1 Kings 4:26 ← Like "40,000 aircraft hangars" (Total sum of all aircraft accommodation facilities nationwide)

The same military power can be counted in two different ways.
```

### Demonstration in the Movie Ben-Hur: Ancient Chariot Military Management
- In ancient chariot warfare, the horses assigned to each chariot must **train together**.
- Horses pulling the same chariot must live in the **same space** and become accustomed to each other.
- Horses not suitable as chariot horses are **reclassified for load carrying or cavalry use** and moved to other stalls.
- Therefore, the number of operational unit stalls (2 Chron) and the total number of individual horse stalls (1 Kings) **must be different.**

## 6. Refuting the Copyist Error Theory: Copyological Review (A-02)

The attacker's claim is "one 0 was left out due to a copyist error." However:

- **Masoretic Text**: The numbers in 1 Kings 4:26 and 2 Chron 9:25 are consistently maintained respectively.
- **Septuagint (LXX)**: Records 1 Kings 4:26 as 40,000.
- **Samaritan Pentateuch**: Verse does not exist.
- **If it were a copyist error**: If 1 Kings 4:26 were originally 4,000, one 0 would have to be added to become "40,000," but there is no explanation for how this error occurred in the ancient Hebrew copying tradition. Hebrew numbers are written in an alphabetical system, so the error method of leaving out a single 0 like Arabic numerals does not apply.

## 7. Final Verdict

> The 40,000 in 1 Kings 4:26 and the 4,000 in 2 Chron 9:25 are numbers pointing to facilities of different functional categories. The fact that all extant manuscripts maintain the two numbers respectively supports that this is an intentional record, not an error.
