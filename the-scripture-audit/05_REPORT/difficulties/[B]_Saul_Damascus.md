## 1. Establishing the Point of Conflict (PHASE 1: Dissecting the Verses)

### The Core Argument of the Attackers
> "Acts 9:7 says they heard a voice but saw no man. 22:9 says they saw the light but heard not the voice. They are exactly the opposite. Paul's claim of meeting Jesus is a lie and he is giving contradictory statements."

## 2. The Key to the Solution: Jesus Spoke Twice (Sequential Parallel Reading)

> **Core Insight**: The two verses are not recording the same moment.
> On the road to Damascus, Jesus spoke **two times with different contents**,
> and what the companions **heard/did not hear was different each time.**

### Actual Sequence of Events: Fitting the Two Records in Chronological Order

```
[Phase 1] A light from heaven shines round about Saul (Acts 9:3 = 22:6)
         → Companions: See the light and are afraid (Acts 22:9 first half)

[Phase 2] Jesus speaks in a voice audible only to Saul
         "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?" (Acts 9:4 = 22:7)
         → Companions: Do not hear this voice (Acts 22:9 second half)

[Phase 3] Saul answers
         "Who art thou, Lord?" (Acts 9:5 = 22:8)
         → Companions: Sounds like Saul talking to himself (Can be witnessed)

[Phase 4] Jesus speaks to Saul a second time, audible only to him
         "I am Jesus. it is hard for thee to kick against the pricks" (Acts 9:5 = 22:8)
         → Companions: Do not hear this voice either (Acts 22:9)

[Phase 5] Saul speaks
         "What shall I do, Lord?" (Acts 22:10)

[Phase 6] Jesus now commands in a voice audible to all
         "Arise, and go into Damascus!" (Acts 9:6 = 22:10)
         → Companions: They hear this voice (sound)! But they see no man speaking
         → This is the scene of Acts 9:7 "hearing a voice, but seeing no man" ✅

[Phase 7] Saul is blinded and led by the hand into Damascus (Acts 9:8)
         → The men remember the voice they just heard commanding to "go into Damascus" and lead Saul
```

### ✅ Integrated Sequential Reading (Reading the Two Records Together)

> *"As Saul journeyed and came near Damascus, suddenly there shone from heaven a great light round about him. And he fell unto the ground, and heard a voice. 'Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?' — **They that were with him saw indeed the light, but they heard not this voice.** (Acts 22:9) Saul answered, 'Who art thou, Lord?' And he said, 'I am Jesus whom thou persecutest.' Saul answered again, 'What shall I do, Lord?' And the Lord said unto him, 'Arise, and go into Damascus!' — **This time the men which journeyed with him stood speechless, hearing a voice, but seeing no man.** (Acts 9:7)*"

## 3. Original Language (KJV) Verification: Analysis of the Meaning of "Voice/φωνή" (V-03, V-05)

The attackers primarily exploit the ambiguity of the translated word "sound" in the **Korean Revised Version**.

| Classification | Korean Revised Version | KJV | Greek Original Language |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Acts 9:7 | "hearing only the sound" | *"hearing a voice"* | **φωνήν** (phone-n, accusative) |
| Acts 22:9 | "did not hear the voice" | *"they heard not the voice"* | **φωνήν** (same word) |

*   The Greek word **φωνή (phōnē)** means "voice, sound, utterance."
*   The identical Greek word is used in both verses. This confirms that it is not "a different kind of sound" but refers to **the different voices of Jesus spoken at different points in time**.
*   The KJV faithfully translates both as *"a voice / the voice"*, distinguishing through context that the two voices refer to different times of utterance.

## 5. Pointing Out the Problems of the Korean Revised Version (V-03 KJV Contrast)

One of the reasons attackers can raise this difficulty is the problem of **translation condensation** in the Korean Revised Version.

*   In Acts 22:9 of the Standard KJV (Korean translation), the phrase *"And they that were with me saw indeed the light, **and were afraid**"* is included.
*   The Korean Revised Version abbreviates this context of "being afraid," making it difficult to know that the companions were in a state of being overwhelmed by the light.
*   When read with the KJV and Standard KJV, **the state of the companions (falling down in fear of the light → not hearing Jesus' first voice / hearing Jesus' commanding voice after Saul's conversation)** is revealed much more clearly.

## 6. Final Verdict

### ✅ CONSISTENT — Consistency Confirmed

> **Reason for Verdict**:
> Acts 9:7 and 22:9 are not contradictory records. The two verses record **different points in time** where Jesus spoke different contents twice during the road to Damascus event. 22:9 records the early part of the event, where the companions did not hear the first voice ("Saul, Saul...") that was heard privately only by Saul. 9:7 records the latter part of the event, where the companions heard the second command ("go into Damascus") that was audible to everyone, but did not see the one speaking. When the two records are pieced together in chronological order (sequential parallel reading), a single, complete narrative is formed without any contradiction. This insight rather proves that the Bible is not merely a flat record from a single perspective, but preserves the details of various viewpoints three-dimensionally.
>
> **Core Counter-Argument Logic**:
> If the two records were truly a lie or a contradiction, the same author (Luke) would not have recorded exactly opposite contents as they are in chapters 9 and 22 of the same book (Acts). The very existence of this 'tension' is rather evidence of the authenticity of the records.
>
> **Confirmation of the Bible's Three-Dimensionality**:
> This difficulty is not a flaw in the biblical records, but evidence of the **three-dimensional narrative structure of the Bible** that is only revealed when the two records are integrated and read sequentially. The detailed phases of the event, which cannot be captured by a single record, are completely restored through the cross-referencing of the two records.
