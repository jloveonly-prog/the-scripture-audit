## 3. Genealogical Telescoping Technique (A-03)

In ancient Hebrew genealogical literature, the word 'sons' (ben) does not simply mean direct biological sons. It is frequently used as a term encompassing 'descendants belonging to him' or 'all members belonging to his household'.

Chronicles had to **compress (Telescope)** the epic history of all of Genesis into short scenes. The primary readers of Chronicles were Jews who were already perfectly familiar with Genesis.

- Genesis Explanation: "Timna was a concubine and she bare Amalek."
- Chronicles Compression: "...Kenaz, and Timna (the concubine), and Amalek (her son)."

The author wasn't wrong because he didn't know the explanation in Genesis, but **on the premise that the readers already knew Timna was a concubine and Amalek was her son**, he simply omitted the explanation "concubine" and compressed them together into a household list (the red group). This is not an error, but highly advanced 'narrative telescoping'.
