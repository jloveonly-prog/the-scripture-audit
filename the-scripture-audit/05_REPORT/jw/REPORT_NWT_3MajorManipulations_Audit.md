## Verification Item 1 — John 1:1: Deviation from Mainstream Academic Interpretation

### Verse Comparison

| Translation | John 1:1 (Last Clause) |
|:---|:---|
| **Greek Original** | *θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος* |
| **KJV (1611)** | *"the Word was **God**"* |
| **Revised New Korean Standard** | "the Word was **God**" |
| **Saebunyeok** | "the Word was **God**" |
| **NIV, ESV, NASB...** | *"the Word was **God**"* (All identical) |
| **New World Translation** | "the Word was **a god**" |

### 🔴 Academic Judgment: Greek Interpretation and Translation Status

In the Greek original text, *θεὸς* (theos) is used as a **Predicate Nominative** in this position.

> ⚠️ **Precise Grammatical Summary**: Greek originally does not have an indefinite article ('a'). Anarthrous (without article) nouns can be interpreted in three ways depending on the context: ① Definite, ② Qualitative (nature), ③ Indefinite (Colwell's Rule, Harner's Rule).
> Mainstream New Testament academia interprets θεὸς in John 1:1c as **"Qualitative"**, meaning *"the Word was one with the nature of God"*.
> The absolute rule that "if it's anarthrous, it must be 'a god'" does not exist. NWT's Indefinite interpretation is a minority position significantly different from the academic mainstream.

**Scholars' Judgment:**

> Greek Scholar **Julius Mantey** (when the NWT cited his research):
> *"Do not use my research as a basis for this translation. This translation is neither scholarly nor reasonable."* (Protest letter exists)

> **Overwhelming majority** of New Testament Academia: Among thousands of translations worldwide, the **New World Translation is almost the only one** that translates it as "a god".

### 💡 Core Point of Dispute — Double Standard Applied Only in Jesus' Declaration of Deity

**NWT's Defense**: *"Because there is no article, 'a god' is correct."*

> ⚠️ **Caution**: The claim that "the NWT translates anarthrous theos as 'a god' ONLY in John 1:1" is **not true**.
> In Acts 28:6 (what the Maltese said looking at Paul), John 10:33 (Jews criticizing Jesus), etc., the NWT also translates anarthrous theos as 'a god'.
> This false claim can destroy the credibility of the entire report with a single counterexample. **Do absolutely not use it.**

**Correct Counterattack — Stubbornly adhering to a minority academic interpretation only in the context declaring Jesus' deity**:

> "The problem with the NWT is not 'how to translate anarthrous theos'.
> The problem is selectively applying a minority academic interpretation **only in the context declaring Jesus' deity**.
>
> For example, look at John 20:28, where Thomas confessed to the resurrected Jesus:
> 'My Lord and my God (ὁ θεός μου)'
> The NWT also translates this verse as 'my God'.
>
> But why is 'the Word was God (θεὸς)' in John 1:1 translated as 'a god'?
>
> The anarthrous theos in Acts 28:6 (a scene where pagans call Paul a god) is correctly 'a god'.
> The anarthrous theos in John 10:33 (a scene where Jews criticize Jesus) can also contextually be 'a god'.
