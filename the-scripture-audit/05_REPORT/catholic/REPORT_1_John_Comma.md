## 1. Setting the Problem — The Reality and Limits of Manuscript Numbers

### 1-1. The Tripartite Structure of Manuscript Families — Overall Status

The New Testament manuscript tradition must be understood as a **tripartite structure** consisting of 2 Greek families + 1 Latin family.

| Family | Language | Representative Manuscripts | Comma Inclusion | Current Usage |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Alexandrian Text-Type** | Greek | Sinaiticus (א), Vaticanus (B) | ❌ Absent | NA28/UBS5 → NIV, ESV, NASB, Korean Revised Version |
| **Byzantine / Majority Text (TR)** | Greek | Majority of minuscules (~80-90%) | ❌ **Mostly Absent** | KJV Base (Comma is present in ~8 manuscripts only) |
| **Latin Vulgate** | Latin | Jerome's Translation (382-405 AD) | ✅ **Included** (Spread since the Middle Ages) | Official Catholic Bible |

> **The Latin Vulgate is an independent Western Latin tradition, neither Alexandrian nor Byzantine.**  
> Jerome's original Vulgate did not have the Comma, but during the medieval Latin copying process, it gradually entered through the African Latin tradition (Priscillian, ~380 AD), and was confirmed as the official Catholic canon at the Council of Trent in 1546.

### 1-4. Why the Catholic Church Pressured Erasmus

Erasmus (1469-1536) was a Catholic theologian and humanist. When he published his Greek New Testament in 1516 and excluded the Comma, the Catholic side immediately applied pressure for three reasons:

1. **Threat of the Collapse of Vulgate's Authority**  
   The Latin Vulgate, the official Bible of the Catholic Church, contained the Comma. Its absence in Erasmus' Greek text implied that *"there is an error in the Vulgate."* This was a direct challenge to the authority of the Church.

2. **Disappearance of the Trinitarian Proof Text**  
   The Comma ("the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one") was the core verse with which the Catholic Church defended the doctrine of the Trinity. With its omission, critics attacked Erasmus on suspicion of being an **Arian (a heretic denying the Trinity)**.

3. **Sparks of the Reformation (1517)**  
   It was the very moment Luther's Reformation was beginning. They feared Erasmus' Greek text would become a weapon for reformers opposing the Catholic Church.

**Core Irony — Erasmus and GA 61:**
```
Erasmus 1516: 1st edition published — Excludes Comma because it's absent in Greek manuscripts
Erasmus 1519: 2nd edition published — Still excludes Comma → Strong Catholic backlash
Catholic side: Presents GA 61 (Codex Britannicus) (creation suspicions exist)
Erasmus 1522: 3rd edition — Inserts Comma (while expressing personal doubts about the manuscript's reliability)
Result: Catholic pressure to protect their Latin Vulgate leads to inclusion in TR → KJV
```
> ⚠️ **Academic Correction:** Traditionally, an anecdote has widely circulated that Erasmus made a promise saying, "If you show me a single manuscript, I will insert it." However, according to H.J. de Jonge's research (1980), **documentary evidence of this promise exists nowhere in Erasmus' writings.** Bruce Metzger also retracted this "rash promise" anecdote in his later editions. Current corrected academic consensus views that Erasmus inserted it in the 3rd edition due to Catholic pressure and consistency issues with the Vulgate.  
> In other words, **the Catholic Church pressured Erasmus to protect its own Latin Vulgate**, and as a result, the Comma was included in the KJV via the TR—a paradoxical history.  
> Calling this passage a "Catholic insertion" is a misunderstanding of historical causality.

### 1-7. Recognizing the Limits of the Manuscript Number Debate

The formula "More manuscripts = Closer to the original" **does not hold true.** Here is why:

- A large number of manuscripts simply means **that text was copied many times**, not that it is **closer to the original**.
- The small number of Alexandrian manuscripts is because papyri were physically preserved due to Egypt's dry climate, not because the Alexandrian tradition was a minority.

> **⚠️ Strategic Conclusion:**  
> Greek manuscripts containing the Comma **do exist** (GA 629, GA 61, GA 918, etc.).  
> However, they are few in number and reliability debates exist for each manuscript.  
> Therefore, the front line of apologetics must shift **from the "battle of manuscript numbers" to "internal textual evidence."**  
> **Manuscripts are supporting evidence; the text's internal structure is the primary evidence.**

### 1-9-A. Old Latin Versions (Vetus Latina) — Latin Bible Before the Vulgate

**Old Latin versions (Vetus Latina, "Old Latin Bible")** is a collective term for Latin Bible translations used in North Africa and the Western Church during the 2nd to 4th centuries, before Jerome's Vulgate appeared.  
It is not a single translation but consists of **multiple early Latin translations that varied by region**.

> ⚠️ **Important Distinction:** The fact that the "Old Latin version tradition" has existed since the 2nd century means that **translation activities began in the 2nd century**.  
> **Extant physical manuscripts (copies)** containing the Comma exist only **after the 5th century**.  
> Evidence for the Comma in the 2nd-3rd centuries is confirmed only through **quotations within Patristic writings** (Tertullian, Cyprian).

```
Greek Original Text (1st Century)
    ↓
Old Latin Version translation begins (2nd Century~) ← Start of an independent early Latin translation tradition
    ↓
Patristic Quotation Evidence: Tertullian ~200 AD / Cyprian ~250 AD ← Quotations in theological works, not manuscripts
    ↓
Oldest Extant Latin ms with Comma: 5th Century~ (Speculum, etc.)
    ↓
Jerome's Vulgate (382-405 AD) ← Revises and unifies Old Latin
    ↓
Medieval Vulgate spread → Officialization at Council of Trent (1546)
```

#### Comma-Related Testimony (*Against Praxeas*, Ch.25, ~200 AD)

**Core Latin Original Phrase:**
```latin
"Ita connexus Patris in Filio et Filii in Paracleto,
 tres efficit cohaerentes, alterum ex altero.
 Qui tres unum sunt, non unus."
```
*(Translation: Thus the connection of the Father in the Son, and of the Son in the Paraclete, produces three coherent Persons, who are yet distinct One from Another. **These Three are one [essence]**, not one [Person].)*

**English Translation (ANF Standard):**
> *"Thus the connection of the Father in the Son, and of the Son in the Paraclete, produces three coherent Persons, who are yet distinct One from Another. **These Three are one [essence], not one [Person]**, as it is said, 'I and my Father are One,' in respect of unity of substance not singularity of number."*

#### BVCAP Evidence Grade Evaluation

| Item | Evaluation |
|:---|:---|
| **Date** | ~200 AD — More than 180 years before the Vulgate |

## 2. Core Evidence 1 — Greek Chain Editing (Heaven/Earth Contrast)

This is the most powerful argument that bypasses the manuscript number debate.

### 2-1. Greek Comparison of 1 John 5:7-8: TR vs. Alexandrian Manuscripts

**TR (Textus Receptus) Text:**

```greek
[Verse 7] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ,
          ὁ Πατήρ, ὁ Λόγος, καὶ τὸ Ἅγιον Πνεῦμα·
          καὶ οὗτοι οἱ τρεῖς ἕν εἰσι.

[Verse 8] καὶ τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῇ γῇ,
          τὸ πνεῦμα, καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ, καὶ τὸ αἷμα·
          καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
```
→ Verse 7: **ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (in heaven)** / Verse 8: **ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)** — A perfect contrasting pair

**NA28 / UBS5 (Alexandrian Critical Text):**

```greek
[Verse 7] (Deleted)

[Verse 8] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες,
          τὸ πνεῦμα καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ καὶ τὸ αἷμα,
          καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
```
→ In verse 8, **ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth) is also deleted**

### 2-2. Core Discovery — The Alexandrian Tradition Did Not Just Delete Verse 7

> **When Alexandrian scribes deleted Verse 7 (Comma), they also had to remove "ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)" from Verse 8.**

The logical implication of this fact:

1. **Why delete "in earth" from verse 8?**  
   The locational modifier "in earth" **only makes sense when contrasted with "in heaven" (verse 7)**.  
   If verse 7 is deleted leaving only "in earth," the reader naturally asks: *"What about in heaven?"*  
   They removed "in earth" to prevent this question.

2. **What this means:**  
   This is circumstantial evidence that Alexandrian scribes themselves recognized that "ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (in heaven)" and "ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)" were **structurally designed as a pair in the original text**.  
   They recognized the structural rupture caused by deleting verse 7, and altered verse 8 to patch it up.

3. **Conclusion:**  
   Because the "heaven/earth" contrasting structure of verses 7 and 8 is original, removing one caused a chain edit requiring the removal of the other. **This chain editing itself proves the existence of the original structure.**

### 2-3. Structural Visualization

```
【 Original TR Structure (Internally Complete) 】
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Verse 7  ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ  (in heaven)  → 3 witnesses in heaven: Father, Word, Holy Ghost
          ↕ Contrast
 Verse 8  ἐν τῇ γῇ      (in earth)   → 3 witnesses in earth: Spirit, water, blood
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Heaven and earth, above and below form a perfect rhetorical symmetry.

【 Structure After Alexandrian Editing (Collapse of the Contrasting Pair) 】
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Verse 7  (Deleted)
 Verse 8  (ἐν τῇ γῇ also deleted)  → 3 witnesses: Spirit, water, blood
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 "In earth" vanishes, and the locational contrast itself disappears. Verse 8 alone
 does not even raise the question, "Why specifically limit the location?"
 Alexandrians deleted "in earth" because they knew this question would arise.
```

## 5. Core Evidence 4 — Structural Resonance with Genesis 1:26-27

### 5-1. Grammatical Structure of Genesis 1:26-27

Verses 26 and 27 are an inseparable theological unit:

- **Verse 26 (Planning):** "Let **us** make man in **our** image, after **our** likeness..."  
  → God's plural pronouns ("us", "our") — A joint declaration of the Trinity
- **Verse 27 (Execution):** "So God **created** man in his own image, in the image of God **created** he him; male and female **created** he them."  
  → **"created" (Hebrew: בָּרָא, bara) is repeated exactly 3 times.**

### 5-2. 3 Repetitions of 'bara' — Rhetorical Analysis

In Hebrew prose, the threefold repetition of the same verb is a **Climactic Repetition**,  
where the subject of creation declared as "us (Trinity)" in verse 26 is expressed as a **threefold creative act** in verse 27.

```
Genesis 1:26  "us / our"    → Declaration of plural creative subject
Genesis 1:27  bara × 3      → Threefold emphatic creative act
                   ↕ Structural Resonance between Old and New Testaments
1 John 5:7    3 Witnesses   → Father, Word, Holy Ghost
```
