## 2. [TYPE-C] Functional Category Verification of Biblical Text (John 21)

**Catholic Claim**: Jesus telling Peter to "feed my sheep" in John 21:15-17 is an commissioning ceremony of absolute power (the papacy) to rule the global church.

**[BVCAP Reverse Calculation and Anchor Cross-Verification]**
We must apply the biblical timeline to ask why Jesus repeated the exact same question, "Lovest thou me?", to Peter specifically **'Three times'**.
- **3rd Anchor (Matthew 26:75)**: Just before the crucifixion, Peter **denied Jesus exactly three times** with oaths and curses, claiming he did not know Him.
- **Functional Separation (TYPE-C)**: The conversation in John 21 is not a 'papal coronation,' but a **prescription for spiritual restoration and reinstatement** directed at the disciple who most miserably betrayed his Master. Transforming an event of 'pastoral compassion'—covering three betrayals with three confessions of love to heal a sinning disciple's wounds—into a 'delegation of supreme power' is a severe distortion of context (C-12).
- **Peter's Own Testimony (1 Peter 5:1-4)**: Peter himself did not call himself the supreme ruler (Pope) but humbled himself as "who am also an elder," explicitly stating that the only supreme authority is Jesus Christ, the "Chief Shepherd."

## 3. [TYPE-N] Verification of Exclusive Monopoly (Luke 22)

**Catholic Claim**: The verse in Luke 22:32, "But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not... strengthen thy brethren," guarantees Peter's unique infallibility (error-free nature) and special primacy.

**[BVCAP Collapse of Exclusivity]**
- This prayer is not a special privilege given because Peter was 'greater' than the other apostles. Look at the very next verse (Luke 22:34). "the cock shall not crow this day, before that thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me."
- Jesus promised this 'prayer of restoration' because He knew that in the impending severe trial of Satan (sifting as wheat), Peter would be **the person to fail and fall most thoroughly**. Using a prayer of compassion predicated on failure and betrayal as the basis for Infallibility and absolute governing power is a logical self-contradiction.

## 4. [TYPE-D] Cross-Verification of the Blind Spots in the Historical Tradition of the Church Fathers

Catholics use the writings of early Church Fathers as evidence of papal authority in Rome, but when 'cherry-picking' (extracting only favorable passages) is excluded and the full context is examined, the claim collapses.

1. **Cyprian's Paradox**
   - Catholics claim he called Rome the 'Chair of Peter' and the 'principal church' (Epistulae 59,14).
   - **Counter-evidence**: Looking at chapter 4 of Cyprian's major work, *On the Unity of the Catholic Church*, he clearly declares, **"The other apostles were also endowed with an equal partnership both of honour and power as Peter."** Crucially, in the issue of the rebaptism of heretics, he directly rejected the order of Stephen I, Bishop of Rome, and opposed him by convening an African council. This is clear historical evidence that the 'absolute primacy' or 'infallibility' of the Bishop of Rome was not recognized at all in the 3rd century.
2. **The Essence of 1 Clement and Irenaeus**
   - The actions of Clement sending a letter to the Corinthian church or Irenaeus using the Roman church as the standard of orthodoxy were acts of respecting the symbolic leadership of Rome as the 'spiritual eldest brother' church, the capital of the empire where the two apostles Peter and Paul were martyred. Interpreting the presentation of the best-preserved church at the time as a 'standard' to fight 2nd-century Gnostic heresy into an 'absolute, infallible papal power' where a specific office legally controls the whole world transcending time, is a later embellishment.

## 6. 📖 Easy Technical Summary (LESSON-6: Technical Summary in Plain Korean)

The contents of this Part 2 are also easily explained in everyday language with three key points.

**① [Context Restoration] The words to feed the sheep were not a letter of appointment, but medicine for wounds.**
Catholics interpret Jesus asking Peter "Do you love me?" three times by the Sea of Galilee as a 'papal appointment ceremony.' However, Peter was a man who cursed and denied Jesus three times before the cross. Receiving three confessions of love from a disciple who betrayed Him three times was a 'tearful wound treatment' event, stitching up his torn conscience and reinstating him as an apostle so he could work again. Packaging this as a delegation of power is a severe violation of the text's emotion and purpose.

**② [Collapse of Exclusivity] The reason he received the prayer was because he would be the one to fall the hardest.**
Catholics use the scene where Jesus says, "Peter, I have prayed that your faith may not fail," as evidence of Peter's unique authority (infallibility). However, the very next line is a terrible prediction: "Today you will deny me three times." In other words, this prayer was not a medal given because Peter was the greatest Pope, but a 'spiritual first-aid kit' left in advance for the pitiful disciple who would fail most miserably in the upcoming trial.

**③ [Cross-Verification] The actual actions of the Church Fathers deny the absolute power of the Pope.**
Catholics cherry-pick phrases where early Church Fathers (e.g., Cyprian) praised the Roman church and use them as evidence for papal authority. But if you turn back the history books just a bit, when the Roman Pope (Stephen I) issued an order, Cyprian directly disobeyed, saying, "All apostles are equal!", and held his own regional council. If there had been a rule back then that 'the Pope is unconditionally right and everyone must obey,' this could not have happened.

It is true that in the early church era, the Roman church located in the capital of the empire played the role of a respected older brother. However, inflating the respect for a great older brother into an 'infallible papal power' that legally rules the global church and is never wrong is merely an exaggeration created by history.
