# Why Catholics Cannot Confess Jesus as Savior
## — The Difference in Soteriology Revealed by a Single Yes/No Question —

---

## Introduction: Why This Question is Core

An interesting thing happens when you ask a Catholic the following question:

"Do you believe and accept that you are a sinner, and that Jesus, who is God, bled, died, was buried, and resurrected in your place for those sins? Please answer only with Yes or No."

On the surface, this question is simple. It seems like any Christian should be able to answer "Yes". However, trained Catholics find it extremely uncomfortable to respond with a solitary "Yes/No".

This phenomenon is not simple evasion. The structural characteristics of Catholic soteriology are revealed precisely in front of this single sentence.

---

## Chapter 1. Theological Implications Within This Question

There are five theological statements contained within this short question.

First, the expression "I am a sinner" presupposes that humans cannot stand before God on their own.

Second, the expression "in place of those sins" is the concept of substitutionary atonement, that Jesus paid the price for my sins.

Third, "Jesus, who is God" is a confession explicitly acknowledging the divinity of Jesus Christ.

Fourth, the expression "bled, died, was buried, and resurrected" contains the exact core facts of the gospel spoken of in 1 Corinthians 15:3-4.

Fifth, the question "Do you believe and accept" presupposes that this salvation is received through personal faith.

To answer "Yes" alone to this question is to simultaneously acknowledge that Jesus' death and resurrection are sufficient for my salvation, that I have accepted it by faith, and that salvation is established by that alone. This is the core of Protestant "Sola Fide (Faith Alone), Solus Christus (Christ Alone)".

---

## Chapter 2. Why Catholics Cannot Answer with a Single "Yes"

### The Structure of Catholic Soteriology

Catholic salvation is a structure where multiple channels operate together. Based on the Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC), it is as follows.

Catholicism teaches that faith alone is insufficient for salvation. Baptism is necessary (CCC 1257: "Baptism is necessary for salvation"), the Eucharist is necessary (quoting John 6:53), reconciliation after mortal sin through the Sacrament of Penance is necessary (CCC 980), merits must be accumulated (quoting James 2:24), there can be a purification process in Purgatory after death (CCC 1030), and the intercession of Mary and the saints is also allowed (CCC 969). All of these combined complete salvation.

### The Problem Brought by a Solitary "Yes"

Within this structure, answering with a solitary "Yes" brings serious implications. The moment one answers "Salvation is completed by believing and accepting Jesus," all the sacraments, Purgatory, and Marian intercession listed above become unnecessary. This shakes the entirety of the soteriology taught by Catholicism.

---

## Chapter 3. Three Choices and Their Outcomes

A Catholic essentially has only three choices when faced with this question. And all three are difficult.

### Choice 1: Answering "Yes"

It becomes an acknowledgment that salvation is completed by Jesus' single sacrifice. Hebrews 10:12-14 says He has "perfected forever" with "one sacrifice." If so, why is the daily Mass (re-presentation of the sacrifice) necessary? Why is purification in Purgatory necessary? Why is Mary's intercession necessary? Why is absolution of sins through Penance necessary? If these are "necessary" for salvation, it conflicts with Hebrews 10:14, and if "unnecessary," it becomes self-contradictory with the Catechism.

### Choice 2: Answering "No"

It becomes a declaration: "I do not believe and accept that Jesus died and resurrected for my sins." This is a denial of the core Christian confession of 1 Corinthians 15:3-4, placing oneself outside of Christianity. Realistically, a Catholic cannot answer this way.

### Choice 3: Attaching a Condition like "Yes, but..."

Since the question explicitly asked to "answer only with Yes or No," it becomes a rule violation. In this case, the questioner simply points out, "I asked you to answer only with Yes or No."

### Summary

| Choice | Theological Outcome | Outcome in Practice |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Yes | Additional requirements of Catholic soteriology become unnecessary | Exposed to Hebrews 10:14 + Galatians 1:8 argument |
| No | Denial of core Christian confession | End of debate |
| Yes, but... | Attempted conditional escape | Loss of credibility due to rule violation |
| Silence or Evasion | — | Impression of "Cannot answer a simple question" |

## Chapter 4. The Completeness of Salvation — Differences Between Protestantism and Catholicism

### Protestant Position — Hebrews 10:12-14

"But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God... For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified."

Based on this passage, Protestantism teaches that Jesus' single sacrifice makes salvation permanently perfect. Therefore, no additional sacrifices, additional works, or additional processes are needed, and receiving that perfect salvation by faith is everything.

### Catholic Position — CCC and James 2:24

Catholicism views Jesus' sacrifice as having acquired the "merit" of salvation, but teaches that the "application" of that merit to individuals is accomplished through Baptism (initial application), the Eucharist (continuous nourishment), Penance (restoration after mortal sin), Purgatory (post-mortem purification), and the intercession of Mary and saints (auxiliary mediation). Therefore, because "believing and accepting Jesus" alone does not activate these channels, acceptance of salvation is not completed with a single "Yes."

## Chapter 5. What Does the Bible Say?

The following are key biblical passages related to this topic.

Ephesians 2:8-9 says, "For by grace are ye saved through faith... Not of works," supporting salvation by faith alone.

John 3:16 says, "that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."

Romans 10:9 says, "That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved."

Hebrews 10:18 says, "Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin," raising questions about the daily re-presentation of Jesus' sacrifice in the Mass.

1 Timothy 2:5 says, "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus," raising questions about Mary's intercessory prayer.

James 2:24 says, "Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only," which is the verse Catholicism cites when emphasizing works.

## Chapter 6. How to Use in Conversation — After Receiving a "Yes"

The moment the other person answers "Yes," proceed to the following steps.

### First — Confirm Completeness of Salvation (Heb 10:14)

"You said 'Yes', right? Hebrews 10:14 says, 'For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.' If Jesus' one offering has made me 'perfected forever', why is the daily Mass (re-presentation of the sacrifice) necessary?"

### Second — Confirm Once-for-all Sacrifice (Heb 10:18)

"'Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.' It says there is 'no more' offering for sin. How does the daily re-presentation of Jesus' sacrifice in the Mass align with this verse?"

### Third — Confirm the Only Mediator (1 Tim 2:5)

"'For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.' If there is 'one' mediator, how does Mary's intercessory prayer align with this verse?"

### Fourth — Warning of Another Gospel (Gal 1:8)

"'But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.' Isn't adding Purgatory, Marian intercession, and merits to Jesus' perfect sacrifice adding 'another' thing to the gospel of 1 Corinthians 15:3-4?"

## Chapter 7. Opponent's Counterattacks and Responses

### Counterattack 1: "James 2:24 — Faith without works is dead"

James chapter 2 is not adding works as a condition for salvation, but stating that works appear as evidence of living faith. The "faith" in James 2:14 refers to a dead intellectual assent (an empty confession without works), while the "faith" in Ephesians 2:8 refers to living trust (saving faith). The two authors used the same word with different meanings. Looking at the example of Abraham in James 2:21-22, he was accounted righteous by faith in Genesis 15:6, and the works in Genesis 22 appeared as evidence of that faith.

### Counterattack 2: "The CCC does not say Jesus' sacrifice is insufficient"

Then are Baptism, sacraments, and Purgatory essential for salvation or not? If they are essential, it implies that Jesus' sacrifice alone is insufficient. If they are not essential, it is a self-contradiction with CCC 1257 ("Baptism is necessary for salvation").

### Counterattack 3: "Sacraments are means of salvation, not additional conditions"

Then can one be saved without sacraments? If yes, sacraments become unnecessary for salvation, and Catholic soteriology collapses. If no, sacraments become an essential requirement for salvation, conflicting with Ephesians 2:8.

## Chapter 8. Why This Question is Important

The moment one answers "Yes," the entire structural system of Catholic soteriology — Baptism, sacraments, penance, Purgatory, Marian intercession — becomes biblically unnecessary. This is not a matter of personal faith, but a structural problem of the Catholic doctrinal system.

This was the core reason the Reformation (1517) occurred, and Luther's declaration of "Sola Fide" is the point of complete separation from Catholicism.

## Practical Application Example — What Happened in an Actual Conversation

Below is a transcript of an actual conversation using this method, with questions, answers, and corrections. The meaning and logic are maintained, but reorganized.

---

The questioning method was set simply.

"Do you believe that Jesus bled, died, and resurrected for my sins, and have you accepted Him as your Savior? 1 for Yes, 2 for No. Please answer only with numbers."

The opponent answered as follows.

"I also say Yes, and all 1.3 billion Catholics around the world also answer Yes. You cannot set a trap in a place where we already agree."

And immediately launched two counterattacks.

---

### Opponent's Counterattack 1: Romans 6:3-4 — The argument that baptism is a sacrament

Opponent's claim:

"You asked if I accepted Him as my Savior, right? What does the Bible call that acceptance? Romans 6:3-4. 'Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death...' Paul directly nails the act of accepting the cross to the sacrament called baptism. Mr. Protestant, you were also baptized, right? Then you have also already accepted the cross through a sacrament. The fight between faith or sacrament doesn't stand from the beginning."

Rebuttal to this:

Let's look at Acts 10:44-48. "While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word... Then answered Peter, Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?" Look at the order. Receiving the Holy Spirit comes first. Receiving baptism comes later. Salvation is first, and baptism is later. Romans 6 does not say baptism gives salvation, but that the salvation already received (union with Christ) is publicly declared through baptism. Baptism is the result of salvation, not the cause of salvation.

---

### Opponent's Counterattack 2: James 2:24 — The Bible says it is not "by faith alone"

Opponent's claim:

"The expression 'faith only' appears in exactly one place in the entire Bible. James 2:24. And that verse says 'Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.' Mr. Protestant, do you believe this Bible verse? If you say yes, you must explain how you will reconcile the Protestant doctrine of faith alone with this verse; if you say no, you, who cry out for the Bible, must explain why you deny a single Bible verse."

Rebuttal to this:

Yes. I believe James 2:24. But James 2:19 must be read together. "The devils also believe, and tremble." The faith James attacks is demon-level faith. It is a dead faith that has only intellectual assent and the life does not change. In James 2:18, when he says "I will shew thee my faith by my works," the object of "shew thee" is people. James is speaking of proving faith before people, not justification before God. Paul speaks of justification before God in Romans 3:28. The two men used the same word "faith" in different contexts. Galatians 5:6 expresses it as "faith which worketh by love," and Paul also agrees that living faith appears as life.

---

### Two Things Confirmed in This Response

First, the number '1' was not used.

I requested a single number, but a long paragraph returned. They said "Yes" but never wrote "1" to the end. Why was just one letter so difficult? If "Yes" was truly safe, it would have ended with "1. Next question?". Transitioning immediately to a counterattack means they know the logical repercussions a solitary "Yes" brings.

Second, right after "Yes," they immediately attached "through sacraments."

Saying "Because faith connects to the cross through sacraments," they attached the sacrament condition right after saying "Yes." It is not a solitary "Yes," but "Yes, but sacraments." In this moment, they unknowingly revealed the structural problem.

## Catholic Eucharist and the Problem of John 6 Interpretation

If the opponent continues to maintain the claim that "we are saved through sacraments," we can examine the Eucharist, the core of the 7 Catholic sacraments, directly with the Bible.

"Since you say sacraments are important, shall we analyze just the Eucharist? Let's read John 6, which Catholicism cites as the biblical basis for the Eucharist, from beginning to end."

---

### First — Jesus defined "eating" first (John 6:29, 35)

In verse 29, Jesus said, "This is the work of God, that ye believe on him whom he hath sent." In verse 35, He said, "he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst."

Coming is eating, and believing is drinking. Before the word "must eat flesh" in verses 53-55, Jesus already defined it as "eating = coming = believing".

### Second — Jesus Himself attached an explanation (John 6:63)

Verse 53 says, "Except ye eat the flesh (σάρξ, sarx)... ye have no life in you." But verse 63 says, "It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh (σάρξ, sarx) profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life."

The core is that "flesh (eat)" and "flesh (profiteth nothing)" are the exact same word in Greek (σάρξ, sarx).

Reading it the transubstantiation way creates a self-contradiction. Saying "you must eat my σάρξ for eternal life" in verse 53, and "σάρξ is unprofitable" in verse 63, it ends up being Jesus saying "you must eat to live" earlier, and "that is unprofitable" later, with the same word.

Reading it spiritually removes the contradiction. "Eat my flesh (σάρξ) for eternal life" = "Believe and accept me, Jesus, for eternal life," and "Flesh (σάρξ) is unprofitable. My words are spirit and life" = "The physical act of chewing and eating is unprofitable. Receive my words spiritually." Verse 63 is Jesus Himself directly explaining the meaning of verses 53-55 in a spiritual sense.

### Third — What the disciples found "hard" was not eating flesh (John 6:60-62)

What the Jews already found difficult in John 6:41-42 was: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? how is it then that he saith, I came down from heaven?" The stumbling block was Jesus' claim of divinity, not the method of eating flesh.

In John 6:62, Jesus' response upon hearing the saying was "hard" was, "What and if ye shall see the Son of man ascend up where he was before?" He did not explain how to eat. He brought up "What if I ascend to heaven?". What was difficult was believing Jesus' divinity, not eating flesh.

In conclusion, "Will ye also go away?" (verse 67) is a question of "Will you believe my divinity?", not a question of "Will you eat flesh?".

### Fourth — Jesus has a pattern of explaining parables Himself (John 12:32-33)

After saying "And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me," He immediately explained it Himself with His death on the cross. John 6:35 is the same pattern; He already explained eating as believing.

### Fifth — "Will ye also go away?" is not maintaining realism (John 6:67)

What the disciples said was hard was the claim of divinity (verse 60). Verse 67: "If you will not accept this divinity, then you go too" is maintaining the divine revelation, not physical eating.

All five of these are in the same chapter. John 6 speaks of "belief" from beginning to end. Transubstantiation brings out only verses 53-55 without reading verses 35 and 29.
