## ⚔️ Case 1: Transubstantiation — "Does the bread really turn into flesh and blood?"

### 🔴 Arguments by the Prosecution (TheScriptureBeliever)

**[Argument 1] Terminology Clarification First — Transubstantiation = Change of Substance, the same word**

Catholics retort that "화체설 (transubstantiation) is an incorrect expression and 실체변화 (change of substance) is correct," but these two are identical concepts. The official Catholic Latin Transubstantiatio, English Transubstantiation — when translating into Korean, Protestants simply call it '화체설', and Catholics call it '실체변화'. Only the terms are different; the meaning they point to is identical.

**[Argument 2] The Official Interpretative Key Given Directly by Jesus — John 6:63**

> "It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, **they are spirit, and they are life**." (John 6:63)

If one uses "eat my flesh" in John 6:53 as the basis for transubstantiation, they must acknowledge that Jesus Himself provided the official interpretative key in verse 63 of the very same chapter, saying, "The words I have spoken are spiritual." Skipping this key and interpreting only verse 53 literally is ignoring Jesus' own right to interpret His words.

**[Argument 3] Internal Contradiction of the 'Essential Flesh' Claim — The Beef Analogy (TYPE-C)**

The Defense's logic: "It does not change into physical flesh and blood, but into essential flesh and blood."
Applying this to everyday life:

Someone points to a piece of bread and says, "This is real beef." You look at it under a microscope and there is no protein, no muscle fibers, and no blood. When you ask, "I thought you said it was beef?" they reply, "Physically it is bread, but the unseen essence has changed into beef."

If it looks, tastes, and has the components of bread, but you say "the essence is beef," then ultimately, that is a **symbol**. Claiming "it is not a symbol" while actually using symbolic language is an internal contradiction.

**[Argument 4] Reductio ad absurdum — Did the 5,000 loaves of the miracle also become flesh? (TYPE-AI)**

In the miracle of the five loaves and two fishes in Matthew 14:19, Jesus took the loaves, looked up to heaven, prayed (gave thanks), and brake the loaves to give to them in the **exact same way**. The structure is identical to the Last Supper.

Did those loaves that 5,000 people ate all turn into the 'essential flesh' of Jesus? Even Catholics would say "No." Then **why selectively apply transubstantiation only to the bread of the Last Supper?** This is a lack of consistency.

**[Argument 5] The Metaphor of the Cup and the Covenant — Luke 22:20 (TYPE-G)**

> "This **cup** is the new **testament** in my blood" (Luke 22:20)

Taken literally, the metal cup itself must undergo an essential change into a 'covenant (contract)'. Did the metal cup turn into a contract? No. Everyone knows this is a literary metaphor. "This is my body" in the same setting and context is an identical literary expression.

Jesus also said, "I am the **door** of the sheep" (John 10:9) and "I am the true **vine**" (John 15:1). Applying magic exclusively to the bread and wine is **selective literalism**.

**[Argument 6] The Jewish Passover Context — The blessing is not magic (TYPE-AA)**

The prayer Jesus made while holding the bread was the traditional prayer of thanksgiving (**Berakhah**) that the head of the household offers to God the Father during the Jewish Passover meal (Seder).

**"Baruch atah Adonai Eloheinu Melech ha-olam, ha-motzi lechem min ha-aretz"**
— "Blessed art Thou, O Lord our God, King of the universe, who bringest forth bread from the earth."

The object of the prayer was **'God the Father', not the 'bread'**. Reinterpreting this traditional prayer of thanksgiving as a magic spell that changes the essence of the bread is a product of medieval philosophy that completely ignores 1st-century Jewish culture.

The fact that it is a holy ordinance accompanied by prayer does not change the bread into the flesh of the Creator. It is the same principle that the water in the baptismal font does not turn into Jesus' blood even though a solemn prayer is offered during baptism.

### 🔵 Rebuttal by the Defense (Catholic)

**[Defense 1]** John 6:53-56 is a literal command, and the disciples also understood it literally and were shocked (verse 60). The shock is circumstantial evidence that it is a literal command, not a spiritual metaphor.

**[Defense 2]** The Last Supper is different from the feeding of the 5,000. It is followed by the special institution declaration, "This is my body (Hoc est corpus meum)," and the command, "This do in remembrance of me."

**[Defense 3]** Transubstantiation utilizes Aristotelian philosophy's concepts of Substance (Substantia) and Accidents (Accidens); the outward appearance (accidents) is bread, but the substance (essence) is the flesh and blood of Christ. It is not a physical change.

### ⚖️ The Judge's Verdict — Case 1

> **Verdict: ✅ CONSISTENT (Prosecution Wins) — Transubstantiation has weak biblical backing**

1. **Self-Refutation**: Jesus immediately provided the spiritual interpretative key in verse 63. To use the shock (verse 60) as a basis for literal interpretation, one must also acknowledge verse 63 which corrected that shock.
2. **Undefeated Reductio ad absurdum**: Not applying transubstantiation to the identical structure of the feeding of the 5,000 while selectively applying it to the Last Supper — Lack of consistency.

In the context of 1 Cor 3:12-13, it is explicitly stated that the fire shall try every man's **"work (ergon, ἔργον)"**. **Replacing ergon (work of ministry) with psyche (soul) is a distortion of the text.**

> "Let us therefore come **boldly (directly)** unto the throne of grace," (Heb 4:16)

**[Argument 6] 1 Corinthians 15:29 — The True Meaning of 'Baptized for the Dead' (TYPE-G Original Language Analysis) (Rebuttal to anticipated claims by liberal theologians active in Catholic circles)**

Catholicism and some cults use 1 Cor 15:29, "which are baptized for the dead," as a basis for Purgatory or communicating with the dead. However, we must look at this verse accurately starting from the words.

**First, the original text is 'immersion', not 'sprinkling'.** The Greek **βαπτίζω (baptizo)** means 'to be completely immersed in water', not sprinkling water on the head. Even Jesus came up out of the water in the Jordan River, He did not receive water sprinkled on His head.

> "he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire:" (Matt 3:11)

| Type of Baptism | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| Water Baptism | Being immersed in water as an outward sign of repentance |
| Holy Spirit Baptism | The Holy Spirit coming upon the believer |
| Fire Baptism | Judgment, that is, the hell of fire |

**Third, the core of 1 Cor 15:29 is 'because of'.** It does not mean they were baptized "**on behalf of** the dead," but rather they are baptized "**because of** the dead." Water baptism? Holy Spirit baptism? Or fire baptism?

**The very next verse confirms this.**

> Verse 30: "And why stand we in jeopardy every hour?"
> Verse 31: "I die daily."

Verse 29 is about persecutors who killed the saints receiving the fire baptism (judgment) **because of** those martyrs (the dead), and verses 30-31 speak of the suffering of the persecuted saints. **If there is no resurrection, then neither the judgment of the persecutors nor our suffering has any meaning — this is Paul's argument.**

Using 1 Cor 15:29 as a basis for Purgatory or communicating with the dead is a misreading that completely ignores the context.
