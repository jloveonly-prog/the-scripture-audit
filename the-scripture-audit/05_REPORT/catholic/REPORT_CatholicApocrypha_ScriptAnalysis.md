# Catholic Apocrypha Script — Anti-Catholic Doctrine Rebuttal Report

---

### [TARGET 01] Rebuttal of Mary's Intercession

**Catholic Claim**: Mary can act as a mediator and pray on our behalf.

**Rebuttal Arguments**:

1 Timothy 2:5 states, "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus." The mediator is only one.

Mary is a creature and a finite being. Hearing the prayers of hundreds of millions of people worldwide simultaneously presupposes the ability of omnipresence (being everywhere at once). Omnipresence is an attribute exclusive to God. Endowing the creature Mary with this attribute is to deify her.

The incident at the wedding at Cana (John 2), where Mary mentioned the lack of wine, is used as a basis for intercession, but Jesus' response was, "Woman, what have I to do with thee?" (John 2:4). This is not an acknowledgment of Mary's intercession.

### [TARGET 02] Rebuttal of Apostolic Succession

**Catholic Claim**: The Pope and bishops have directly inherited the authority of Peter and the apostles.

**Rebuttal Arguments**:

A prerequisite for an apostle is to have been an eyewitness who saw and met the resurrected Jesus directly (Acts 1:21-22). The current Pope and bishops do not meet this condition.

Matthias became an apostle as the fulfillment of a prophecy in the Psalms (Acts 1:20, Psalm 109:8). The current Catholic apostolic succession is not based on the fulfillment of biblical prophecy.

Apostolic signs (miracles, healing) had already disappeared by the end of Paul's life. In 1 Timothy 5:23, Paul prescribed to the sick Timothy to "use a little wine," rather than performing a miracle. The miraculous powers of the early apostolic era do not appear in the later epistles.

### [TARGET 03] Rebuttal of Peter as the Rock and First Pope Theory

**Catholic Claim**: In "Upon this rock I will build my church" (Matthew 16:18), Peter (the rock) is the foundation of the church and the first Pope.

**Rebuttal Arguments**:

In John 1:42, Jesus gave Simon the name "Cephas." In Greek, Cephas means a small stone. The "rock (petra)" in Matthew 16:18 is a different word meaning a large boulder. Petros (small stone), meaning Peter, is distinguished from petra (large boulder), meaning rock.

1 Corinthians 10:4 states explicitly, "and that Rock was Christ." The true rock is not Peter, but Jesus Christ.

Ephesians 2:20 says, "Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone." Peter alone is not the foundation of the church, and Jesus is the supreme cornerstone.

**Catholic Claim**: Peter was martyred by being crucified upside down in Rome, and this supports the legitimacy of the Roman Papacy.

**Rebuttal Arguments — The Babylon Dilemma**:

If Babylon in 1 Peter 5:13 is interpreted symbolically as Rome, then the harlot Babylon, which is eternally destroyed in Revelation 18, also becomes Rome. Rome is still standing today. Interpreting Babylon symbolically as Rome creates a self-contradiction. Conversely, if Babylon is interpreted literally as Mesopotamian Babylon, the conclusion is that Peter was never in Rome. Either interpretation is unfavorable to Catholicism.

**Rebuttal Arguments — Place of Martyrdom**:

In John 13:36, Jesus said, "whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow me afterwards." In 21:18, He said, "and carry thee whither thou wouldest not." The place Peter wanted to go the least was Calvary in Jerusalem, where he had denied Jesus three times.

In John 12:33, "what death" is the Bible's own explanatory phrase indicating death by crucifixion. If Peter's "what death" means the same, then Peter was martyred at Calvary.

### [TARGET 05] Rebuttal of Infant Baptism

**Catholic Claim**: There are no explicit verses prohibiting it in the Bible, and it is a gift of prevenient grace that washes away original sin, thus supporting infant baptism.

**Rebuttal Argument 1 — The Logical Fallacy of Permitting through Silence**:

The logic that "you can do it because there is no verse saying not to" is inherently dangerous. There are no explicit sentences in the Bible forbidding the establishment of a Pope, the sprinkling of holy water, or bowing to a statue of Mary. There are also no sentences forbidding smoking or doing drugs. Does that mean it is permissible to do so?

To declare that "since it is not forbidden, we will do it by tradition" regarding something God has not commanded is the very tradition of the Pharisees that Jesus rebuked (Mark 7:13).

Women's participation in communion is based on Galatians 3:28 and 1 Corinthians 11:28. Infant baptism alone has absolutely no textual basis. It is different from other traditions.

**Rebuttal Argument 2 — The Absolute Order of Biblical Baptism**:

Without exception, the New Testament follows the order of "Hearing the Gospel → Personal Faith and Repentance → Baptism."

- Mark 16:16 — He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved
- Acts 2:41 — Then they that gladly received his word were baptized
- Acts 8:36-37 — If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest

Infant baptism directly reverses this absolute order.

**Rebuttal Argument 3 — 1 Peter 3:21**:

The Bible defines baptism as "the answer of a good conscience toward God." How can a newborn baby, who is incapable of even rational judgment, respond with a conscience?

**Rebuttal Argument 4 — Precise Reading of Mark 16:16**:

"He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned."

The condition for receiving eternal punishment is not "he that is not baptized," but "he that believeth not." If baptism were a condition for salvation, it should have been written, "he that is not baptized shall be damned." In the Bible, both the presence and absence of words have meaning.

### [TARGET 06] Rebuttal of the Doctrine of Purgatory and the Apocrypha

**Catholic Claim**: Based on the Book of Maccabees (Apocrypha), one can go to heaven after being purified in Purgatory. 1 Corinthians 3:15 ("saved; yet so as by fire") is also evidence of Purgatory.

**Rebuttal Argument 1 — The Issue of the Canonicity of the Apocrypha**:

The Book of Maccabees is an apocryphal book not recognized as canonical by the Jews. Jesus never quoted from the Apocrypha when quoting the Old Testament. The KJV 1611 also distinguishes it from the canon and classifies it as Apocrypha. Basing doctrine on uncanonical books undermines the very authority structure of the Bible.

**Rebuttal Argument 2 — The Dichotomy in John 3:16**:

John 3:16 declares that only two states exist: "perish (hell)" and "everlasting life (heaven)." There is no third place (Purgatory) in the Bible.

**Rebuttal Argument 3 — The Blockade of Hebrews 9:27**:

"And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). The doctrine that there is an opportunity for post-mortem purification (Purgatory) after death directly clashes with this verse.

**Rebuttal Argument 4 — Exposing the Misreading of 1 Corinthians 3:15**:

The fire in 1 Cor 3:15, which Catholicism uses as the basis for Purgatory, is not the fire of Purgatory that cleanses souls. Looking at the context (verses 12-13), that fire is the fire of judgment that tests **the results of ministers' ministries (works)**. It is not the minister's own soul burning in the fire, but the fruit of his ministry being judged.

**Rebuttal Argument 5 — Exposing the Misunderstanding of Paradise (Abraham's Bosom)**:

Catholicism misunderstands the "bosom of Abraham (Paradise)," the underworld where Old Testament saints stayed after death, as Purgatory. However, from Jesus' words to the thief on the cross, "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" (Luke 23:43), we can see that Paradise is not a place of torment in Purgatory, but a place of immediate blessing and fellowship.

#### Changes in Moral Law According to Dispensations — The True Meaning of "Seemingly Contradictory Biblical Records"

There are verses that perplex first-time Bible readers. Catholicism and some critics attack these as contradictions within the Bible. However, these are not contradictions but **evidence that God's laws were applied differently according to the dispensation**.

**① Adamic Age — Only One Prohibition**

The prohibition God gave to Adam was only one: "But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it" (Genesis 2:17). In this age, there was no law against incest. Adam's children had no choice but to marry each other, and this was not a sin. Where there is no forbidden law, there is no transgression (Romans 4:15: "for where no law is, there is no transgression").

**② Abrahamic Age — Marriage to a Half-Sister**

Abraham married his half-sister Sarah (Genesis 20:12: "And yet indeed she is my sister; she is the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother"). If this were after the implementation of the Law of Moses, it would have been a crime punishable by death according to Leviticus 18. However, in Abraham's time, that law did not yet exist. God did not condemn Abraham, but rather accounted his faith for righteousness.

**③ Abrahamic Age — Polygamy**

Abraham had Sarah, Hagar (Genesis 16), and a concubine Keturah (Genesis 25:1). Jacob had four wives: Leah, Rachel, Bilhah, and Zilpah. The fact that the qualifications for an elder and a deacon in the New Testament are specified as "the husband of one wife" (1 Timothy 3:2) proves that this standard was only applied in the New Testament era.

**④ Jacob's Son Reuben — Sleeping with His Father's Concubine**

Genesis 35:22: "And it came to pass, when Israel dwelt in that land, that Reuben went and lay with Bilhah his father's concubine." The record calmly describes this fact. Reuben was not put to death immediately. If this event had occurred in the Mosaic era, he would have been executed according to Leviticus 20:11 ("And the man that lieth with his father's wife... shall surely be put to death"). However, that law did not yet exist.

**⑤ Mosaic Age — Detailed Laws Finally Appear**

It was only in the Mosaic age, through Leviticus and Deuteronomy, that hundreds of laws were given, including prohibitions against incest, restrictions on polygamy, dietary laws, and festival laws. Things that were permitted in previous ages were forbidden in this age.

**⑥ New Testament Age — Fulfillment of the Law**

Galatians 6:15: "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but a new creature." Even Old Testament circumcision became ineffective in the New Testament.

> **Conclusion**: God is the same, but **the content of the response He required from people differed in each dispensation.** The claim that "the Bible is contradictory" comes from the error of not knowing this principle of division (2 Timothy 2:15) and lumping the words of all dispensations into a single doctrine. Catholicism's mixing of Old and New Testament verses as the basis for salvation by works is precisely this error.

- The context (John 20:19-23) connects to receiving the Holy Spirit after the resurrection: "Receive ye the Holy Ghost... Whose soever sins ye remit"—this is a declaration of forgiveness of sins through preaching the gospel (if they believe, they are forgiven; if they do not believe, the sin remains), not an administrative delegation of authority for a priest to forgive an individual's sins in a confessional.
- The Third Anchor (Acts 2:38): When Peter preached on Pentecost teaching the method of forgiveness of sins, he said, "Repent, and be baptized every one of you **in the name of Jesus Christ**." He did not mention confession to a priest.

> **Conclusion**: The forgiveness of sins is an authority that belongs exclusively to God (Mark 2:7), and the mediator is Jesus Christ alone (1 Timothy 2:5). The priest's right of absolution through confession has no biblical basis. If you go directly before God and confess your sins in faith, you will be forgiven (1 John 1:9).
