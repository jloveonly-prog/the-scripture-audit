# Genesis 1 — The Primeval World and Traces of Judgment

---

## 1. The Scene of Creation Viewed from Heaven

Genesis 1 begins with a landscape looking down from heaven. In that scene, the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep.

Waters covered the earth, and what's notable here is that 'waters' is recorded not in the singular but in the plural form. The Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters (two or more).

God moved because that state was not normal. After the movement ceased, God spoke. "Let there be light" — This was the first action to divide the light from the darkness.

The darkness was the evening, and the first day began by dividing the light.

---

## 2. "Called" — 6 Days of Reorganization and Naming

It is necessary to distinguish when God newly made something and when He granted a name to something that already existed in Genesis 1. The key clue is the word **"called"**.

| Day | Object | New Name | KJV Verb |
|----|------|---------|---------|
| Day 1 | Light | Day | **called** |
| Day 1 | Darkness | Night | **called** |
| Day 2 | Firmament | Heaven | **called** |
| Day 3 | Dry land | Earth | **called** |
| Day 3 | Gathering together of the waters | Seas | **called** |

There is a decisive clue here. Looking at Genesis 1:9, it is recorded as follows:

> **Genesis 1:9 (KJV):** *"Let the waters be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land **appear**"*
> → It does not say He **created** the land, but that He made the land **appear**.

The dry land was not newly made but what already existed under the water was revealed. The waters also already existed in 1:2. God **gathered** those waters into one place and **called** them Seas.

**The reason this observation is important is as follows.**

The first world also had heaven and earth, day and night, and seas. After its order collapsed and became a state of chaos due to judgment, God **gathered, separated, and newly granted names to them again** over 6 days. "Called" is exactly that act of reorganization and naming.

This is evidence supported by the KJV text itself that the 6 days were not a pure creation out of nothing (creation ex nihilo), but a **reorganization from chaos to order**.

## 3. Special Distinction of Creation — bara (Create) and "brought forth"

Looking at the 6 days of Genesis 1 in order, we can see that God did not make everything in the same way.

| Day | Content | Method |
|----|------|------|
| **Day 3** | Herb & Fruit tree | Let the earth bring forth |
| **Day 4** | Sun, Moon, Stars | Set them in the firmament to give light upon the earth |
| **Day 5** | Fowl & Fish | Let the waters bring forth |
| **Day 5** | **Great whales** | **God directly created (bara)** |
| **Day 6** | Cattle & Beast | Let the earth bring forth |
| **Day 6** | **Man** | **Created (bara) in God's image** |

**A decisive distinction appears on the 5th day.**

> **Genesis 1:21 (KJV):** *"And God **created** great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which **the waters brought forth** abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind"*

In this verse, the verbs are divided into two. For great whales, the word **"created(bara)"** was used, and for the rest of the fishes and fowls, it said **"the waters brought forth"**.

This is an important theological distinction. The fishes were living creatures that also existed in the first world coming forth from the waters again, but **only the whales were beings newly created by God at this point.**

Throughout Genesis 1, **"bara(create)"** appears a total of **5 times**, concentrated on **3 events**.

> 1. **Genesis 1:1** — Creation of heaven and earth (1 time)
> 2. **Genesis 1:21** — Creation of whales (1 time)
> 3. **Genesis 1:27** — Creation of man (**Repeated 3 times** — "created man / created he him / created he them")

Repeating bara three times in the creation of man is a special emphasis on human creation.

And what's noteworthy is that **"called" also appears exactly 5 times**. There is not a simple positional correspondence but a semantic connection structure between the two words.

| Count | called (Reorganization) | Semantic Connection Basis | created (New Creation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light → Day (1:5) | The reference point of creation, the source driving out darkness and illuminating the world | Heaven and earth (1:1) |
| 2 | Darkness → Night (1:5) | A creature made to dwell in the darkness of the deep covered by judgment (Symbol of darkness Jonah was trapped in) | Great whales (1:21) |
| 3 | Firmament → Heaven (1:8) | God gave man dominion over the fowl of the air (heaven), the sea, and the earth | man (His own image) |
| 4 | Dry land → Earth (1:10) | The physical body of man created in God's image (he him) is formed from the dust of the Earth | he him (Image of God) |
| 5 | Gathering of waters → Seas (1:10) | As waters gather (unite) to bring forth life, male and female (he them) unite to conceive life and multiply | he them (Male and female) |

**5 times "called" = 5 times "created"** — Reorganization (called) and new creation (created) balance exactly within the 6 days, even forming a semantic correspondence structure between them.

**Supplementary Verification ② — The firmament was "made(asah)" — not bara**

> **Genesis 1:7 (KJV):** *"And God **made** the firmament"*

For the firmament, bara (create) was not used, but **asah (make)** was used. This means the firmament was not created out of nothing, but a structure newly manufactured utilizing existing materials (waters). Although a new concept that was not in the first world, it is not a bara from nothing. Therefore, bara is still only applied to the 3 events (heaven and earth, whales, man).

**Supplementary Verification ③ — Do whales (tanninim) exist both above and below the firmament?**

The firmament divided the waters which were above and the waters which were under (Gen 1:7). Whales (tanninim) are beings created by bara. Looking at the existence scope of tanninim in the Bible:

> **Psalm 148:7 (KJV):** *"Praise the LORD from the earth, ye dragons (tanninim), and all deeps"*

In this regard, the correspondence between the firmament (the boundary dividing above and below) and whales (creatures existing transcending that boundary) holds a semantic connection rather than a simple positional match.

**Why were whales specially created? (Semantic connection between darkness and whales)**

After the judgment, heaven and earth were covered with water (the deep) and darkness (Night) (Gen 1:2). That is, the deep of the sea is a space of judgment and darkness. A whale is a massive creature specially created to swim through that very sea of judgment, the deepest 'darkness', and carry out God's commands.

The event of swallowing the prophet Jonah and moving him to dry land is its perfect typology. When Jonah entered the belly of the whale, that place was perfect darkness (Night), which shows Jesus staying in the heart of the earth (the darkness of Hades) for 3 days and nights after His death on the cross. Since the whale is a being specially created (bara) as a vessel symbolizing the deep of judgment and darkness, it is semantically connected exactly with 'darkness (Night)'.

## 4. The Trinity Seen in Genesis 1

Genesis 1 does not directly declare the Trinity, but the existence and participation of the three persons are clearly engraved within the structure of the text.

**① Elohim — The name of God is plural**

The word referring to God throughout Genesis 1 is **Elohim**. This word is grammatically **plural**. The singular form is El. The very use of a plural name while being one is a clue to the Trinity.

**② Genesis 1:2 — Three persons appear within 1 verse**

> **Genesis 1:2 (KJV):** *"And **the Spirit of God** moved upon the face of the waters"*

- Gen 1:1 — "**God** (Father)" created
- Gen 1:2 — "**the Spirit of God** (Holy Spirit)" moved
- Gen 1:3 — "And **God said**" → John 1:1-3 confirms that this **Word is the Son**

> **John 1:1-3 (KJV):** *"In the beginning was **the Word**... All things were made **by him**"*

The three persons of Father, Holy Spirit, and Word (Son) appear in sequence in Genesis 1:1-3.

**③ Genesis 1:26 — "Us" is declared**

> **Genesis 1:26 (KJV):** *"Let **us** make man in **our** image, after **our** likeness"*

God spoke not in the singular ("I") but in the **plural ("us"/"our")**. This is a scene where the Trinity discussed human creation together.

**④ Genesis 1:27 — bara is repeated 3 times**

> **Genesis 1:27 (KJV):**
> *"So God **created** man in his own image,*
> *in the image of God **created** he him;*
> *male and female **created** he them."*

Right after the declaration of "us" (v. 26), bara (create) is repeated exactly **3 times**. This can be viewed as structurally reflecting the act of creation in which each of the three persons participated.

**⑤ Structural Comparison with 1 John 5:7-8 — The Johannine Comma and the Witness of Heaven/Earth**

> **1 John 5:7 (KJV):** *"For there are **three** that bear record in **heaven**, the Father, the Word, **and** the Holy Ghost: and these three are one."*
> **1 John 5:8 (KJV):** *"And there are **three** that bear witness in **earth**, **the Spirit**, and the water, and the blood: and these three agree in one."*

1 John 5:7-8 clearly distinguishes and testifies to the **"three in heaven"** and the **"three in earth"**. This structure of "heaven and earth" aligns amazingly with the structure of Genesis 1.

| Structural Element | Genesis 1 | 1 John 5:7-8 |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **Spatial Background** | **heaven and earth** (Gen 1:1) | **in heaven / in earth** (5:7-8) |

**[Core Structure 1: Grammatical Vertical Connectivity (The Role of "and")]**
In verse 7, the **"and"** attached before the Holy Ghost naturally continues to the first witness of verse 8, **"the Spirit"**, creating a vertical continuity where the witness of heaven (the standard) flows down to the witness of earth. Only when verse 7 (heaven) exists first can the grammatical and theological flow of verse 8 (earth) be completed. This is powerful internal evidence proving that verse 7 (the Johannine Comma) cannot be deleted as in modern versions, but is the perfect original text of the Bible.

**[Core Structure 2: Perfect Symmetry of 22 Words]**

## 5. Plurality of Waters — The Two Waters That Judged Heaven and Earth

God made the firmament in the midst of the waters. The plural form of 'waters' is not a mere linguistic expression. In the judgment of the first world, God judged the earth with water, and He judged heaven with another water. Therefore, the expression 'moved upon the face of the waters' can be seen as pointing to two types of water that judged heaven and earth respectively.

The structure that can be inferred through this is as follows. Originally, a perfect heaven and earth existed as one. However, as problems arose, heaven was divided into two, separating into the heaven where God dwells and the heaven and earth beneath it. Ultimately, God judged the lower heaven and earth with waters.

After the judgment, the lights were in darkness, and God divided the light and called it Day. This is what He did on the first day. An important point can be known from this fact: in the very first world, there was originally no concept of night and day at all.

The first world was an era of eternity, and the division of night and day is a concept after the new creation began. At some point, the first world became a state where darkness and light coexisted together, and as a result, the perfect heaven and earth came to be divided into 'heavens and earth'. Ultimately, the lower heaven and earth received judgment.
