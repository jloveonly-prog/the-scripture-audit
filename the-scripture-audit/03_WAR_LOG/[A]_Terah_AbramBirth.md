## 3. Analysis of Hebrew Original and KJV Translation Philosophy (A-01a + A-09)

### The Core Hebrew Structure of Genesis 11:26

**"וַיְחִי תֶרַח שִׁבְעִים שָׁנָה וַיּוֹלֶד אֶת-אַבְרָם אֶת-נָחוֹר וְאֶת-הָרָן"**

*"And Terah lived seventy years, and **begat** [wayōled] Abram, Nahor, and Haran."*

- Hebrew **וַיּוֹלֶד (wayōled)**: Repeated past tense of "began to beget / begat." It does not signify a single birth event, but can mean **the beginning of a series of births**.
- This structure is **repeatedly used** in the genealogical formulas of Genesis, meaning not simultaneous birth at a single point, but that childbearing 'began' at that age.

### Hebrew Narrative Convention: Listing by Importance (Analysis of Ancient Law/Culture A-09)

In the Old Testament, when listing brothers, it is an established Hebrew literary convention to list them **in order of importance (theological status)** first, rather than birth order.

| Case | Examples of Listing by Importance First | Actual Birth Order |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shem, Ham, Japheth** (Gen 5:32) | Shem is always recorded first | Japheth is the actual firstborn (Gen 10:21) |
| **Abram, Nahor, Haran** (Gen 11:26) | Abram is recorded first | Haran is the actual firstborn (Gen 11:28 — Haran died first) |
| **Ephraim, Manasseh** | Ephraim is recorded first | Manasseh is the actual firstborn |

> **Conclusion**: Genesis 11:26 is not a record that "Terah simultaneously begat three sons at 70," but a genealogical formula meaning "When Terah was 70 years old, he began to beget [his sons] Abram, Nahor, and Haran." Abram is mentioned first solely because of his theological importance, but **the actual firstborn is Haran**.

## 6. Decisive Counter-Evidence: The Same Formula of Shem, Ham, and Japheth (V-06 Similar Case Comparison)

If we apply the attacker's logic identically to Genesis 5:32, an immediate contradiction arises.

> **Genesis 5:32 (KJV)**: *"And Noah was five hundred years old: and Noah begat Shem, Ham, and Japheth."*
> → "Noah was 500 years old when he begat Shem, Ham, and Japheth"

*   However, Genesis 10:21 specifies that Japheth is the elder.
*   Noah began to beget his firstborn (Japheth) at 500 years old; he did not beget the three simultaneously.
*   Shem was born when Noah was 502 years old (Gen 11:10 reverse calculation).

**This structure is identically repeated in Genesis 11:26.** According to the attacker's logic, Noah would also have to have begotten three sons simultaneously at 500, but the Bible clearly denies this. The Bible proves within Genesis itself that this pattern is an established Hebrew narrative formula.

## 7. Final Verdict

### ✅ CONSISTENT — Consistency Confirmed

> **Reason for Verdict**:
> The record in Genesis 11:26 that "Terah lived 70 years and begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran" does not mean he begat the three sons simultaneously at 70. This is an established Hebrew narrative formula, meaning "When Terah was 70 years old, he began to beget [his sons] Abram, Nahor, and Haran." The firstborn Haran was born around 70, and Abram, the most theologically important, was born last around 130. The calculation that Abram was exactly 75 years old when Terah died at 205 matches perfectly without error. The same narrative formula is already established in Genesis 5:32 (Noah, Shem, Ham, Japheth), making this an internally verifiable Hebrew literary custom within the Bible, neither a translation error nor a scribal error.
>
> **Basis for Academic Consensus**:
> - Josephus's *Antiquities of the Jews* (I.6.5): Interprets Abram's birth at Terah's 130
> - Masoretic Text, Septuagint (LXX), Samaritan Pentateuch — All three manuscript traditions agree on "70 years"
> - KJV 1611 First Edition and 1769 Cambridge Standard Edition — No difference
> - Custom of listing by importance first: Gen 5:32 (Shem first), Gen 11:26 (Abram first) same pattern
>
> **Limits of Analysis Scope**:
> This verdict completely dismisses the claim that the "70 years" record of Genesis 11:26 is a biblical error. Since the exact birth year of Terah and the exact birth intervals of Abram's brothers are not directly recorded in the Bible, this verdict is limited to confirming that "Abram = Born at Terah's 130" is mathematically certain, and that this is not a contradiction with 11:26.
