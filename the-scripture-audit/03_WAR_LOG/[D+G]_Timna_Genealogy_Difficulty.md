## 3. Genealogical Telescoping (A-03)

In ancient Hebrew genealogical literature, the word 'sons (ben)' does not merely mean direct biological sons. It is frequently used as a comprehensive term covering 'descendants belonging to him' or 'the entire members of his household'.

Chronicles had to **telescope** the epic history of the entire book of Genesis into brief scenes. The primary readers of Chronicles were Jews who had already perfectly mastered Genesis.

- The explanation in Genesis: "Timna was a concubine and she bare Amalek."
- The telescoping in Chronicles: "...Kenaz, and Timna (the concubine), and Amalek (her son)."

The author was not incorrect out of ignorance of the Genesis account, but rather, **on the premise that the readers already knew that Timna was a concubine and Amalek was her son**, he deliberately omitted the explanation "concubine" and compressed them into a single household list (Red Group). This is not an error, but highly advanced 'narrative telescoping'.
