## 2. The Contradiction of Daegu's 'Seolhwa(雪花)' Regional Name and Later Interpretations
There is a village called **'Seolhwa'** in Daegu.

* **Scenery-based Origin (Joseon ~ Modern):** The currently known Chinese character meaning for Seolhwa contains the meanings of 'snowflake(雪花)' and 'apricot blossom'. Since the Joseon Dynasty, people have interpreted it as "called so because the apricot blossoms looked like snowflakes(雪花)." That is, it was a metaphorical expression overcoming climatic characteristics.
* **Geographical Contradiction:** However, geographically, Daegu is a place where it rarely snows. Setting the regional name by using 'snow(雪)', which has little relevance to the region, as a metaphor for apricot blossoms does not make sense. It is highly likely a mistake from later generations or an interpretation added after losing the original meaning.

## 3. An Intriguing Historical 'Mystery' (Seolhwa-hyeon vs Seolhwa-ri)
Then what was the real place name during the Silla period? Here, a very important clue connecting to the video contents is discovered.

* **Historical Fact (Silla):** Looking at the 『Samguk Sagi (History of the Three Kingdoms)』 and the historical records of the Dalseong-gun towns and townships, during the reign of King Gyeongdeok of Silla (757 AD), the current Daegu Hwawon area was called **'Seolhwa-hyeon(舌化縣)'**.
* **Meaning of the Chinese Characters:** Surprisingly, the Chinese characters used at this time were **'Tongue Seol(舌)'** and **'Become Hwa(化)'**. In some records, it also appears as 'Seolhwa(舌火)', where 'Hwa' means 'Fire'.
* **Evolution of Meaning and 'Tongues of Fire':** 
  * In the Silla era, 舌化(Seolhwa) meant 'the tongue transforms' or 'is governed'.
  * If the original meaning was 'Tongue Seol(舌)' and 'Fire Hwa(火)', this strongly evokes the **'tongues of fire'** from the Pentecost Holy Spirit descent event in the Bible. (The claim of 'tongues of fire' in the video and the Chinese characters match perfectly.)
