### 1-6. Why the Catholics Pressured Erasmus

Erasmus (1469-1536) was a Catholic theologian and humanist. When he published the Greek New Testament in 1516 and excluded the Comma, the Catholic side immediately exerted pressure for the following three reasons:

1. **Threat of the Vulgate's Authority Collapsing**
   The Latin Vulgate, the official Bible of Catholicism, had the Comma. Its absence in Erasmus' Greek text meant *"there is an error in the Vulgate"*. This was a direct challenge to the church's authority.

2. **Disappearance of the Trinitarian Proof Text**
   The Comma ("the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one") was the core verse by which Catholics defended the doctrine of the Trinity. When it was dropped, critics attacked Erasmus on charges of being an **Arian (a heretic denying the Trinity)**.

3. **Spark of the Reformation (1517)**
   It was just when Luther's Reformation began. They feared that Erasmus' Greek text would become a weapon for the reformers against Catholicism.

**Core Irony — Erasmus and GA 61:**
```
Erasmus 1516: Published 1st edition — Excluded because no Comma in Greek manuscripts
Erasmus 1519: Published 2nd edition — Still excluded Comma → Strong Catholic backlash
Catholic side: Presented GA 61 (Codex Britannicus) (Suspicion of fabrication exists)
Erasmus 1522: 3rd edition — Inserted Comma (Though he himself questioned the manuscript's reliability)
Result: The pressure to protect the Catholic Latin Vulgate → led to TR → then KJV
```
> ⚠️ **Academic Correction:** Traditionally, the anecdote that Erasmus promised "I will insert it if a manuscript is found"
> has been widely circulated, but according to H.J. de Jonge's research (1980), **documentary evidence of this promise
> exists nowhere in Erasmus' writings.** Bruce Metzger also retracted this "rash promise" anecdote
> in later editions. The current revised academic view is that Erasmus inserted it in the 3rd edition due to Catholic pressure
> and the issue of consistency with the Vulgate.
> That is, **Catholics pressured Erasmus to protect their Latin Vulgate**,
> and as a result, it is a paradoxical history that the Comma was included in the KJV via the TR.
> Calling this verse a "Catholic interpolation" misunderstands historical causality.

### 1-7. Recognizing the limits of the manuscript number debate

The formula "More manuscripts = Closer to the original" **does not hold.** The reasons are as follows:

- Having many manuscripts means **that text was copied a lot**, not that it is **closer to the original**.
- The reason there are few Alexandrian manuscripts is that papyri were physically preserved thanks to Egypt's dry climate, not that the Alexandrian tradition was a minority.

> **⚠️ Strategic Conclusion:**  
> Greek manuscripts containing the Comma **do exist** (GA 629, GA 61, GA 918, etc.).  
> However, they are few in number and there is debate over the reliability of each manuscript.  
> Therefore, the front line of apologetics must be moved **from the "battle of manuscript numbers" to "internal textual evidence"**.  
> **Manuscripts are auxiliary evidence, internal textual structure is the primary evidence.**

### 1-9-A. Vetus Latina — Latin Bible before the Vulgate

**Vetus Latina ("Old Latin Bible")** is a collective term for the Latin Bible translations  
used in North Africa and the Western Church from the 2nd to 4th centuries,  
before Jerome's Vulgate came out.  
It is not a single translation, but consists of **many early Latin translations that differed by region**.

> ⚠️ **Important Distinction:** To say that the "Old Latin translation tradition" exists from the 2nd century  
> means **the translation activity began in the 2nd century**.  
> **Extant physical manuscripts (copies)** containing the Comma only exist from the **5th century onwards**.  
> Evidence of the Comma from the 2nd-3rd centuries is only confirmed through **citations within Patristic writings** (Tertullian, Cyprian).

```
Greek Original Text (1st Century)
    ↓
Old Latin translation begins (2nd Century~) ← Start of independent early Latin translation tradition
    ↓
Patristic Citation Evidence: Tertullian ~200 AD / Cyprian ~250 AD ← Citations in theological writings, not manuscripts
    ↓
Oldest extant Latin manuscripts containing the Comma: 5th Century~ (Speculum, etc.)
    ↓
Jerome's Vulgate (382~405 AD) ← Revised and unified the Old Latin
    ↓
Spread of medieval Vulgate → Formalization by the Council of Trent (1546)
```

#### Testimony Regarding the Comma (*Against Praxeas*, Ch.25, ~200 AD)

**Key Latin Original Passage:**
```latin
"Ita connexus Patris in Filio et Filii in Paracleto,
 tres efficit cohaerentes, alterum ex altero.
 Qui tres unum sunt, non unus."
```
*(Translation: Thus the connection of the Father in the Son, and of the Son in the Paraclete,  
makes three coherent ones, one from another.  
These **three are one essence** (unum), not one person (unus).)*

**English Translation (ANF Standard):**
> *"Thus the connection of the Father in the Son, and of the Son in the Paraclete,  
> produces three coherent Persons, who are yet distinct One from Another.  
> **These Three are one [essence], not one [Person]**, as it is said,  
> 'I and my Father are One,' in respect of unity of substance not singularity of number."*

| Item | Evaluation |
|:---|:---|
| **Date** | ~200 AD — More than 180 years before the Vulgate |

#### 📖 Life Story — Why is his testimony special?

Cyprian was originally a **professor of rhetoric from a noble family** in Carthage.  
A wealthy and renowned intellectual, he converted to Christianity in his mid-40s and was so outstanding that he was elected bishop of the church in Carthage just two years later.

His 10 years as bishop (248~258 AD) exactly overlap with a **period of severe persecution in the Roman Empire**:

```
249~251 AD  Persecution by Emperor Decius
            → Systematic oppression of Christianity, large-scale emergence of the lapsed (Lapsi)
            → Cyprian guides the church through letters while in hiding

251 AD      The Novatian Schism
            → Church crisis over how to treat the lapsed
            → Cyprian responds by writing "On the Unity of the Church" (De Unitate)

257~258 AD  Persecution by Emperor Valerian
            → Edict to execute clergy

Sept 258 AD Cyprian sentenced to death at public trial
            "Thanks be to God" (Deo gratias) — His first words upon hearing the verdict
            → Martyred by beheading
```

> **Core:** Cyprian's quotation appears in an **official document written by a martyr-bishop pleading for church unity** while defending the church amidst persecution.  
> It is not a personal theological opinion, but **evidence of the official Latin biblical text** shared by the entire North African church at the time.

#### 📜 Direct Testimony Regarding the Comma (*De Ecclesiae Catholicae Unitate*, Ch.6, ~250 AD)

**Latin Original:**
```
"Dicit Dominus: 'Ego et Pater unum sumus';
 et iterum de Patre et Filio et Spiritu sancto scriptum est:
 'et hi tres unum sunt.'"
```

**Translation:**
> *"The Lord says, 'I and the Father are one' (John 10:30);*  
> *and again it is **written (scriptum est)** of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit:*  
> ***'And these three are one.'***"

**Background of Authorship:**  
This text was written by Cyprian amidst the crisis of church schism following the Decian persecution, **explaining the Trinity as the basis for church unity**.  
He quoted this verse when drawing the oneness of the Trinity from the Bible.

> **Key Point:**  
> "Scriptum est" (it is written) is a **formula used in the early church exclusively when quoting biblical texts directly**.

## 2. Core Evidence 1 — Greek Chained Editing (Heaven/Earth Contrast)

This is the strongest argument that bypasses the manuscript count debate.

### 2-1. Greek Comparison of 1 John 5:7-8 in TR and Alexandrian Manuscripts

**TR (Textus Receptus) Text:**

```
[Verse 7] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ,
      ὁ Πατήρ, ὁ Λόγος, καὶ τὸ Ἅγιον Πνεῦμα·
      καὶ οὗτοι οἱ τρεῖς ἕν εἰσι.

[Verse 8] καὶ τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῇ γῇ,
      τὸ πνεῦμα, καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ, καὶ τὸ αἷμα·
      καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
```
→ Verse 7: **ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (in heaven)** / Verse 8: **ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)** — Perfect contrasting pair

**NA28 / UBS5 (Alexandrian Critical Text):**

```
[Verse 7] (Omitted)

[Verse 8] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες,
      τὸ πνεῦμα καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ καὶ τὸ αἷμα,
      καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
```
→ In Verse 8, **ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth) is also omitted along with it**

### 2-2. Core Discovery — Alexandria did not just delete verse 7

> **When the Alexandrian scribes deleted verse 7 (Comma), they also had to remove "ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)" from verse 8.**

The logical meaning of this fact:

1. **Why did they also delete "in earth" from verse 8?**
   The spatial limiter "in earth" **only completes its meaning when contrasted with "in heaven" (Verse 7)**.
   If "in earth" is left alone after verse 7 is deleted, the reader will naturally ask: *"What about in heaven?"*
   To prevent this question, "in earth" was also removed.

2. **What this means:**
   It is circumstantial evidence that the Alexandrian scribes themselves recognized that "ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (in heaven)" and "ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)" were **originally designed as a pair**.
   They recognized the structural rupture caused by the deletion of verse 7 and tweaked verse 8 to patch it up.

3. **Conclusion:**
   Because the "heaven/earth" contrasting structure of verses 7 and 8 is the original, removing one required removing the other.
   A chain of edits occurred that required removal. **This chain of edits itself proves the existence of the original structure.**

### 2-3. Visualization of Structure

```
【 TR Original Structure (Internally Complete) 】
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Verse 7  ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ  (in heaven)  → 3 Witnesses in heaven: Father, Word, Holy Ghost
      ↕ Contrast
 Verse 8  ἐν τῇ γῇ      (in earth)   → 3 Witnesses in earth: Spirit, water, blood
─────────────────────────────────────────────
  Heaven and earth, the upper and the lower form a perfect rhetorical symmetry.

【 Structure after Alexandrian Editing (Collapse of Contrast Pair) 】
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Verse 7  (Deleted)
 Verse 8  (ἐν τῇ γῇ is also deleted)  → 3 Witnesses: Spirit, water, blood
─────────────────────────────────────────────
  "In earth" vanishes, so the location contrast itself disappears. With verse 8 alone,
  the question "Why specifically limit the location?" never even arises.
  Alexandria deleted "in earth" knowing that this question would arise.
```

## 4. Core Evidence 3 — Historical Apologetic Function and the Cerinthian Heresy

Context of 1 John 5:6:

> "This is he that came **by water and blood**, even Jesus Christ; **not by water only, but by water and blood**." (1 John 5:6)

### 4-1. Historical and Exegetical Meaning of 'Water' and 'Blood'

The overwhelming consensus of early church fathers (Tertullian, Irenaeus, Augustine) and modern exegesis:

| Evidence | Historical Meaning | Pointed Event |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Water (the water)** | **Beginning** of Jesus' public ministry | **Baptism at Jordan** — Presence of the Spirit, Voice of the Father |
| **Blood (the blood)** | **Completion** of Jesus' public ministry | **Death on the cross** — Atonement, Redemption |
| **Spirit (the Spirit)** | Confirms the two past events within us now | **Indwelling Holy Spirit** — Continuous testimony |

### 4-2. Direct Connection to the Cerinthian Heresy

The phrase **"not by water only, but by water and blood"** in 1 John 5:6 aims at **Cerinthus**.  
Cerinthus: *"The divine Christ spirit descended upon Jesus at baptism (water), and departed just before the cross (blood).  
Therefore, the divine Christ did not shed blood and did not die."*

John's Refutation: **The same Jesus Christ came by water (start of baptism ministry) AND by blood (the cross).**

> **If the Comma (verse 7) is a later insertion**, that inserter must be someone who perfectly  
> understood the core of the Cerinthian heresy debate and elaborately designed the theological structure of verses 7-8.  
> At that level, it must be the Apostle John himself, or a figure of comparable authority.

## 5. Core Evidence 4 — Structural Resonance with Genesis 1:26-27

### 5-1. Grammatical Structure of Genesis 1:26-27

Verses 26 and 27 are an inseparable theological unit:

- **Verse 26 (Planning):** "Let **us** make man in **our** image, after **our** likeness..."  
  → Plural pronouns for God ("us", "our") — A joint declaration of the Trinity.

- **Verse 27 (Execution):** "So God **created** man in his own image, in the image of God **created** he him; male and female **created** he them."  
  → **"created" (Hebrew: בָּרָא, bara) is repeated exactly 3 times.**

### 5-2. 3 Repetitions of 'bara' — Rhetorical Analysis

In Hebrew prose, a threefold repetition of the same verb is a **Climactic Repetition**.  
The subject of creation declared as "us (Trinity)" in verse 26 is expressed as a **threefold act of creation** in verse 27.

```
Genesis 1:26  "us / our"    → Declaration of plural creator subject
```
