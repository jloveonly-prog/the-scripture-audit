## 1. Establishing the Point of Conflict (PHASE 1: Dissecting the Verses)

### The Core Argument of the Attackers
> "In Acts 9:7, they heard the voice but saw no one. In Acts 22:9, they saw the light but did not hear the voice. It's the exact opposite. Paul's claim of meeting Jesus is a lie, and he is making contradictory statements."

### Direct Comparison of the Two Conflicting Texts

| Classification | Acts 9:7 | Acts 22:9 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Korean Revised Version (KRV)** | *"The men traveling with him stood speechless, hearing only the voice and seeing no one."* | *"Those who were with me saw the light, but they did not hear the voice of him who was speaking to me."* |
| **KJV** | *"the men which journeyed with him stood speechless, hearing a voice, but seeing no man"* | *"And they that were with me saw indeed the light, and were afraid; but they heard not the voice of him that spake to me"* |
| **Standard King James Version (SKJV - Korean)** | *"Therefore the men who journeyed with him stood speechless, because they heard a voice but saw no man."* | *"And they that were with me indeed saw the light and were afraid; but they heard not the voice of him who spake to me."* |

**Summary of the Apparent Conflict:**
```
Acts 9:7  → Companions: Heard Voice O / Saw Man(Light) X
Acts 22:9 → Companions: Saw Light O / Heard Voice X
```

## 2. The Key to the Solution: Jesus Spoke Twice (Sequential Parallel Reading)

> **Core Insight**: The two verses are not recording the exact same moment. 
> On the road to Damascus, Jesus spoke **two different contents at two different times**, 
> and what the companions **heard/did not hear was different each time.**

### The Actual Sequence of Events: Fitting the Two Records in Chronological Order

```
[Phase 1] A light from heaven shines around Saul (Acts 9:3 = 22:6)
         → Companions: Saw the light and were afraid (Acts 22:9 first half)

[Phase 2] Jesus speaks in a voice only audible to Saul
         "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?" (Acts 9:4 = 22:7)
         → Companions: Did not hear this voice (Acts 22:9 second half)

[Phase 3] Saul answers
         "Who art thou, Lord?" (Acts 9:5 = 22:8)
         → Companions: Sounds like Saul is talking to himself (Can witness this)

[Phase 4] Jesus speaks to Saul a second time, only audible to him
         "I am Jesus. It is hard for thee to kick against the pricks" (Acts 9:5 = 22:8)
         → Companions: Did not hear this voice either (Acts 22:9)

[Phase 5] Saul speaks
         "What shall I do, Lord?" (Acts 22:10)

[Phase 6] Jesus now commands in a voice audible to everyone
         "Arise, and go into Damascus!" (Acts 9:6 = 22:10)
         → Companions: Heard this voice (sound)! But saw no man speaking
         → This is the scene in Acts 9:7 "hearing a voice, but seeing no man" ✅

[Phase 7] Saul is blinded and led by the hands of the men into Damascus (Acts 9:8)
         → The men remember the voice they just heard, "go into Damascus," and lead Saul
```

### ✅ Integrated Sequential Reading (Reading the Two Records Together)

> *"As Saul journeyed and came near Damascus, suddenly there shined round about him a light from heaven. Falling to the earth, Saul heard the voice of Jesus. 'Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?' — **The companions saw the light but did not hear this voice.** (Acts 22:9) Saul answered, 'Who art thou, Lord?' He said, 'I am Jesus whom thou persecutest.' Saul spoke again, 'What shall I do, Lord?' Then Jesus said, 'Arise, and go into Damascus!' — **This time the companions heard the voice but saw no man, and stood speechless.** (Acts 9:7)*"

## 3. Original Language (KJV) Verification: Analysis of the Meaning of "Voice/φωνή" (V-03, V-05)

Attackers often take advantage of the ambiguity of the translated word "sound/voice" in the **Korean Revised Version (KRV)**.

| Classification | KRV | KJV | Greek Original |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Acts 9:7 | "hearing only the sound" | *"hearing a voice"* | **φωνήν** (phonen, accusative) |
| Acts 22:9 | "did not hear the sound" | *"they heard not the voice"* | **φωνήν** (same word) |

*   The Greek word **φωνή (phōnē)** means "voice, sound, utterance."
*   Both verses use the same Greek word. This confirms that it is not "different kinds of sounds," but rather **voices of Jesus spoken at different times.**
*   The KJV faithfully translates both as *"a voice / the voice"*, distinguishing by context that the two voices refer to different moments of speech.

It is an established literary practice in the Bible for two authors (or two records by the same author) to record the same event with different perspectives and details.

## 5. Pointing Out the Problems with the Korean Revised Version (V-03 KJV Comparison)

One of the reasons attackers can raise this issue is the problem of **translation condensation in the Korean Revised Version**.

*   The SKJV's Acts 22:9 includes the phrase *"And they that were with me indeed saw the light **and were afraid**"*.
*   The KRV condenses the context of "being afraid," making it difficult to know that the companions were overwhelmed by the light.
*   Reading with the KJV and SKJV makes the **state of the companions (falling down in fear of the light → not hearing Jesus' first voice / hearing Jesus' command voice after Saul's conversation)** stand out much more clearly.

## 6. Final Verdict

### ✅ CONSISTENT — Consistency Confirmed

> **Reasoning for Verdict**:
> Acts 9:7 and 22:9 are not contradictory records. The two verses record **different moments** during the Damascus road event when Jesus spoke two different contents. Acts 22:9 records the beginning of the event, where the companions did not hear the first voice ("Saul, Saul...") that was only audible to Saul privately. Acts 9:7 records the later part of the event, where the companions heard the second command ("go into Damascus") audible to everyone, but did not see the one speaking. By fitting the two records chronologically (sequential parallel reading), a single complete narrative is formed without any contradiction. This insight rather proves that the Bible is not merely a flat record of a single perspective, but three-dimensionally preserves details from multiple viewpoints.
>
> **Core Counter-Argument Logic**:
> If the two records were truly lies or contradictions, the same author (Luke) would not have recorded the exact opposite contents as they are in chapters 9 and 22 of the same book (Acts). The very existence of this 'tension' is rather evidence of the record's authenticity.
>
> **Confirmation of the Bible's Three-Dimensionality**:
> This dilemma is not a flaw in the biblical record, but evidence of the **Bible's three-dimensional narrative structure** that is only revealed when the two records are integrated sequentially. The detailed phases of the event, which cannot be captured by a single record, are perfectly restored through the cross-comparison of the two records.
