### 1-6. Why did the Catholics pressure Erasmus?

Erasmus (1469-1536) was a Catholic theologian and humanist. When he published the Greek New Testament in 1516 and excluded the Comma, the Catholic side immediately applied pressure for the following three reasons.

1. **Threat of the Vulgate's Authority Collapsing**
   The Latin Vulgate, the official Bible of Catholicism, had the Comma. That it was missing from Erasmus' Greek text meant, *"There is an error in the Vulgate."* This was a direct challenge to Church authority.

2. **Loss of the Proof Text for the Trinity**
   The Comma ("the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one") was the core verse by which Catholics defended the doctrine of the Trinity. With its omission, critics attacked Erasmus with accusations of being an **Arian (a heretic denying the Trinity)**.

3. **The Spark of the Reformation (1517)**
   It was just when Luther's Reformation began. They feared that Erasmus' Greek text would become a weapon for the reformers opposing Catholicism.

**Core Irony — Erasmus and GA 61:**
```
Erasmus 1516: Published 1st edition — Excluded the Comma as it was absent in Greek manuscripts
Erasmus 1519: Published 2nd edition — Still excluded the Comma → Strong Catholic backlash
Catholic side:  Presented GA 61 (Codex Britannicus) (Suspicions of fabrication exist)
Erasmus 1522: 3rd edition — Inserted the Comma (He himself expressed doubt on the manuscript's reliability)
Result:         The pressure to protect the Catholic Latin Vulgate → led to TR → KJV
```
> ⚠️ **Academic Correction:** Traditionally, the anecdote that Erasmus made a promise 
> to "insert it if a manuscript is found" has been widely circulated, but according to 
> the research of H.J. de Jonge (1980), **documentary evidence of this promise exists nowhere in Erasmus' writings.** Bruce Metzger also retracted this "rash promise" anecdote in his later editions. The current corrected view in academia is that Erasmus inserted it in the 3rd edition due to Catholic pressure and issues of consistency with the Vulgate.
> That is, **Catholics pressured Erasmus to protect their Latin Vulgate**, 
> and the result is the paradoxical history of the Comma being included in the KJV via the TR. 
> Calling this verse a "Catholic insertion" is a misunderstanding of historical causality.

### 1-7. Recognizing the Limits of the Manuscript Number Debate

The formula "Number of manuscripts = Closeness to the original" **does not hold.** The reasons are as follows:

- That the number of manuscripts is large means **that text was copied often**, not that it is **close to the original**.
- That the Alexandrian manuscripts are few is because papyri were physically preserved thanks to the dry climate of Egypt, not meaning that the Alexandrian tradition was a minority.

> **⚠️ Strategic Conclusion:** 
> Greek manuscripts containing the Comma **do exist** (GA 629, GA 61, GA 918, etc.). 
> However, the number is small and debates on reliability exist for each manuscript. 
> Therefore, the frontline of apologetics must **shift from the "battle of manuscript numbers" to "internal textual evidence"**. 
> **Manuscripts are auxiliary evidence; the internal textual structure is the primary evidence.**

### 1-9-A. Old Latin Versions (Vetus Latina) — Latin Bibles Preceding the Vulgate

**The Old Latin versions (Vetus Latina, "Old Latin Bible")** is a collective term for Latin Bible translations 
used in North Africa and the Western Church in the 2nd~4th centuries, before Jerome's Vulgate appeared. 
It is not a single translation but consists of **multiple early Latin translations differing by region**.

> ⚠️ **Important Distinction:** That the "Old Latin version tradition" existed from the 2nd century means 
> **translation activity began in the 2nd century**. 
> **Extant physical manuscripts (copies)** containing the Comma exist only from the **5th century onwards**. 
> The evidence of the Comma in the 2nd~3rd centuries is confirmed only through **citations within patristic writings** (Tertullian, Cyprian).

```
Greek Original (1st Century)
    ↓
Old Latin Translation Begins (2nd Century~) ← Start of independent early Latin translation tradition
    ↓
Patristic Citation Evidence: Tertullian ~200 AD / Cyprian ~250 AD ← Citation in theological writings, not manuscripts
    ↓
Oldest Extant Latin Manuscripts Containing the Comma: 5th Century~ (Speculum, etc.)
    ↓
Jerome's Vulgate (382~405 AD) ← Revised and unified the Old Latin
    ↓
Spread of Medieval Vulgate → Officialization at Council of Trent (1546)
```

and was an exceptional figure, elected as Bishop of the Church of Carthage in just two years.

His 10-year tenure as bishop (248~258 AD) precisely coincided with **a period of severe persecution in the Roman Empire**:

```
249~251 AD  Persecution of Emperor Decius
            → Systematic Christian suppression, resulting in large numbers of the lapsed (Lapsi)
            → Cyprian, guiding the church through epistles while in hiding

251 AD      The Novatian Schism
            → Church crisis over the treatment of the lapsed
            → Cyprian responds by writing "On the Unity of the Church (De Unitate)"

257~258 AD  Persecution of Emperor Valerian
            → Order for the execution of the clergy

258 AD Sep  Cyprian, sentenced to death at public trial
            "Thanks be to God (Deo gratias)." — His first words upon hearing the sentence
            → Martyred by beheading
```

> **Core:** Cyprian's quotation appears in **an official document written by a martyr-bishop appealing for church unity** while protecting the church amidst persecution. 
> It is not a personal theological opinion, but **evidence of the official Latin biblical text** 
> shared by the entire North African church at the time.

## 2. Core Evidence 1 — Greek Chain Editing (Heaven/Earth Contrast)

This is the most powerful argument and bypasses the debate over manuscript numbers.

### 2-1. Greek Comparison of 1 John 5:7-8 in TR and Alexandrian Manuscripts

**TR (Textus Receptus) Text:**

```
[Verse 7] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ,
      ὁ Πατήρ, ὁ Λόγος, καὶ τὸ Ἅγιον Πνεῦμα·
      καὶ οὗτοι οἱ τρεῖς ἕν εἰσι.

[Verse 8] καὶ τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῇ γῇ,
      τὸ πνεῦμα, καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ, καὶ τὸ αἷμα·
      καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
```
→ Verse 7: **ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (in heaven)** / Verse 8: **ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)** — A perfect contrasting pair

**NA28 / UBS5 (Alexandrian Critical Text):**

```
[Verse 7] (Omitted)

[Verse 8] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες,
      τὸ πνεῦμα καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ καὶ τὸ αἷμα,
      καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
```
→ **ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth) is also omitted** from verse 8

### 2-2. Core Discovery — Alexandria did not just omit verse 7

> **When Alexandrian scribes deleted verse 7 (the Comma), they also had to remove "ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)" from verse 8.**

The logical implications of this fact:

1. **Why was "in earth" in verse 8 deleted as well?**
   The locative phrase "in earth" **only achieves full meaning when contrasted with "in heaven" (verse 7)**.
   If only "in earth" remained after omitting verse 7, the reader would naturally ask: *"What about in heaven?"*
   To prevent this question, "in earth" was also removed.

2. **What this means:**
   This serves as circumstantial evidence that the Alexandrian scribes themselves recognized "ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (in heaven)" and "ἐν τῇ γῇ (in earth)" as **a structure originally designed as a pair**.
   They recognized the structural rupture caused by the deletion of verse 7 and altered verse 8 to mend it.

3. **Conclusion:**
   Because the "heaven/earth" contrast structure of verses 7 and 8 is original, a chain editing occurred where removing one necessitated removing the other. **This chain editing itself proves the existence of the original structure.**

### 2-3. Structural Visualization

```
【 TR Original Structure (Internally Complete) 】
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Verse 7  ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ  (in heaven)  → 3 Heavenly witnesses: The Father, the Word, the Holy Ghost
      ↕ Contrast
 Verse 8  ἐν τῇ γῇ      (in earth)   → 3 Earthly witnesses: The Spirit, the water, the blood
─────────────────────────────────────────────
  Heaven and earth, above and below form a perfect rhetorical symmetry.

【 Structure After Alexandrian Editing (Collapse of the Contrasting Pair) 】
─────────────────────────────────────────────
 Verse 7  (Omitted)
 Verse 8  (ἐν τῇ γῇ also omitted)  → 3 witnesses: The Spirit, the water, the blood
─────────────────────────────────────────────
  "In earth" vanishes, obliterating the locational contrast itself. In verse 8 alone,
  the question "Why specify the location at all?" does not even arise.
  Alexandria deleted "in earth" knowing this question would arise.
```

## 4. Core Evidence 3 — Historical Apologetic Function and the Cerinthian Heresy

The context of 1 John 5:6:

> "This is he that came **by water and blood**, even Jesus Christ; **not by water only, but by water and blood**." (1 John 5:6)

### 4-1. Historical and Exegetical Meaning of 'Water' and 'Blood'

The overwhelming consensus of early church fathers (Tertullian, Irenaeus, Augustine) and modern exegesis:

| Evidence | Historical Meaning | Event Pointed To |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Water (the water)** | The **beginning** of Jesus' public ministry | **Baptism at the Jordan** — Presence of the Holy Ghost, Voice of the Father |
| **Blood (the blood)** | The **completion** of Jesus' public ministry | **Death on the cross** — Atonement, Redemption |
| **Spirit (the Spirit)** | Confirming the past two events in us now | **Indwelling Holy Ghost** — Continuous testimony |

### 4-2. Direct Connection with the Cerinthian Heresy

**"Not by water only, but by water and blood"** in 1 John 5:6 is aimed at **Cerinthus**.
Cerinthus: *"The divine Christ spirit descended upon Jesus at baptism (water), but departed just before the cross (blood).
Therefore, the divine Christ did not shed blood and did not die."*

John's refutation: **The same Jesus Christ came both by water (the beginning of His baptismal ministry) and by blood (the cross).**

> **If the Comma (verse 7) is a later interpolation**, the interpolator must have been someone who perfectly understood the core of the Cerinthian heresy debate and exquisitely designed the theological structure of verses 7-8.
> At that level, it would rather have to be the Apostle John himself, or a figure of equivalent authority.

## 5. Core Evidence 4 — Structural Resonance with Genesis 1:26-27

### 5-1. Grammatical Structure of Genesis 1:26-27

Verses 26 and 27 are an inseparable theological unit:

- **Verse 26 (Planning):** "Let **us** make man in **our** image, after **our** likeness..."
  → God's plural pronouns ("us", "our") — The joint declaration of the Trinity

- **Verse 27 (Execution):** "So God **created** man in his own image, in the image of God **created** he him; male and female **created** he them."
  → **"created" (Hebrew: בָּרָא, bara) is repeated exactly three times.**

### 5-2. The 3 Repetitions of 'bara' — Rhetorical Analysis

A threefold repetition of the same verb in Hebrew prose is a **Climactic Repetition**,
