# Genesis Chapter 1 — The World of the Beginning and Traces of Judgment

---

## 1. The Scene of Creation Viewed from Heaven

Genesis 1 begins with a landscape looking down from heaven. In that scene, the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep.

The waters covered the earth, and the point to note here is that 'waters' is recorded in the plural, not the singular. The Spirit of God moved upon the face of two or more waters.

God moved because the state was not normal. After the movement stopped, God spoke. "Let there be light" — this was the first act to divide the light from the darkness.

The darkness was the evening, and by dividing the light, the first day began.

---

## 2. "Called" — 6 Days of Reorganization and Naming

In Genesis 1, it is necessary to distinguish when God made something new and when He gave a name to what already existed. The key clue is the word **"called"**.

| Day | Object | New Name | KJV Verb |
|----|------|---------|---------|
| Day 1 | Light | Day | **called** |
| Day 1 | Darkness | Night | **called** |
| Day 2 | Firmament | Heaven | **called** |
| Day 3 | Dry land | Earth | **called** |
| Day 3 | Gathering together of the waters | Seas | **called** |

Here is a crucial clue. Looking at Genesis 1:9, it is written:

> **Genesis 1:9 (KJV):** *"Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land **appear**: and it was so."*
> → It does not say He **created** the earth, but let the dry land **appear**.

The dry land was not newly made, but what was already under the waters was revealed. The waters also already existed in 1:2. After God **gathered** the waters into one place, He **called** them Seas.

**The reason this observation is important is as follows.**

In the first world, there were also heaven and earth, day and night, and seas. After the order collapsed and became a state of chaos due to judgment, God **gathered, divided, and newly named** them during the 6 days. "Called" is exactly that act of reorganization and naming.

This is evidence supported by the KJV text itself that the 6 days were not a pure creation out of nothing (creation ex nihilo), but a **reorganization from chaos to order**.

## 3. The Special Distinction of Creation — bara (created) and "brought forth"

Looking at the 6 days of Genesis 1 in order, we can see that God did not make everything in the same way.

| Day | Content | Method |
|----|------|------|
| **Day 3** | Herb, fruit tree | Let the earth bring forth |
| **Day 4** | Sun, moon, stars | Set them in the firmament to give light upon the earth |
| **Day 5** | Fowl, fish | Let the waters bring forth |
| **Day 5** | **Great whales** | **God directly created (bara)** |
| **Day 6** | Cattle, beast | Let the earth bring forth |
| **Day 6** | **Man** | **Created in God's image (bara)** |

**A crucial distinction appears on the 5th day.**

> **Genesis 1:21 (KJV):** *"And God **created** great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which **the waters brought forth** abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind"*

In this verse, the verb is divided into two. For great whales, the word **"created (bara)"** was used, and for the rest of the fishes and fowls, it said **"the waters brought forth"**.

This is an important theological distinction. The fishes are creatures that existed in the first world coming forth again from the waters, but **only the whales are beings newly created by God at this time.**

Throughout Genesis 1, **"bara (created)"** appears a total of **5 times**, and is concentrated on **3 events**.

> 1. **Genesis 1:1** — Creation of the heaven and the earth (1 time)
> 2. **Genesis 1:21** — Creation of the whales (1 time)
> 3. **Genesis 1:27** — Creation of man (**Repeated 3 times** — "created man / created he him / created he them")

Repeating bara three times in the creation of man is a special emphasis on the creation of man.

And what is noteworthy is that **"called" also appears exactly 5 times**. Between the two words, there is not a simple positional correspondence but a semantic connection structure.

| Count | called (reorganization) | Basis of Semantic Connection | created (new creation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light → Day (1:5) | The reference point of creation, the source that drives out darkness and illuminates the world | The heaven and the earth (1:1) |
| 2 | Darkness → Night (1:5) | A creature made to dwell in the darkness of the deep covered by judgment (Symbol of the darkness Jonah was trapped in) | Whales (1:21) |
| 3 | Firmament → Heaven (1:8) | God gave man dominion over the fowl of the air (heaven), the sea, and the earth | man (His own image) |
| 4 | Dry land → Earth (1:10) | The flesh of man made in the image of God (he him) is formed from the dust of the earth | he him (Image of God) |
| 5 | Gathering together of the waters → Seas (1:10) | Just as the waters are gathered (united) to bring forth life, male and female (he them) unite to conceive life and multiply | he them (Male and female) |

**5 times "called" = 5 times "created"** — Reorganization (called) and new creation (created) perfectly balance within the 6 days, and a semantic correspondence structure is even formed between them.

## 4. The Trinity Seen in Genesis Chapter 1

Genesis 1 does not directly declare the Trinity, but the existence and participation of the three persons are clearly engraved within the structure of the text.

**① Elohim — The name of God is plural**

The word referring to God throughout Genesis 1 is **Elohim**. This word is grammatically **plural**. The singular form is El. Using a plural name while being one is itself a clue to the Trinity.

**② Genesis 1:2 — Three persons appear within verse 1**

> **Genesis 1:2 (KJV):** *"And **the Spirit of God** moved upon the face of the waters"*

- Gen 1:1 — "**God** (The Father)" created
- Gen 1:2 — "**the Spirit of God** (The Holy Spirit)" moved
- Gen 1:3 — "And **God said**" → John 1:1-3 confirms that this **Word is the Son**

> **John 1:1-3 (KJV):** *"In the beginning was **the Word**... All things were made **by him**"*

In Genesis 1:1-3, the three persons—Father, Holy Spirit, and Word (Son)—appear in order.

**③ Genesis 1:26 — "Us" is declared**

> **Genesis 1:26 (KJV):** *"Let **us** make man in **our** image, after **our** likeness"*

God spoke not in the singular ("I") but in the **plural ("us"/"our")**. This is a scene where the Trinity consulted together concerning the creation of man.

**④ Genesis 1:27 — bara is repeated 3 times**

> **Genesis 1:27 (KJV):**
> *"So God **created** man in his own image,*
> *in the image of God **created** he him;*
> *male and female **created** he them."*

Right after the declaration of "us" (v. 26), bara (created) is repeated exactly **3 times**. This can be seen as structurally reflecting the act of creation in which each person of the Trinity participated.

**⑤ Structural Comparison with 1 John 5:7-8 — The Johannine Comma and the Evidence in Heaven and Earth**

> **1 John 5:7 (KJV):** *"For there are **three** that bear record in **heaven**, the Father, the Word, **and** the Holy Ghost: and these three are one."*
> **1 John 5:8 (KJV):** *"And there are **three** that bear witness in **earth**, **the Spirit**, and the water, and the blood: and these three agree in one."*

1 John 5:7-8 testifies by clearly distinguishing the **"three in heaven"** and the **"three in earth"**. This structure of "heaven and earth" astonishingly matches the structure of Genesis 1.

## 5. The Plural of Waters — The Two Waters that Judged Heaven and Earth





God made the firmament in the midst of the waters. The fact that 'waters' is plural is not a simple linguistic expression. In the judgment of the first world, God judged the earth with water, and judged the heaven with another water. Therefore, the expression 'moved upon the face of the waters' can be seen as referring to the two types of waters that judged heaven and earth, respectively.

The structure inferred through this is as follows. Originally, the perfect heaven and earth existed as one. However, as a problem arose, the heaven was divided into two, separated into the heaven where God dwells and the heaven and earth beneath it. Ultimately, God judged the heaven and earth beneath with waters.

After the judgment, lights were in darkness, and God divided those lights and called them Day. This is what He did on the first day. From this fact, an important point can be realized: in the first world, the concept of day and night itself did not originally exist.

The first world was an age of eternity, and the division between night and day is a concept from after the new creation began. From a certain point, the first world became a state where darkness and light coexisted, and as a result, the perfect heaven and earth came to be divided into 'heavens and earth'. Eventually, the heaven and earth beneath were judged.
