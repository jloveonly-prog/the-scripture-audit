## 3. Genealogical Telescoping Technique (A-03)

In ancient Hebrew genealogical literature, the word 'sons' (ben) does not simply mean direct biological sons. It is frequently used as an inclusive term for 'descendants belonging to him' or 'the entire members belonging to his household'.

Chronicles had to **telescope (compress)** the epic history of the entire book of Genesis into a short scene. The primary readers of Chronicles were Jews who had already thoroughly mastered Genesis.

- Genesis's Explanation: "Timna was a concubine and she bare Amalek."
- Chronicles's Telescoping: "...Kenaz, and Timna (the concubine), and Amalek (her son)."

The author was not mistaken because he didn't know the explanation in Genesis. Instead, **under the premise that the readers already knew Timna was a concubine and Amalek was her son**, he intentionally omitted the explanation "concubine" and compressed them together into a single household list (the Red Group). This is not an error, but a highly sophisticated 'narrative telescoping'.
