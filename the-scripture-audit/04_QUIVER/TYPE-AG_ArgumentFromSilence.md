# 🏹 SKILL-34: TYPE-AG — Argument from Silence (Argumentum ex Silentio)
**"The fact that the Bible is silent is itself evidence"**

> **When it triggers**: When the opponent's claim is **completely silent** within the Bible.
> The silence of the Bible may not mean "did not know," but rather "there was no need to record it" or "the event did not happen."
>
> **One-line definition**: *"What the Bible does not mention at all cannot be called biblical truth."*

---

## Trigger Conditions
`
[General]
1. When the opponent's claim does not appear anywhere in the Bible
2. When a certain doctrine/event/person is completely silent in the Bible
   (e.g., specific travel routes of a person, events at a specific location, unmentioned doctrines)
3. When formalizing that "not in the Bible = the Bible does not support it"
4. When external traditions/assumptions/interpretations try to take precedence over internal biblical records

[Typical Code] Trigger immediately upon detecting one or more of the following sentences:
  - "[The claim] has no record in the Bible"
  - "There is no mention in the Bible about [doctrine/person/event]..."
  - "It is a tradition from [after AD 180, after 400...]"
`

## Execution Steps
`
[STEP 1] Determine the Scope of Silence
  → Check verses where the opponent's claim is mentioned in the Bible: 0 or N found
  → Full scan of relevant biblical books (Acts, Epistles, Gospels, etc.)

[STEP 2] Analyze the Meaning of Silence
  → Did the Bible have an opportunity to record the claim?
  → If it was not recorded even with that opportunity → Intentional silence (evidence of absence)

[STEP 3] Contrast Silence vs. Evidence
  → Our claim: Internal biblical structural evidence exists ✅ / ❌
  → Opponent's claim: Complete silence of the Bible

[STEP 4] Verdict
  → A claim about which the Bible is completely silent has no biblical basis.
  → A claim with internal biblical evidence has biblical precedence.
`
