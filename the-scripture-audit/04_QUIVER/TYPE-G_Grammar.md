## 📌 [Sub-Protocol] Ishmael's 7th Rule (Mutually Limiting Clause Verification)

> **"כלל שהוא צריך לפרט ופרט שהוא צריך לכלל"** (Kelal she-hu zerik li-ferat...)
> (A general principle that requires a specific example for explanation, and a specific example that requires a general principle)

```text
[Trigger Condition]
  → When an enemy points to two clauses within the same verse and attacks them as "unnecessary redundancy" or "conflicting with each other."

[Execution Logic]
  1. Identify which of the two clauses is the **General Principle (Kelal)** and which is the **Specific Case (Perat)**.
  2. Prove the misreading (Error A) that occurs when only the general principle exists.
  3. Prove the misreading (Error B) that occurs when only the specific case exists.

  Misreading A (Only General Principle exists): "All the firstborn" → Risk of including a firstborn delivered via C-section.
  Misreading B (Only Specific Case exists): "Whatsoever openeth the womb" → Risk of including a 'second child' that opened the womb after a previous miscarriage.
  Conclusion: The two clauses are not a tautology but a 'mutually interdependent precise design' that perfectly covers each other's weaknesses.
```

## 🆕 Sub-Protocol: Structural Pair Lock — v1.2

> **Core Principle**: If two elements are designed as a Structural Pair in the original design, removing one will invariably create an Orphan Word or a grammatical void in the other.

```text
[STEP 1] Diagramming the symmetrical structure of the verse in question and adjacent verses
  → Place the suspected verse (target of deletion claim) alongside the remaining verses.
  → Mark spatial contrasts, repeated vocabulary, and grammatical connectors.
  → Table format: | Verse | Core Vocab/Structure | Corresponding Verse | Symmetrical Relation |

[STEP 2] Deletion Simulation
  → Virtually remove the suspected verse.
  → Check if the following phenomena occur in the remaining text:
     □ Orphan Word: A word left alone without preceding or following context.
     □ Grammatical Void: The target of a connecting conjunction or relative pronoun disappears.
     □ Symmetry Collapse: The structurally corresponding pair is broken, causing semantic imbalance.

[STEP 3] Checking Pair Consistency Across Manuscript Families
  → Check if the two structural elements must 'be present as a set or absent as a set' in all manuscript families containing the Comma.
  → If a set pattern is confirmed = Proof of original design.

[STEP 4] Verdict
  → Deletion causes Orphan Word/Grammatical Void + Set pattern across families
  → Declare: "This verse is not a deletion but the grammatical foundation for the remaining verses."
```

## 🆕 Sub-Protocol: Greek Gender Mismatch Detection — v1.3

> **Trigger Condition**: When the opponent deletes or reinterprets a portion of a Greek verse, check if a gender (性) mismatch occurs in the Greek grammar of the remaining verse after deletion.
> **Core Principle**: In Greek, the gender of adjectives, participles, and articles must agree with the noun they modify. If a gender mismatch occurs after deletion, it serves as grammatical evidence that the deletion destroyed the original structure.

```text
[STEP 1] Extracting gender/number of participles/articles/pronouns from the text after deletion
  → Virtually remove the suspected verse.
  → List the major grammatical elements possessing gender in the remaining text:
    □ Participle: Check Masculine(ὁ)/Feminine(ἡ)/Neuter(τό) endings
    □ Article: Check Masculine Plural(οἱ)/Feminine Plural(αἱ)/Neuter Plural(τά)
    □ Demonstrative Pronoun: Check agreement with the gender of the noun pointed to

[STEP 2] Verify Nouns for Gender Agreement
  → Check the genders of the nouns pointed to by participles/articles.
  → Check for mismatch occurrence:
    □ Match: Grammatically coherent even after deletion → Favorable to deletion claim
    □ Mismatch: Grammatical error caused by deletion → Proves the necessity of the original verse

[STEP 3] Theological Interpretation of the Mismatch
  → Why did the gender mismatch occur?
  → Prove that the elements (e.g., masculine persons) present in the deleted verse
    were supporting the participles/articles in the remaining verse.

[STEP 4] Verdict
  → Confirm occurrence of gender mismatch + Can only be resolved by the deleted verse
  → Declare: "This verse cannot be deleted. The Greek grammar of the adjacent verses demands its existence."
```

**① Granville Sharp Rule (G-CHK-4)**
> Greek structure: Definite Article(ὁ) + Noun1 + καί + Noun2
> → When there is only one article, both nouns indicate the same person

> → Even with the same root, meaning changes completely depending on the stem
