## 📌 [Sub-Protocol] Rabbi Ishmael's 7th Rule (Mutually Limiting Clause Verification)

> **"כלל שהוא צריך לפרט ופרט שהוא צריך לכלל"** (Kelal she-hu zerik li-ferat...)
> (A general principle that requires a specific example to explain it, and a specific example that requires a general principle to explain it)

```
[Trigger Condition]
  → When the enemy points to two clauses within the same verse and attacks them as "unnecessary redundancy" or "conflicting with each other."

[Execution Logic]
  1. Identify which of the two clauses is the **General Principle (Kelal)** and which is the **Specific Case (Perat)**.
  2. Prove the misreading (Error A) that occurs when only the general principle exists.
  3. Prove the misreading (Error B) that occurs when only the specific case exists.
  4. Declare that the two clauses combine to be Mutually Limiting, a precise design that perfectly defends against both Error A and B.

[Reference Case: Exodus 13:2]
  KJV Original: "Sanctify unto me all the firstborn (General), whatsoever openeth the womb (Specific)..."
  Misreading A (Only general principle): "all the firstborn" → Risk of including a firstborn delivered by Cesarean section.
  Misreading B (Only specific case): "whatsoever openeth the womb" → Risk of including a 'second child' born opening the womb after a previous miscarriage.
  Conclusion: The two clauses are not a redundancy but a 'mutually dependent precision design' that perfectly blocks each other's weaknesses.
```

## 🆕 Sub-Protocol: Greek Gender Mismatch Detection — v1.3

> **Trigger Condition**: When the opponent deletes or reinterprets part of a Greek verse, verify if a gender mismatch occurs in Greek grammar in the remaining verses after deletion.
> **Core Principle**: In Greek, the gender of adjectives, participles, and articles must match the nouns they modify. If a gender mismatch occurs after deletion, it serves as grammatical evidence that the deletion destroyed the original structure.

```
[STEP 1] Extract Gender/Number of participles/articles/pronouns from text after deletion
  → Virtually remove the suspect verse.
  → List major grammatical elements that have gender in the remaining text:
    □ Participle: Check masculine(ὁ)/feminine(ἡ)/neuter(τό) endings
    □ Article: Check masculine plural(οἱ)/feminine plural(αἱ)/neuter plural(τά)
    □ Demonstrative pronoun: Check match with the gender of the noun it points to

[STEP 2] Check gender match target noun
  → Check the genders of the nouns indicated by the participles and articles.
  → Mismatch occurrence status:
    □ Match: Grammar aligns even after deletion → Favorable to deletion claim
    □ Mismatch: Grammatical error occurs due to deletion → Proves necessity of original verse

[STEP 3] Theological interpretation of mismatch meaning
  → Why did the gender mismatch occur?
  → Prove that an element (like masculine persons) that was in the deleted verse was supporting the participles and articles in the remaining verses.

[STEP 4] Judgment
  → Gender mismatch occurrence confirmed + Resolvable only with the deleted verse
  → Declare "This verse cannot be deleted. The Greek grammar of the adjacent verse demands the existence of this verse."
```
