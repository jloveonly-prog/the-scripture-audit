## 🆕 Sub-Protocol: Greek Gender Mismatch Detection — v1.3

> **Trigger Condition**: When the opponent deletes or reinterprets parts of a Greek verse, check if a Greek grammatical gender mismatch occurs in the remaining verse after deletion.
> **Core Principle**: In Greek, the gender of adjectives, participles, and articles must match the noun they modify. If a gender mismatch occurs after deletion, it serves as grammatical evidence that the deletion destroyed the original structure.

```
[STEP 1] Extraction of Gender/Number of Participles/Articles/Pronouns from Remaining Text
  → Virtually remove the suspected verse.
  → List major grammatical elements with gender in the remaining text:
    □ Participle: Check for masculine(ὁ)/feminine(ἡ)/neuter(τό) endings
    □ Article: Check for masculine plural(οἱ)/feminine plural(αἱ)/neuter plural(τά)
    □ Demonstrative Pronoun: Check for agreement with the gender of the indicated noun

[STEP 2] Check Gender Agreement Target Noun
  → Confirm the gender of the nouns indicated by the participles/articles.
  → Occurrence of mismatch:
    □ Agreement: Grammatically coherent even after deletion → Favorable to deletion claim
    □ Mismatch: Deletion causes a grammatical error → Proves the necessity of the original verse

[STEP 3] Theological Meaning Interpretation of the Mismatch
  → Why did the gender mismatch occur?
  → Prove that elements (such as masculine persons) that were within the deleted verse
    were supporting the participles/articles of the remaining verse.

[STEP 4] Determination
  → Confirm occurrence of gender mismatch + Can only be resolved by the deleted verse
  → Declare "This verse cannot be deleted. The Greek grammar of the adjacent verse demands the existence of this verse."
```
