# 🏹 SKILL-43: TYPE-AP — Speech Act Theory
**"An utterance is not just information — it's a promise, command, prophecy, or declaration"**

> **When to Activate**: When confirming the exact meaning by analyzing not the linguistic form of the utterance,
> but the **function of the speech act** (is it a promise, command, or prophecy?).
>
> **One-Line Definition**: *"We analyze not what is said, but what act the words are performing."*

> [!NOTE]
> **Austin and Searle's 3-part Speech Act Theory:**
> - **Locutionary Act**: What is literally said
> - **Illocutionary Act**: The act performed by the utterance (Promise/Command/Warning/Prophecy)
> - **Perlocutionary Act**: The effect the utterance has on the listener

---

## Activation Conditions
`
[General]
1. When the linguistic form of the utterance differs from its actual function (Promise/Command/Prophecy/Warning)
2. When dismissing the counterargument that "This word is merely an invitation/consolation/explanation"
3. When determining what speech act God's/Jesus' utterance performs
4. When it is necessary to distinguish whether a prophet's/apostle's utterance is a prophecy, command, or declaration

[Typical Code] Activate immediately upon detecting the following sentences:
  - "This is merely [Encouragement/Consolation/Advice]"
  - "This command is a spiritual meaning, not a physical command"
  - "When there is a dispute over whether [God/Jesus/Apostle]'s utterance is a promise, prophecy, or command"
`

## Execution Steps
`
[STEP 1] Establish Utterance Text
  → Establish the target utterance for analysis in the original language.

[STEP 2] Classify Illocutionary Act
  → What does this utterance perform?
     Promise / Command / Prophecy / Warning / Declaration

[STEP 3] Apply Context
  → Finalize the speech act in the context of the speaker, listener, and situation.

[STEP 4] Declare Meaning
  → "The illocutionary act of this utterance is [Function]."
`

## 📌 Reference Case

**Analysis of John 21:18-19:**

| Utterance | Literal Content | Illocutionary Act | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|
| 21:18 "Someone else will dress you and lead you" | Future event description | **Prophecy** | σημαίνων marker (John 12:33 = Crucifixion) |
| 21:19 "Jesus said this to indicate the kind of death by which Peter would glorify God." | Author's commentary | **Declaration** | John's direct interpretation |
| 21:19b "Follow me (ἀκολούθει)" | Imperative verb | **Command + Command to execute prophecy** | Urging fulfillment of the 13:36 prophecy |

> **TYPE-AP Verdict**: "Follow me" is not merely an invitation but
> a command to execute the 13:36 prophecy + a request to accompany to the death of the cross.
> This illocutionary act presupposes accompaniment to a physical place (Golgotha). ✅ CONFIRMED
