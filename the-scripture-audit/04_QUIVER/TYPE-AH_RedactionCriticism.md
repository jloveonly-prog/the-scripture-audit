# 🏹 SKILL-35: TYPE-AH — Redaction Criticism
**"The author chose and recorded this — the choice itself is the intention"**

> **When it triggers**: When the author **selectively records** specific details out of numerous events.
> That choice is not a coincidence, but an expression of theological and narrative intent.
>
> **One-line definition**: *"Not only what is recorded, but also what is chosen to be recorded reveals the author's intention."*

> [!NOTE]
> **Combination with TYPE-W Retrospective Description**: The author recorded retrospectively (TYPE-W) + selected specific details (TYPE-AH) = the choice is an intentional decision made under complete information.

---

## Trigger Conditions
`
[General]
1. When the reason the author recorded a specific word, place, or name becomes debated
2. When dismissing the counterargument that "this is an accidental record"
3. When the repeated use of words/structures by the same author becomes debated
4. When proving the author's theological intention from the selection of the text

[Typical Code] Trigger immediately upon detecting one or more of the following sentences:
  - "What is the reason the author used [a specific word/structure]?"
  - "Is it a coincidence that [Paragraph A] and [Paragraph B] use the same word?"
  - "The author repeats the same pattern in different Gospels/books"
`

## Execution Steps
`
[STEP 1] Identify the Selected Item
  → Identify the specific details recorded by the author

[STEP 2] Present Alternative Selection Possibilities
  → List other options the author did not have to record

[STEP 3] Prove the Uniqueness of the Selection
  → Why is this choice narratively and theologically important?

[STEP 4] Intent Declaration
  → "The author knew this, chose it, and intentionally recorded it."
`

## 📌 Reference Case: The Choice of ὅπου + τόπος in John 19:20

John 19:20:
> *"Therefore many of the Jews read this title, for the place where Jesus was crucified (ὅπου ἐσταυρώθη) was near the city; and it was written in Hebrew, Latin, and in Greek."*

| Question | Analysis |
|:---|:---|
| Why did John use ὅπου in 19:20? | A structure where the author, who already used ὅπου prophetically in 13:36, reveals the identity of that ὅπου in 19:20 |
| Why did John specify τόπος (place) in 19:17? | An intentional choice to confirm that the ὅπου of 13:36 points to a physical location |
| Is this a coincidence? | Same author, same Gospel, same vocabulary — an **intentional redaction** made under retrospective description |

> **TYPE-AH Verdict**: John's choice of ὅπου in 19:20 is an intentional redaction that completes the prophecy of ὅπου in 13:36.
> This choice was knowingly designed by the author (Deepened combination with TYPE-W). ✅ CONFIRMED
